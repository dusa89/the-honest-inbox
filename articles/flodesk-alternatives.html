Why users actually leave Flodesk

Six real reasons, ranked by how often they come up — not the invented grievances that pad out other "alternatives" listicles.

1. The flat-rate era ended — for you, not for everyone. The number-one driver, and it's informational. On December 2, 2025, Flodesk retired its legendary flat-rate unlimited pricing for all new users; existing accounts are grandfathered indefinitely. Half the reviews on Google still lead with "$38/month flat, unlimited everything," which means new signups arrive expecting the legendary deal and find contact-tiered pricing like everyone else. At 5,000 subscribers, Pro is $59/mo and Lite is $52/mo — neither is the bargain the reviews promised. If you joined because of the flat rate, you joined for a price that no longer exists.

2. The one-workflow ceiling on Lite. Flodesk Lite ($25/mo at 1,000) includes a single workflow automation — currently positioned as a "limited-time bonus," an honest flag that it could change. One welcome sequence. Your abandoned-cart flow, your post-purchase sequence, your lead-magnet series — all need Pro. Anyone running two automations is paying Pro's $28/mo at 1k, not Lite's $25. The entry plan's automation story is a single drip campaign, and growing out of it means a tier jump, not a settings change.

3. The free plan sends zero emails. Flodesk's free tier gives you forms, landing pages, templates, and segmentation — everything to build a list — and then sends exactly nothing. The first email to anyone is a paid plan. Every exit on this page except Flodesk has a free tier that actually does the core job (Kit's covers 10,000 subscribers; AWeber's covers 500; MailerLite's sends 2,500 emails to 250 subs). There's no slow-validation runway on Flodesk — you pay before you send.

4. The 25,000-subscriber Lite ceiling. Lite has a hard cap: 25,000 total subscribers, and then you're on Pro regardless of which features you use. It's a forcing function disguised as a tier limit — same mechanics as ActiveCampaign's 10k Starter ceiling, different number. Growing businesses don't get to stay on the cheap plan just because their needs stay simple.

5. The cost curve punishes growth quietly. Flodesk looks gentle at 1,000 (Lite $25, Pro $28) and stops being gentle at 5,000 (Lite $52, Pro $59) — while MailerLite Comfort, which includes real automations on the cheap plan, is $49. By 10,000 it's Lite $72 / Pro $79 against MailerLite's ~$79 — but the MailerLite figure buys behavioral automation with conditions and triggers, and the Flodesk figures buy template beauty plus shallower workflows. The one honest modifier: Flodesk bills active subscribers only — unsubscribed, bounced, and cleaned contacts don't count — which genuinely slows the curve for list-hygiene-conscious senders.

6. Automation is shallow by design. The workflow builder does "send this series of emails with time delays" beautifully — and that's nearly all it does. Conditional branching, behavioral triggers ("if they clicked, do X"), lead scoring, purchase-event logic: Flodesk doesn't offer them, and it's not a roadmap problem, it's a design philosophy. Businesses that start as newsletters and grow into funnels hit the wall in month three to six. MailerLite's builder has conditions, splits, and triggers Flodesk simply doesn't.

The 6 alternatives, matched to your reason for leaving

1. MailerLite — if you're paying a design premium for features you don't have

The direct swap for anyone leaving over reasons #1, #2, or #5. Comfort runs $19/mo at 1,000 contacts and $49 at 5,000 — cheaper than Lite at both bands — with automations, conditions, triggers, and segmentation included on the cheap plan instead of gated behind Pro. The free tier (250 subs, 2,500 sends) gives you the validation runway Flodesk's free plan doesn't. What you gain: the cheapest credible option at every list size and a builder that grows with your funnels. What you give up: the templates. MailerLite's are fine; Flodesk's are in another league, and for an aesthetic audience that's not a trivial loss. If your emails are broadcasts and your automations are multiplying, you were never paying for templates — you were paying a design tax on a marketing system.

2. Kit — if you're a creator whose "design" was really creator tooling

For the Flodesk user who is actually a creator — courses, paid newsletters, launches — and bought Flodesk for the look when they needed the machinery. Kit's free tier covers 10,000 subscribers (Flodesk charges from the first send), Creator at $39/mo adds commerce, the Creator Network for cross-promotion, and automations built around selling rather than dripping. The trade: the templates are functional, not gorgeous — you trade beauty for a sales engine. The exit to take if your welcome sequence is supposed to sell things. Our Kit alternatives guide charts the full creator-side exits.

3. GetResponse — if you need automation with actual logic

For the Pro user who outgrew the shallow workflows and wants the depth Flodesk will never build. Marketer is the only exit at Flodesk's price level that replaces workflows with real automation: webinar modules, funnels, a course builder, behavioral triggers, and unlimited emails — $59/mo at 1,000 contacts, dollar-for-dollar with Pro, and $114 at 5,000. The trade: it's the second-busiest dashboard on this page, and nothing here is pretty. Only take this exit if the automation is what you're buying. Our GetResponse alternatives guide runs the full module math.

4. AWeber — if your newsletter was always simple

The quiet exit for the Flodesk user whose actual needs never went past a newsletter and one welcome email. Lite runs $25/mo at 1,000 contacts — the same price as Flodesk Lite — the free tier covers 500 subscribers and actually sends email, and there is no workflow ladder to climb. The trade: AWeber bills unsubscribed contacts (flagged in our AWeber vs GetResponse head-to-head), which stings exactly the hygiene-conscious senders who liked Flodesk's active-subscriber billing. If your list is clean and your needs are basic, it's honest money — just keep the hygiene, because the billing model punishes you for not having it.

5. Brevo — if your list is big, clean, and quiet

The send-based exit. Brevo bills emails instead of contacts, so a large list that sends occasionally can run far cheaper than any contact-tiered plan on this page — $97/mo for 50,000 emails a month against a big stored list, with real automation included from Standard up. This is the exit for the Flodesk user whose list is an asset that sends monthly, not weekly. The trade: frequent senders get punished by the meter (see our Brevo alternatives guide for the send-profile math), and the interface is the category's busiest. Only take this exit if your sends-per-subscriber-per-month is under 1 — otherwise MailerLite is the cheaper door.

6. ActiveCampaign — if you outgrew Flodesk entirely

The depth exit, for the business that started as a newsletter and turned into a funnel. ActiveCampaign Starter matches Flodesk Pro's entry price at $19/mo for 1,000 contacts, but buys conditional branching, lead scoring, and a real CRM pipeline that Flodesk doesn't offer at any price — and Plus ($59/mo at 1k) is the genuine automation product. The trade: the steepest learning curve in the category, and Starter rations automation to 5 actions (our ActiveCampaign alternatives guide covers the cap wall). Only take this exit if behavioral automation is your revenue engine — otherwise it's trading the template trap for the complexity tax.

The honest case for staying: when design IS the marketing

Here's what the exit guides won't say: for a specific reader, Flodesk is the right tool and the alternatives are all downgrades. If your audience buys with their eyes — photographers, boutiques, fashion, lifestyle bloggers, wedding vendors — the template library is not decoration, it's conversion. Flodesk's templates are genuinely in another league, and switching to MailerLite to save $6/mo at 1,000 contacts while your open-to-click rate drops is the most expensive savings on this page.

The stay math, by profile. Aesthetic brands with clean lists: the active-subscriber-only billing is your friend — hygiene-conscious senders blunt the tier curve, and Pro's $28/mo at 1k is fair for the design system. One broadcast + one welcome sequence: you're exactly the user Flodesk was built for; the shallow automation is a feature, not a bug, and Pro covers it. Grandfathered flat-rate accounts: never leave. The deal you have no longer exists for new signups, and every exit on this page is a price increase for you. Checkout sellers on Everything ($54/mo): unlimited checkouts, subscriptions, and abandoned-cart flows with Stripe pass-through rates is competitive — run the number against Kit Commerce before moving.

Migration notes

Contacts export cleanly — but nothing else does. The templates are the trap in reverse: they don't transfer, so every email has to be rebuilt in the new tool's builder, and budget a full weekend if your design system lives in Flodesk rather than in a separate brand kit. The automation rebuild is the easy part: Flodesk workflows are simple time-delay series, so mapping them into MailerLite or Kit takes an afternoon, not a weekend — the one time Flodesk's shallowness works in your favor. Rebuild your forms and landing pages before you cut over, since Flodesk's free tier keeps them live. And one billing warning before you import: Flodesk's active-subscriber-only billing means you may be arriving from a cheaper-than-headcount bill — clean the list (unsubscribed, bounced, inactive) before importing into a tool that bills every contact, or your first month's bill is the surprise.

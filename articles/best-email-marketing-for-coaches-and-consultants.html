Most "email marketing for coaches" lists rank tools like you're an e-commerce store. You're not. A coach's email list exists to do two things: book discovery calls and sell courses. Those are two different funnels with different tool requirements — and picking the tool before you've picked your funnel is why so many coaches pay for software they use as a newsletter.

The thing nobody tells coaches: email tools don't book calls

A coach's funnel is three pieces, and the email tool is only one of them:

  • Your calendar (Calendly or similar) actually takes the booking. No email tool replaces this.
  • Your email tool drives traffic to the calendar — freebie opt-in, nurture sequence, "book your call" CTA, post-call follow-up.
  • Your payment/course platform takes the money and hosts the course — unless you pick a tool that bundles it.

The honest setup: pick your funnel first (calls, courses, or both), then pick the email tool that serves that funnel's bottleneck. A discovery-call funnel's bottleneck is automation; a course funnel's bottleneck is webinar + payment friction. Different problems, different tools.

What coaches actually need (and don't)

Coach list sizes are small. A working coach's list typically runs 200–2,000 contacts (our estimate — lead-magnet lists from a single funnel). Entry-level paid plans cover you; ignore "best for 50k subscribers" advice.

What matters: opt-in forms and landing pages for your lead magnet, drip automation (the nurture sequence IS your sales team), segmentation (prospects vs. clients vs. course students — never send pitch emails to current clients), booking-tool integration, and, if you sell courses, webinar or course-hosting bundled in.

What doesn't matter much: e-commerce features (abandoned cart is for stores), SMS (your prospects are not impulse buyers), and multi-user seats (you're a solo operator).

Kit — the discovery-call funnel pick

Kit was built for creators who sell themselves, which is what coaching is. Forms, landing pages, and broadcast emails are excellent, and the free plan covers 10,000 subscribers — your whole list at $0. The catch that matters for coaches: free-plan automation is limited — the visual automation builder (welcome sequences, nurture drips) is a Creator feature. Your discovery-call sequence is an automation, so budget Creator at $39/mo at 1k from the start, not $0.

Course sellers note: Kit Commerce lets you sell digital products with a 3.5% + 30¢ fee per transaction on every plan. A $297 course sale costs you ~$10.70 in fees (our calculation: 3.5% of $297 = $10.40 + $0.30). Ten sales a month = ~$107 in fees — compare that honestly against a flat-fee course platform.

If you sell courses, the math changes. The discovery-call sequence (5 emails, 7 days)

Your lead magnet (checklist, mini-audit, free chapter) buys the email. This sequence turns it into a booked call:

  1. Immediately — Deliver the freebie. One link, one line of thanks. No pitch. Pitching in email 1 trains people to ignore you.
  2. Day 1 — The diagnosis. "Here's the mistake I see most [your niche] make." Plain-text, personal tone — the email that gets replies gets calls.
  3. Day 3 — The proof. One client story, specific numbers, one transformation. Not testimonials — a story with a before and an after.
  4. Day 5 — The objection handler. Answer the #1 reason people don't book ("I don't have time," "I tried X before"). Name it before they think it.
  5. Day 7 — The direct invite. "I open 5 call slots a week. Here's the booking link." Scarcity only works if it's true — actually limit the slots.

The course cart sequence (6 emails, 5 days)

Cart open to cart close — the launch engine for course sellers:

  1. Day 0 (open) — Cart is open. What's inside, who it's for, the price, the link. Long email; this one does the selling.
  2. Day 1 — The curriculum walkthrough. Module by module, what changes for them in each. Kill the "will this work for me" doubt.
  3. Day 2 — Student stories. Two or three specific results from past students or beta testers.
  4. Day 3 — FAQ email. Time commitment, skill level, refunds, "what if I'm behind." Every question you preempt is a sale saved.
  5. Day 4 (morning) — Closing tonight. Reminder of the deadline, restate the transformation.
  6. Day 4 (evening) — Last call. Short. Deadline, link, done. Two emails on close day consistently outperforms one (a standard launch observation, not a guarantee).

Segmentation: the rule coaches break most

One list, one message is how coaches annoy their best customers. Minimum viable segmentation:

  • Prospects (never bought) — get the sequences above.
  • Clients (in a program now) — get delivery and support emails only. Never send a pitch to someone mid-program.
  • Past clients — your highest-converting audience for the next offer. Tag them; they convert at multiples of cold prospects (our estimate — past-buyer conversion is consistently the highest segment in every funnel we've studied).

Every tool on this list does tag-based segmentation. The differentiator is automation depth: ActiveCampaign and Kit handle "moved from prospect to client → stop the pitch sequence automatically" natively; on MailerLite's Comfort plan, check that your specific trigger is supported before you assume it.

Billing traps, coach edition

  • Unsubscribed contacts still bill (ActiveCampaign, Mailchimp) — coaches collect dead freebie-seekers fast. Clean quarterly or pay for ghosts.
  • Kit's $0-to-$39 cliff — free covers everything until you need the automation builder, then it's $39/mo at 1k. Build your call sequence on free and you'll rebuild it on Creator.
  • Kit's 3.5% + 30¢ commerce fee — selling a $297 course 10× a month costs ~$107 in fees. At that volume, a flat-fee course platform + cheap email tool may beat Kit Commerce.
  • GetResponse's net pricing — the $69 Creator price is before local tax. And Starter's single automation workflow is a demo, not a funnel.
  • MailerLite's 10× send cap — fine for weekly, tight during a launch month with daily cart emails. Price the launch, not the average.
  • Webinar math — if you run live masterclasses to sell calls, GetResponse Marketer ($59/mo at 1k, 100 attendees) replaces a separate webinar subscription. If you don't, don't pay for it.

The bottom line

Discovery-call coaches: Kit free while you build the list, Creator ($39/mo at 1k) the month your nurture sequence goes live. If your ticket is $2k+ and you want behavioral booking automation, ActiveCampaign Starter ($19/mo at 1k) earns its keep — just clean the list quarterly.
Course sellers: price the all-in-one honestly — GetResponse Creator ($69/mo at 1k, courses + webinars + email) vs. your current stack of course platform + webinar tool + email. If the stack costs more than $69, switch; if it doesn't, Kit Commerce (3.5% + 30¢ per sale) plus the email tool you already like is the cheaper path at low volume.
Newsletter-first coaches: MailerLite Comfort ($19/mo at 1k) is the cheapest complete option; Brevo's free tier is the $0 way to validate.

Start with the sequence, not the tool. Write your 5-email call sequence this week — the tool barely matters until the emails exist.

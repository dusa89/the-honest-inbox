Most comparisons frame this as "cheap creator tool vs expensive automation powerhouse." That framing is wrong on the price part. ActiveCampaign's cheapest plan is $19/mo at 1,000 contacts — half the price of Kit's Creator plan. The real trade-off is what that $19 actually buys you, and whether the automation you're paying ActiveCampaign for is automation you'll ever run.

TL;DR
At automation parity, Kit wins on price at every list size — ActiveCampaign's real automation lives on Plus ($59+/mo), and its $19 Starter caps each automation at 5 actions. But if your business runs on behavior-triggered journeys, lead scoring, and a sales pipeline, Kit can't do that job at any price — there's no CRM and no deal stages. Buy the tool that matches the job, not the price table.

What the money actually buys (it's not just price)

These tools were built for different jobs, and the feature gap runs in both directions.

ActiveCampaign is a marketing automation platform. Behavioral triggers (site visits, purchases, cart abandonment), lead scoring, split automations, conditional content, SMS, and a real sales CRM with deal pipelines and lead stages — the CRM comes as an add-on on Plus and up, which is how the $59 plan quietly grows. Predictive sending and AI personalization sit on Pro. If your funnel has stages and your team has a follow-up process, this is the only tool here built for it.

Kit is a creator business platform with email at its core. Tag-based subscribers, visual sequences, landing pages, and the differentiator: built-in commerce. Paid newsletters, digital products, tip jars, and the Creator Network for cross-promotion — you can sell directly from Kit with no checkout tool. There is no CRM, no deal pipeline, no lead scoring, and automation triggers are content-engagement-oriented (joins a form, gets a tag, makes a purchase) rather than sales-behavior-oriented.

Three gotchas before you decide

  • ActiveCampaign bills your dead contacts. Since November 2025, new accounts are charged for all contacts — including unsubscribed, bounced, and unconfirmed ones. A 10,000-contact list with 3,000 dead emails bills like 10,000. MailerLite and Kit only bill active subscribers. Clean lists before you migrate, and price the list you have, not the list you think you have.
  • The $19 Starter is a price trap. Five actions per automation, no branching, no landing pages, no CRM. One real nurture sequence (welcome email → wait → check opens → branch → tag → offer) burns the whole allowance. Most buyers outgrow it in weeks and land on Plus at $59 — triple the headline price.
  • Kit's free plan limits the thing creators need first. Welcome sequences and drip campaigns — the first automation most businesses build — need the Creator plan. And Kit has no answer to deal stages, lead scoring, or sales pipelines: if your business needs those, Kit's price advantage is irrelevant because it can't do the job.

Who should pick which

Pick ActiveCampaign if: you run lead-nurturing or e-commerce funnels with behavioral triggers; you need a CRM with deal stages in the same tool; or you have a team running automations weekly. Start on Plus — budget the real plan, not the $19 teaser.

Pick Kit if: you're a creator, coach, or newsletter operator selling to an audience; your automations are sequences and broadcasts, not pipelines; or your list is under 10,000 and the free plan's $0 floor buys you a year of runway. At automation parity it's also simply cheaper.

Switching between them

Kit migrates your list from another email tool free on Creator and Pro plans. Migrating to ActiveCampaign is where the work lives: rebuild automations (its trigger vocabulary is far richer, so a 1:1 port usually gets rewritten), scrub unsubscribed contacts before import (you'll be billed for them), and budget Plus — not Starter — or the first sequence you rebuild will hit the 5-action cap. Going ActiveCampaign → Kit, expect to lose CRM and lead-scoring data and to rebuild pipeline follow-ups as tags and sequences.

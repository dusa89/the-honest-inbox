This is the trickiest alternatives article on the site, because GetResponse sells you two different products under one name. The $19 Starter tier is the cheapest major-tier entry in the email category — unlimited emails, landing pages, a genuinely good sender. And then you hit the one-automation-workflow wall in month two, realize the webinars and funnels you signed up for live on the $59 Marketer tier, and the $19-to-$59 jump is the steepest single-tier leap in email marketing. Below: the six exits that genuinely make sense, matched to which of the two GetResponses you're actually leaving — and the honest case that the bundle, at scale, is still cheaper than the pieces.

TL;DR
Leaving because Starter's limits bit you (one automation workflow, no webinars) but you only need email? That's a marketing-to-product mismatch — MailerLite Comfort does everything Starter does plus real automation for $19/mo at 1,000 contacts, and AWeber Lite keeps it simple at $25. Leaving because Marketer is $59 at 1k and $179 at 10k and you don't run webinars? You're paying the all-in-one tax — Kit or MailerLite cost half for the email half of your stack. But if you run webinars, funnels, and courses every month, GetResponse's Marketer bundle still undercuts buying a webinar platform, a funnel builder, and an email tool separately — and nothing on this page does all three. Leaving is about which tools you actually open.

Why users actually leave GetResponse

Four real reasons, ranked by how often they come up — not the invented grievances that pad out other "alternatives" listicles.

1. The $19-to-$59 jump. The number-one driver, and it's a pricing-page psychology trick. Starter at $19/mo looks like the cheapest serious tier in the category — and it is, for what it is: unlimited emails, landing pages, and exactly one automation workflow. No webinars, no conversion funnels, no contact scoring. The product most people bought GetResponse for is Marketer at $59/mo: unlimited workflows, native webinars, funnels. That's a 3× jump from the price that got you in the door, and it lands exactly when your automation needs get serious. We charted the full ladder in our GetResponse pricing guide.

2. The all-in-one scale tax. Once you're on Marketer or Creator, growth is expensive. Marketer runs $59/mo at 1,000 contacts, $114 at 5,000, $179 at 10,000, and $349 at 25,000. Creator is $69/$149/$229/$449 at the same sizes. The bundle that felt like a deal at 1,000 contacts starts costing more than the individual tools it replaced — and you notice it right when the list is big enough that you can't casually switch. MailerLite's Comfort plan, for comparison, runs $19/$49/$179 at 1k/5k/25k — at 25,000 contacts, GetResponse Marketer costs $170/mo more for the platform layer.

3. Paying for tools you never open. This is the review pattern that shows up across every independent source: most Marketer users actively use two or three of the modules — email, automations, maybe webinars — while the course builder, funnel templates, and contact scoring sit untouched. A $59–$349/mo platform you use as a $19 email sender is a subscription waiting to be canceled. Audit your own usage before anything else: open your GetResponse dashboard, count the modules you touched in the last 30 days, and price that list instead of the bundle.

4. The billing quirks. Three small ones that add up. Contacts duplicated across lists can inflate your billable count — our AWeber vs GetResponse comparison flagged this against AWeber's unsubscriber-billing habit, and both are worth a list-cleanup pass. Published prices are net of local tax, so your invoice runs higher than the pricing page. And the free plan (500 contacts, 2,500 emails/month) is a genuine 30-day-style trial, not a long-term home — fine for testing deliverability, not for running a business.

The 6 alternatives, matched to your reason for leaving

1. MailerLite — if it's simplicity and the price

The direct swap for anyone who bought GetResponse for email and doesn't touch the platform modules. Comfort runs $19/mo at 1,000 contacts — the same sticker as Starter, but with the automation Starter withholds, an interface that takes an afternoon to learn instead of a week, and a Power tier ($25 at 500, $39 at 1,000) that undercuts Marketer the moment your list passes a few thousand. What you give up: native webinars, conversion funnels, the course builder. What you gain: the same bill at 1,000 contacts, then roughly half the bill from 5,000 on up. Full band math in GetResponse vs MailerLite.

2. ActiveCampaign — if you're on Marketer and hitting the automation ceiling

This is the upgrade move, not the cost play — for the Marketer user whose automations have outgrown what GetResponse's builder does gracefully. Starter matches GetResponse's own Starter price at 1,000 contacts ($19/mo) with deeper branching, lead scoring, and a real CRM attached; the trade is that the bill climbs to $99/mo at 5,000 — cheaper than Marketer's $114, but you're buying business systems, not saving money. Only take this exit if automations are the work: newsletter-only users are buying complexity they'll never use. See ActiveCampaign vs Kit for the automation-vs-simplicity trade-off.

3. Kit — if you're a creator who wandered into an all-in-one

For the Creator-plan user whose business is courses, paid newsletters, and launches — the creator stack wearing an all-in-one's clothing. Kit Creator runs $39/mo at 1,000 contacts versus GetResponse Creator's $69 — $30/mo cheaper for commerce features, the Creator Network, and a platform built around creators instead of webinars. The trade runs both ways: Kit has no native webinar module, and its paid plans run ~2x the value tools, so only switch if the creator tooling is what you actually bought GetResponse for. Our Kit alternatives guide charts the creator-side exits in full.

4. AWeber — if you want the old-guard simple

The least-fashionable exit, and the right one for a specific reader: the small business that wants email to just work and never wants to think about it again. Lite runs $25/mo at 1,000 contacts, the free tier covers 500 subscribers, and there's no platform to learn because there's barely a platform — it's the anti-GetResponse. The trade: it bills unsubscribed contacts (flagged in our head-to-head), the automation is a generation behind, and power users will feel the ceiling fast. For a newsletter and a welcome sequence, it's honest money.

5. Brevo — if it's the cheapest serious sender, full stop

Brevo prices by emails sent rather than contacts stored, which makes it the cheapest exit for senders with big, quiet lists — the exact list profile GetResponse punishes hardest. Our Brevo vs MailerLite comparison runs the full send-based-vs-contact-based math. The trade: the interface and deliverability tooling are a step behind the majors, and it's a sender, not a platform — no webinars, no funnels. This is the exit for the Marketer user who finally admitted they only ever sent emails.

6. Flodesk — if you send beautiful broadcasts and nothing else

The design-first exit: Flodesk's flat-rate pricing and genuinely best-in-class email templates make it the pick for brands where the email is the product experience — boutiques, studios, photographers. Our Flodesk vs MailerLite guide covers the template trap (gorgeous emails, thin automation). Only switch if your automation needs fit in a shoebox; anyone running real workflows on Marketer will feel the downgrade in week one.

The honest case for staying: the bundle is real

Here's what the exit guides won't say: if you actually run webinars, funnels, and courses every month, GetResponse Marketer is still the cheapest way to own all three. A standalone webinar platform runs $40+/mo before you've sent a single email; a funnel builder runs $97+/mo; an email tool runs $19–$59. Marketer at $59/mo replaces all three, and the integration friction you pay is zero because there's nothing to integrate. The alternatives above beat GetResponse by deleting features you weren't using — they can't touch it on the features you were.

The stay math, by tier. On Starter: if a newsletter, landing pages, and one automation workflow is genuinely all you do, nothing is cheaper at 1,000 contacts except Kit's free tier — don't upgrade to Marketer "for later"; upgrade when the one-workflow wall actually stops you. On Marketer: count your modules. Three or more used monthly — webinars, automations, funnels — and the bundle math holds to 25,000 contacts. Two or fewer, and you're donating the platform premium; take the MailerLite exit. On Creator: only if the course builder and paid newsletters are revenue, not plans. And the 30-day no-card trial is genuinely useful — run your deliverability and webinar load through it before any paid decision, so you're buying evidence, not marketing.

How to actually switch (if you're sure)

1. Clean first. GetResponse's duplicate-across-lists billing means your export carries contacts the next tool bills once — dedupe and archive cold subscribers before you export, and price the new plan on the cleaned number. 2. Export everything — lists, segments, tags, templates. 3. Rebuild automations by hand — this is the real cost; GetResponse's visual workflows don't export to any other platform, so budget the afternoon and rebuild the money workflows (abandoned cart, welcome series) before the vanity ones. 4. Warm up the new sender — run both tools in parallel for one full send cycle and watch deliverability before you cancel.

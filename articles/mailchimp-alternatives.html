Two reasons everyone is shopping for Mailchimp alternatives. First, the billing: Mailchimp charges you for unsubscribed contacts — people who explicitly told you to stop emailing them still count toward your billable subscriber total. Second, the price: Essentials runs $26.50/mo at 1,000 subscribers, $75/mo at 5,000, and roughly $270/mo at 25,000. Every tool below beats all three numbers. The only question is which one fits how you actually work.

TL;DR
MailerLite is the cheapest straight switch ($19/$49/$179 — saves you $7–$91/mo at every tier). Brevo is cheapest if you send a lot to a big list (volume pricing: $9–$18 to start, ~$139 at 25k). Kit is free to 10,000 subscribers if your needs are basic. GetResponse matches Mailchimp's feature depth at a lower price. Flodesk wins on design if you care how your emails look more than anything else. Switching takes an afternoon — export, import, recreate automations.

The 5 alternatives, matched to your situation

1. MailerLite — the straight switch

If your Mailchimp setup is newsletters, promos, and a welcome email, MailerLite does all of it for 30–40% less. The builder is simpler than Mailchimp's (fewer template quirks), automations are included at the entry paid tier, and the migration is the easiest here — most Mailchimp users recreate their setup in an afternoon. Watch the Comfort plan's send cap (10× subscriber count per month), and note the free tier only covers 250 subscribers.

2. Brevo — the volume play

Brevo doesn't charge per subscriber at all — it charges per email sent. If you have a big list you email infrequently (a 25,000-person list with a monthly newsletter), Brevo is dramatically cheaper than anything per-subscriber: roughly $139/mo where Mailchimp charges ~$270. The tradeoff: the entry Starter tier has no automation, so if welcome sequences and abandoned-cart flows matter, you need the Business tier. 3. Kit — the free-to-10k option

Kit's free plan covers 10,000 subscribers — more generous than every other free tier combined. If your needs are basic (broadcasts, simple sequences, landing pages), you pay $0 for years. Paid starts at $39/mo (Creator), which is more than MailerLite at every paid tier — so Kit is the answer only while you fit in the free plan. Creators and newsletter operators fit; e-commerce businesses with automation needs don't.

4. GetResponse — feature parity, lower price

If you actually use Mailchimp's deeper features (advanced segmentation, multivariate testing, e-commerce integrations), GetResponse is the closest match — and it bundles webinars and richer automation at Starter ($19/$54/$174). It saves you $7–$96/mo depending on list size while keeping the features. See our GetResponse vs MailerLite head-to-head for the full comparison.

5. Flodesk — the design-first choice

Flodesk looks better than everything else here and knows it. The templates are genuinely beautiful, the builder is the most pleasant in the category, and pricing is flat-ish ($25–$155 annual). It loses on power: weaker automation, weaker segmentation, weaker analytics. If your brand lives or dies on how the email looks (wedding planners, photographers, boutiques, coaches), that's a fair trade. If you need segmentation and testing, pick anything else.

How to actually switch

One afternoon, three steps. 1. Dedupe and clean Mailchimp first — every alternative bills by unique subscribers (except Brevo), so removing duplicates and cold subscribers shrinks the plan you need; Mailchimp's own archive tool works. 2. Export your lists and templates. 3. Recreate automations — this is the real cost of switching: no tool imports Mailchimp's automation logic, so budget the afternoon to rebuild welcome sequences and flows by hand. Run the new tool in parallel for one send cycle before canceling Mailchimp, so nothing breaks mid-campaign.

And cancel correctly: Mailchimp bills for unsubscribed contacts right up to the cancellation, so archive or delete them before your renewal date, not after.

The honest channel rule (read this before picking any tool)

Email open rates average around 20–22% — meaning roughly four out of five reminder emails never get opened (medium.com/@johnwinter3710's no-show data). SMS open rates run above 90%, with most texts read within three minutes. That single split defines salon email strategy:

  • Reminders belong on SMS. The day-before + 2-hours-before double reminder cuts no-shows 29–38% (same source, vendor-tracked — directional), and those numbers are SMS-driven. An email-only reminder strategy reaches one client in five.
  • Email belongs to nurture and rebooking. Post-visit thank-yous, the "your color is due for a refresh" 6-week trigger, lapsed-client win-back, birthday promos, membership announcements. These are emails people read on the couch, not texts they glance at in a meeting.
  • Reviews get requested, not hoped for. Phorest's whole "salon software that grows revenue" thesis is that review requests go out automatically after every visit — the tool doesn't matter, the automation does.

So the ranking below is really a ranking of one question: which tool covers the SMS-for-reminders plus email-for-nurture job for the least money and setup pain?

What a salon or spa actually needs from email

Forget "engagement." The five jobs that matter:

  1. No-show prevention. 62% of no-shows happen because the client simply forgot (Phorest's data, via their revenue-growth guide). The fix is a 48-hour confirmation with a confirm/reschedule link, a 24-hour nudge for the unconfirmed, and a 2–3-hour same-day reminder (medium.com/@johnwinter3710). Deposit collection layers on top — deposits alone are linked to a 65% reduction in no-shows — and online bookers miss 49% fewer appointments than phone bookers (Phorest). Your email tool doesn't take the deposit; your booking app (Fresha, Vagaro, Booksy, GlossGenius, Square Appointments) does. The email tool supplies the reminder automation.
  2. Rebooking automation. A client who rebooks at checkout is meaningfully more likely to return than one told to call later (Phorest). The email side: trigger-based "your [service] is typically due in X weeks" messages — the 6-week color refresh, the 3-week lash fill, the quarterly facial. This is where automation depth actually matters, and where shallow tools fail.
  3. Lapsed-client win-back. Phorest's "Client Reconnect" feature auto-detects clients who haven't rebooked within their typical service interval and sends an automated SMS or email with a booking link. That's the job; any tool that can segment by "last appointment date" and send a conditional sequence can build it. The payoff is real: Boulevard's industry report found salons that proactively follow up by email earn up to 15% more revenue per location (businesswire.com, 2023 — Boulevard's data, their number, directional).
  4. Review-request sequences. One email after every visit: thank-you + direct Google review link. This is the intake funnel for salons the way referrals are for law firms — and it runs on a single post-visit automation.
  5. Membership and package announcements. Zenoti's 2026 benchmark is the strongest argument in beauty marketing right now: salons with membership programs grew revenue and retained guests at four times the rate of non-membership salons (cdn.sanity.io — Zenoti's data, their number, directional). Memberships, blowout clubs, and package subscriptions need email — launch announcements, renewal reminders, expiry alerts on prepaid credits. If you're launching one, your email tool earns its keep this year.

Notice the retail upsell isn't on the list. Boulevard's report found just 13% of retail sales happen during the first appointment — top-performing salons educate during the visit and sell retail through follow-up, not pressure at the chair. The post-visit "the product your stylist used, order it here" email is the retail channel. Pressure at checkout is what everyone else does.

The 5 campaigns that pay (steal this sequence)

Whichever tool you pick, build these five first:

  1. The reminder chain. 48 hours before: confirmation email or SMS with service, stylist, date, time, and a confirm/reschedule link. 24 hours before: nudge only the unconfirmed. 2–3 hours before: the same-day nudge. This is SMS-first, and it's the single highest-ROI automation a salon can build — two reminders cut no-shows 29–38% (medium.com/@johnwinter3710 — vendor-tracked, directional).
  2. The rebooking trigger. Per service, set the interval: color 6 weeks, lash fill 3 weeks, facial quarterly. "Your [service] with [stylist] is typically due — book your slot" goes out at interval minus 5 days. The tool needs service-history tagging for this; don't try to fake it with one generic blast.
  3. The post-visit sequence. Thank-you + review link the next day. The retail email 3 days later: "the [product] your stylist used, order it here" — educational, not pushy (Boulevard's 13%-at-first-visit finding is why). Two emails, one job each.
  4. The lapsed-client win-back. Segment by "no visit in 1.5x the typical interval for their service." Email 1: the soft check ("we miss you — here's a booking link"). Email 2 (10 days later, only to non-openers): a real offer, not a discount on everything — a complimentary add-on, a birthday-month treat. Win-back converts because the client already knows you; new-client acquisition is the expensive channel, and it's declining (Zenoti 2026).
  5. The membership launch drip. If you're launching a membership or blowout club: announcement email (what it is, the math), FAQ email (the objections), and the founding-member deadline. Zenoti's "4x revenue and retention growth vs non-membership salons" finding (their data, directional) is the pitch's proof point — and renewals run on the same reminder automation as rebooking.

The honest catches

Your booking app already does half this job — don't double-buy it. Fresha, Vagaro, Booksy, GlossGenius, and Square Appointments all ship SMS reminders, online booking, and review prompts. Before buying an email tool, audit what your booking app covers: if reminders and deposits live there (and they should — deposits alone cut no-shows ~65%), the email tool only needs to handle nurture. Two tools doing the same reminder job is how salons end up with double reminders and annoyed clients.

The 4x-membership stat is vendor data, not a promise. Zenoti sells salon software; its benchmark is real client data but selected and framed by the vendor. Memberships genuinely stabilize revenue — predictable monthly cash in a walk-in business — but 4x depends on execution: pricing the membership, promoting it, and keeping members booked. Treat it as direction, not a guarantee.

No-show math: most of your "loss" is not actually losable. A $400k-revenue salon bleeds $60k–$80k a year to no-shows (Professional Beauty Association figures via medium.com/@johnwinter3710) — but that number assumes every missed slot stays empty. A self-serve waitlist that auto-fills cancellations shrinks the real loss a lot. Price the prevention (reminders + deposits) against the slots you actually couldn't refill, not the gross figure.

Zenoti's calendar-inflation warning. Salons with rebooking rates above 30% show a striking pattern: 72% of first rebooks cancel (Zenoti 2026 benchmark). Rebooking without confirmation inflates the calendar with ghost appointments. Your rebooking trigger needs the confirmation step — the 48-hour link — or you're trading one problem for another.

FAQ

What's the cheapest email tool for a small salon?

MailerLite from $10/mo if your booking app handles SMS reminders. Brevo ($9/mo Starter for 5,000 emails, $18/mo Standard for the automations worth using) if you want email and SMS in one tool. All prices checked October 3, 2026 — verify live before checkout.

Should salon reminders be email or SMS?

SMS first, email as backup. Email open rates average 20–22% vs 90%+ for texts (medium.com/@johnwinter3710). The day-before + 2-hours-before SMS pair is the proven no-show killer; email is for the rebooking triggers and win-back sequences that need more room to persuade.

Is Flodesk good for salons?

Good for the weekly promo email that needs to look beautiful; wrong for the money sequences. No SMS, shallow automations without Pro ($28/mo at 1k) or Everything ($54/mo). Pick it for aesthetics, not for rebooking logic.

Do I even need email marketing if I use Fresha or Vagaro?

Your booking app covers reminders, deposits, and basic review prompts — that's the plumbing. Email marketing covers what the booking app can't: segmented newsletters, conditional rebooking triggers by service, lapsed-client win-back, membership launches, and retail follow-up. If you're doing none of those, the booking app is enough; if you're doing any of them, add the email tool.

What's the one metric that matters?

Rebooking rate — the percentage of clients who leave with their next appointment booked — and the confirmed version of it (Zenoti's 72%-of-first-rebooks-cancel finding is why). Track it per stylist, and let the automation chase the gap.

TL;DR
At 1,000 subscribers they're the same price ($19/mo). At 5,000, MailerLite is $5 cheaper. At 25,000, GetResponse is $5 cheaper. Price is a tie. Pick GetResponse if you want webinars, richer automation, and e-commerce features bundled into the starter tier. The billing traps

MailerLite's send cap. Comfort gives you 10× your subscriber count in sends per month. On a 5,000-subscriber list that's 50,000 sends — plenty for weekly newsletters (4 emails × 5,000 = 20,000), but a re-engagement campaign plus a welcome series plus weekly sends in the same month can get you close. Check before you buy.

GetResponse's feature cliff. The Starter tier includes webinars and automations, but the good versions of both live one tier up. If webinars are the reason you picked GetResponse, verify the Starter webinar limits against your actual event size before committing — the upgrade to Marketer is the real price of that feature.

Pick GetResponse if: you run webinars and want them in the same tool; you sell online and want abandoned-cart and promo codes without a second app; or you like having every feature available even if you use half of them.

Pick MailerLite if: you send newsletters and promos and want the tool to stay out of your way; you're setting this up for a client or a team that hates software; or you simply don't need webinars and e-commerce automation.

Migration notes

GetResponse offers free migration (lists, automations, templates) from most competitors including MailerLite. MailerLite imports lists easily but doesn't rebuild automations — budget an afternoon to recreate your workflows if you switch to them. Both charge by unique subscribers, so dedupe your list before importing; a 5% duplicate rate is a free $2–$5/mo.

Awkward article to write about the tool with the best free plan in email marketing: Kit (formerly ConvertKit) emails up to 10,000 subscribers at $0 — nothing else comes close. Yet "Kit alternatives" is one of the most-searched phrases in the category, and the searches spike around two moments: when a free-plan user's list crosses 10,001 subscribers and the bill goes from $0 to $139 a month overnight, and when a paid-plan user does the annual math and realizes they're paying creator prices for a newsletter. Both complaints are legitimate. Below: the six exits that genuinely make sense, matched to the reason you're leaving — and the honest case that most of you should stay right where you are.

TL;DR
Leaving over the cliff (10,001 subscribers = $0 to $139/mo)? If you're a newsletter-only business, that's a real reason — MailerLite or AWeber cost a fraction at every paid band. Leaving because you monetize with paid subscriptions? Beehiiv charges 0% platform fee and Ghost gives you full ownership — both beat Kit's commerce fees. Leaving because you sell courses and need funnels? GetResponse bundles webinars and funnels for about the same money. Leaving because you want real automation/CRM? ActiveCampaign. But if your list is under 10,000 and you're mostly broadcasting, there is no cheaper alternative than Kit's $0 free plan — staying is the correct financial decision and almost nobody says it.

Why creators actually leave Kit

Four real reasons, ranked by how often they come up — not the invented grievances that pad out other "alternatives" listicles.

1. The 10,001-subscriber cliff. This is the number-one driver, and it's structural. Kit's free plan covers 10,000 subscribers; subscriber 10,001 moves you to Creator at $139/mo — instantly, from $0. That's the steepest $0-to-paid jump in the entire email category. A newsletter growing steadily hits it as a surprise bill, usually mid-year, and the first reaction is to price the competition out of pure spite. We charted the full band-by-band curve in our Kit pricing guide.

2. Paid plans cost roughly double the value tools. Kit Creator runs $39/mo at 1,000 subscribers, $89 at 5,000, $199 at 25,000. MailerLite's Comfort plan — with comparable core features — runs $19, $49, and $179 at the same sizes. You're paying a $20–$60/mo premium at every band for Kit's creator-specific tooling (commerce, Creator Network, free migration). If you use that tooling, it's a fair premium. If you just send newsletters and a welcome sequence, you're paying for someone else's feature set. And note: Kit raised prices roughly 35% in late 2025 — any guide showing a $29 entry price is stale.

3. Selling costs you on every transaction. Kit Commerce — digital products, paid newsletters, subscriptions — carries a transaction fee on every plan, including free. Kit's own docs state 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction (their cut is 0.6%; the rest is card processing). But independent sources have reported different figures through 2026, so check the current Kit Commerce page before you price a product — the fee landscape here is genuinely in flux. The Creator Network's Paid Recommendations (getting paid to promote other newsletters) carries a 23.5% fee to Kit. It's a growth feature, not a revenue feature: run the math before counting that income.

4. The free plan excludes the visual automation builder. The free tier's one real catch: welcome sequences, drips, and behavior-triggered emails are Creator features. The moment a new subscriber should automatically receive a 5-email welcome series, you're paying $39/mo. Kit's free plan is the best deal in the category for broadcasters — and a $39/mo floor for anyone who needs automation. Price the plan you'll need in 6 months, not the one you need today.

The 6 alternatives, matched to your reason for leaving

1. MailerLite — if it's the price

The direct swap for paid-plan Kit users who don't use the creator tooling. Comfort runs $20/mo cheaper than Creator at 1,000 subscribers and $40/mo cheaper at 5,000 — with the automations Kit's free plan withholds, a drag-and-drop editor that's just as fast, and unlimited-subscriber imports. What you give up: the creator commerce features, the Creator Network, and Kit's free migration service. What you gain: the cheapest paid tiers in the category and none of the cliff anxiety — though Comfort caps sends at 10× your subscriber count, so frequent senders should price the Power tier instead. Full band math in MailerLite vs Kit.

2. Beehiiv — if it's paid newsletters and growth

The newsletter-native exit. Beehiiv was built for exactly what many Kit users do: grow a newsletter and monetize it. It charges 0% platform fee on paid subscriptions (versus Kit's per-transaction commerce cut and Substack's 10%), bundles a real referral program, an ad network, and newsletter recommendations into the platform, and its free Launch tier covers 2,500 subscribers. The trade: Scale runs $49/mo at 1,000 subscribers — $10 more than Kit Creator — and if your business is email courses, product launches, and automations rather than a paid newsletter, Beehiiv's tooling is thinner than Kit's. Our authors & newsletters guide runs the full fee showdown.

3. Ghost — if it's ownership

The exit for creators who've decided platform fees are a tax they refuse to pay. Ghost is open-source publishing with built-in paid memberships at 0% platform fee — you pay for hosting (Ghost Pro Starter ~$9–15/mo, or self-host for the cost of a server) and Stripe's processing, nothing to Ghost. You own the code, the data, and the audience relationship in a way no SaaS email tool allows. The trade: it's a publishing platform with email attached, not an email platform — the automation, segmentation, and deliverability tooling are far leaner than Kit's, and you're the one keeping it updated. Right for established publishers; overkill for a newsletter still finding its voice.

4. Substack — if it's dead-simple paid newsletters

The simplest exit of all, and the one with the biggest hidden cost. Substack is free until you charge: no monthly fee, instant setup, built-in discovery that actually sends you readers. Then it takes 10% of every paid subscription, forever — on top of Stripe's processing. At $1,000/mo in paid subscriptions, that's $100/mo to Substack for sending email; at $5,000/mo, it's $500. Kit's $39–$199/mo starts looking cheap the moment revenue scales. Switch to Substack if you're validating a paid newsletter from zero and discovery matters more than margins; graduate off it (or never start) once the 10% cut exceeds a flat-fee tool's bill.

5. GetResponse — if it's webinars, funnels, and marketing breadth

The graduation move in the other direction: not cheaper, broader. GetResponse Starter matches Kit Creator's ballpark at every size ($19/$54/$174 vs $39/$89/$199 — actually cheaper at 1k and 5k) while bundling native webinars, conversion funnels, and deeper automation that Kit doesn't have. If you're a creator whose business is becoming courses, coaching, and launches rather than a newsletter, this is where the tooling grows with you instead of against you. The step up to Marketer ($59/mo) is where the real automation depth lives. See GetResponse vs MailerLite for the head-to-head.

6. ActiveCampaign — if it's real automation and CRM

When "email tool" undersells what you need: ActiveCampaign is a marketing-automation platform with a CRM attached. Starter runs $19/mo at 1,000 subscribers — half of Kit Creator — but $99/mo at 5,000, which is $10 more than Kit. You pay for branching automation, lead scoring, and sales pipelines that Kit will never have. This is a business-systems upgrade, not a cost play; creators who just send newsletters are buying complexity they won't use. See ActiveCampaign vs Kit for the automation-vs-simplicity trade-off.

The honest case for staying: the best free tier in the category

Here's the part most "alternatives" articles won't tell you, because they'd rather earn the switch commission: if your list is under 10,000 and you're mainly sending newsletters or broadcasts, there is no cheaper alternative than Kit's $0 free plan. MailerLite's free tier covers 250 subscribers. AWeber's covers 500. Beehiiv's covers 2,500. Kit covers 10,000 — and you can sell digital products on it with 0% platform-side fee on the commerce side. A newsletter growing from 500 to 8,000 subscribers costs you literally nothing on Kit and real money almost everywhere else.

The stay math is simple. Stay on free while you're under 10,000 and broadcasting — the cliff only hurts if you walk into it unplanned, so set a reminder at 9,000 subscribers and decide then. If you cross 10,000 and the $139/mo stings, that's the genuine decision point: either the creator tooling earns its keep (commerce, Creator Network, paid recommendations as growth) or the cheaper tools above are your move. And if you're on Creator already and using the commerce features, don't let a listicle talk you into a migration that costs an afternoon plus a deliverability warm-up to save money you weren't actually wasting.

How to actually switch (if you're sure)

1. Clean first. Kit counts unique, active subscribers only — unsubscribed and duplicates don't inflate the bill — so dedupe before export anyway; every alternative bills by subscriber count and a clean list shrinks the plan you need on day one. 2. Export everything — lists, segments, tags, sequences. 3. Rebuild automations by hand — this is the real cost; no tool imports another's automation logic, so budget the afternoon and rebuild the welcome sequence first. 4. Warm up the new sender — run both tools in parallel for one full send cycle and watch deliverability before you cancel Kit.

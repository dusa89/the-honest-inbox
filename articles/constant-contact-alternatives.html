Two reasons everyone is shopping for Constant Contact alternatives. First, the billing: contact bands that punish growth — going from 500 to 5,000 contacts takes Standard from $35/mo to $110/mo, and the same list at 10,000 costs $160. Second, the plan ladder: real automation and Constant Contact-logo-free emails live behind Standard ($35/mo) or Premium ($80/mo) — the $12 Lite plan shows Constant Contact's branding in your emails and ships only basic automation. Every tool below costs less than Standard at 1,000, 5,000, and 25,000 contacts. The only question is which one fits how you actually work.

MailerLite is the straight switch ($19/$49/$179 — saves $36–$131/mo vs Standard at every size). Kit is free to 10,000 subscribers if your needs are basic. The 7 alternatives, matched to your situation

1. MailerLite — the straight switch

If your Constant Contact setup is newsletters, promos, and a welcome email, MailerLite does all of it for roughly a third of Standard's price: $19 vs $55–$75 at 1,000 contacts, ~$49 vs $110 at 5,000. The builder is simpler than Constant Contact's (fewer template quirks), automations are included at the entry paid tier — the same automations that cost you Standard on Constant Contact — and there's no platform branding. Watch the 10x-list send cap on Comfort (10,000 emails/month on a 1,000-contact plan — fine for weekly senders, tight for daily ones) and the free tier's 250-subscriber ceiling. Our full math is in MailerLite pricing explained.

2. GetResponse — feature parity for less

If you actually use Constant Contact's deeper features (segmentation, multivariate testing, event tools), GetResponse Starter is the closest match — $19/$54/$174, with webinars and richer automation bundled in. The warning from our GetResponse pricing explainer: the $19-to-$59 Starter-to-Marketer jump is the steepest single-tier jump in email — Marketer is where the real automation lives, so budget for $59 if your workflows are complex. Either way it undercuts Standard by $36–$136/mo.

3. Kit — the free-to-10k play

Kit's free plan covers 10,000 subscribers — more generous than every other free tier combined. If your needs are basic (broadcasts, simple sequences, landing pages), you pay $0 for years while Constant Contact charges you $12–$120 for the same list. Paid starts at Creator ($39/$89/$199) — more than MailerLite at every paid tier — so Kit is the answer only while you fit in the free plan. Creators and newsletter operators fit; e-commerce businesses with automation needs don't. Full math in Kit pricing explained.

4. AWeber — the friendliest switch

AWeber is the other old guard, and it kept up better than Constant Contact did: $19/mo Starter at 1,000 contacts, a $45/mo tier that adds webinars and richer automation, and phone support on every paid plan — the hand-holding that Constant Contact refugees miss. It doesn't win on raw price at 5,000+ (its bands climb steeply up there, per our AWeber vs GetResponse research), but for businesses that want to feel looked after while paying less than Standard, it's the natural landing spot.

5. Brevo — the volume play

Brevo doesn't charge per subscriber at all — it charges per email sent. A 25,000-person list with a monthly newsletter costs roughly $139/mo where Constant Contact Standard charges $310. The tradeoffs are real: the entry Starter tier has no automation, and the 2,000-contact cumulative automation ceiling is the quietest trap in the category — read our Brevo-to-MailerLite migration guide before committing. Brevo is the answer when your list is big and your send cadence is modest. (No verified affiliate program on our bench — an uncompensated recommendation.)

6. Flodesk — the design-first pick

Flodesk looks better than everything else here and knows it: $38/month flat, unlimited contacts and sends — no contact bands, no growth penalty. The templates are genuinely beautiful and the builder is the most pleasant in the category. It loses on power: weaker automation, weaker segmentation, weaker analytics than Standard or any tool above. If your brand lives or dies on how the email looks (wedding planners, photographers, boutiques, coaches), that's a fair trade. If you need segmentation and testing, pick anything else. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

7. ActiveCampaign — the automation power pick

If the drip logic is your business — behavior-triggered sequences, lead scoring, CRM-grade automation — ActiveCampaign's Starter ($19/mo at 1,000 contacts) is the cheapest entry into serious automation, and the $59/mo tier is the real product. The catch at Starter: exactly one automation, which makes it a demo rather than a tool — budget the jump. Constant Contact Premium ($80/mo at 500 contacts) is the plan you'd be leaving, and ActiveCampaign does more at $59. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

Who should stay on Constant Contact

Three honest cases. One: nonprofits on the 30%-off annual prepay — at some bands that discount genuinely changes the math; run it against Comfort before moving. Two: SMS users — Constant Contact's SMS add-on ($10+/mo) keeps text and email under one roof, and MailerLite is email-only, so switching means a second vendor and a second bill. Three: a tiny list on Lite at $12 — the cheapest bill in the category, as long as you're honest that the branding and missing automation aren't costing you customers. Everyone else is paying the contact cliff: price the list you'll have in 12 months, not the one you have now.

How to actually switch

One afternoon, three steps. 1. Clean first — every alternative bills by unique contacts, so dedupe and remove cold subscribers before you export; the plan you need shrinks with the list. 2. Export contacts (unsubscribes included — your new tool needs the suppression list), lists, and templates. 3. Rebuild automations by hand — this is the real cost of switching; no tool imports Constant Contact's automation logic. Run the new tool in parallel for one send cycle before canceling. And cancel correctly: a 2026 review reports Constant Contact subscriptions require calling customer service to cancel — plan for the call, and don't cancel before your final send.

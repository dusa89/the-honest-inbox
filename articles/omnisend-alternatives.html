Omnisend is a genuinely good ecommerce email tool — the free plan (250 contacts, 500 emails, 500 web push, with 24/7 support and A/B testing) is the most generous structure in the category. But four things push stores off it. One: Standard caps your monthly sends at 12 times your contact count — a 1,000-contact store gets 12,000 sends, and frequent senders hit the ceiling (verified September 2026). Two: unused SMS credits expire after 60 days with no rollover. Three: advanced reporting lives only on Pro ($59/mo and up). Four: the bill counts non-subscribers — abandoned-cart visitors who never opted in still move the tier. Each alternative below fixes a specific one of those four.

TL;DR
Drip is the unlimited-sends ecommerce pick ($39 to 2,500 people — pricier at entry than Omnisend's $20, but no 12x cap ever). MailerLite is the simple cheap switch ($19/$49/$179). GetResponse is the funnels-and-webinars pick ($19/$54/$174). Brevo is the anti-Omnisend billing (send-volume pricing, unlimited contacts). Kit is free to 10,000 subscribers. ActiveCampaign is the automation-depth + CRM pick ($19/$59). Klaviyo is the data-heavy upgrade — costs more, buys deeper analytics. Every paid tier below is monthly billing; the switches take an afternoon.

The 7 alternatives, matched to why you're leaving

1. Drip — the unlimited-sends ecommerce pick

If the 12x send cap is your actual complaint, Drip kills it outright: one plan, unlimited email sends, ecommerce-native workflows (abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase, win-back), onsite popups, and dynamic segments. The honest price math: Drip is $39/mo where Omnisend is $20 at 1,000 contacts — nearly double at entry. But the gap closes fast: $89 vs $81 at 5,000, and Drip's sends never cap while Omnisend's do. Pick Drip when you're a frequent sender hitting the ceiling. Skip it if you send twice a month — you'd pay more for capacity you don't use. No free plan (14-day trial only). (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

2. MailerLite — the simple cheap switch

If your Omnisend setup is campaigns, promos, and a handful of flows, MailerLite does all of it for less: $19 vs $20 at 1,000 contacts, ~$49 vs $81 at 5,000. Simpler builder, no send-cap anxiety, no non-subscriber billing weirdness. The honest gap: MailerLite is generalist email — it doesn't do Omnisend's ecommerce-specific flow depth or web push. Stores whose flows are the business logic should look at Drip or ActiveCampaign instead. Full math in MailerLite pricing explained.

3. GetResponse — the funnels-and-webinars pick

Stores that sell through content — webinars, courses, funnels — get more here than on Omnisend: Starter at $19/$54/$174 beats Omnisend Standard at every tier, and adds built-in webinar hosting, funnel builders, and course tools Omnisend never had. The warning from our GetResponse pricing explainer: the $19-to-$59 Starter-to-Marketer jump is the steepest single-tier jump in email — real automation lives in Marketer. Either way it undercuts Omnisend's bill.

4. Brevo — the anti-Omnisend billing

Brevo is the structural answer to Omnisend's two billing complaints: it charges by email sent, not by contact — unlimited contacts, so abandoned-cart visitors who never opted in cost you nothing. $9–$18 to start, roughly $139 at 25k-send volume vs Omnisend's $282. The honest tradeoffs are real and we name them in our Brevo-to-MailerLite migration guide: the entry tier has no automation, and the 2,000-contact automation ceiling is the quietest trap in the category. Pick Brevo when your list is big and your automation needs are modest — exactly the stores Omnisend's contact-counting punishes most. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

5. Kit — the free-to-10k play

Kit's free plan covers 10,000 subscribers — it swallows Omnisend's entire entry-and-mid tier whole. If your needs are basic (broadcasts, simple sequences, landing pages), you pay $0 for years while Omnisend charges $20–$132 for the same range. Paid starts at Creator ($39/$89/$199) — pricier than Omnisend Standard paid tiers — so Kit is the answer only while you fit in free. Simple stores fit; automation-heavy stores don't. Full math in Kit pricing explained.

6. ActiveCampaign — the automation-depth + CRM pick

If your objection is "Omnisend is ecommerce-shallow for my workflow" — service businesses, B2B, anything with a sales process — ActiveCampaign's automation depth and built-in CRM is the real upgrade. Starter at $19/mo (1,000 contacts) is the cheapest entry into serious automation, but the fine print matters: exactly one automation on Starter makes it a demo — the $59/mo tier is the actual product. The stores that should pick it are the ones whose flows outgrew "campaigns plus flows" and became business logic. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

7. Klaviyo — the data-heavy upgrade

Leaving Omnisend for more depth, not less? Klaviyo's predictive analytics, revenue-based segmentation, and Shopify attribution are in another league — but the bill goes the wrong way: $30/$100/$360–$400 vs Omnisend's $20/$81/$282. You don't switch from Omnisend to Klaviyo to save money; you switch because your team lives in the analytics dashboards and the lift pays the surcharge. The full honest math — including who should stay — is in Best Klaviyo alternatives. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

Honorable mentions

Moosend ($9/mo, 30-day trial, unlimited sends) is the budget ecommerce pick — simple automation at a price that undercuts everything here except Kit's free tier. Sender has one of the most generous free plans anywhere (2,500 subscribers, 15,000 emails/month with core automation) if the free-plan hunt is your game.

Who should stay on Omnisend

Three honest cases. One: free-plan users — 250 contacts, 500 emails, 500 web push, with 24/7 support and A/B testing is the best-structured free plan in the category; nothing above matches it. Two: multichannel stores — if you actually use email + SMS + web push inside the same flows, Omnisend's omnichannel builder is genuinely the product, and rebuilding that across two tools is work. Three: stores the billing doesn't punish — if your sends stay inside the 12x cap and your list is clean (non-subscribers archived), Omnisend's $20/$81/$282 is fair value and the migration labor buys nothing. Clean the list first either way: archiving abandoned-cart ghosts and dead profiles can drop you a full tier before you spend a dollar.

How to actually switch

One afternoon, three steps. 1. Clean first — Omnisend bills contacts including non-subscribers, so every ghost profile is real money; archive and delete before you export, and the plan you need shrinks with the list. 2. Export contacts (unsubscribes and bounces included — your new tool needs the suppression list), segments, and templates. 3. Rebuild flows by hand — no tool imports another's flow logic; this is the real cost of switching. Run the new tool in parallel for one send cycle before canceling, and don't cancel Omnisend before your final send.

Locksmithing is the emergency business — the customer who is locked out at midnight remembers exactly one name: whoever answered. A typical emergency lockout call runs about $75–$150, rekeying runs roughly $20–$50 per cylinder, and a full lock change lands around $100–$200 (published ranges from HomeAdvisor and Angi 2026 roundups — real markets vary by labor rates and hardware). The playbook: a lockout follow-up that turns a one-time panic call into the household's forever locksmith, a move-in rekey engine that rides the turnover wave, a commercial master-key and access-control drip that lands property managers and HOAs, a seasonal lock-health campaign, and a referral machine built on the deepest trust signal in home services — "he got me in."

Forget "engagement metrics." The five jobs that matter:

  1. The lockout follow-up. The emergency call is locksmithing's whole reputation engine — and the customer who just paid $75–$150 to get back inside is your most grateful audience on Earth, for about 72 hours. The follow-up: the thank-you email the next morning ("glad we got you in — here's my direct number, save it"), the rekey prompt two weeks later (most lockouts end with a rekey — the customer just met you, trusts you, and still has the receipt), and the review ask attached (the 5-star written at peak relief is the review that wins the next 2 a.m. Google search). One panic call, one follow-up sequence, a lifetime of calls — that is the highest-ROI email in this trade.
  2. The move-in rekey engine. Every lease turnover and every house sale is a rekey job waiting for an invitation: new owners and new tenants should rekey every exterior lock, and most of them know it. The capture: the realtor-adjacent list (the agents who close in your market — one relationship email a quarter), the property-manager rekey agreement (turnover units on a standing rate), and the new-resident welcome drip for your consumer list ("moved in? here's the rekey checklist"). At roughly $20–$50 per cylinder and several cylinders per door, the rekey is the volume product of the trade — and it is recurring forever, because people keep moving.
  3. The commercial master-key drip. The commercial buyer is worth twenty residential ones: the property-manager segment (master key systems, turnover rekeys, the standing "our guy" agreement), the HOA segment (common-area locks, mailbox systems, the annual rekey), and the small-business segment (access control upgrades, the smart-lock conversion). The engine: the monthly B2B touch — capacity, current availability, one useful line — segmented away from consumers entirely. The master-key system is a relationship product, and the drip is the relationship.
  4. The seasonal lock-health campaign. Locks have a calendar: the pre-winter freeze campaign (frozen locks, stiff deadbolts — the service call that prevents the January lockout), the pre-summer travel season (the smart-lock upgrade pitch for vacationers), and the storm-season security check. The calendar is honest demand — cold and travel sell locks the way heat sells air conditioners.
  5. The referral machine. Nobody forgets the locksmith who got them in at 2 a.m. The machine: the saved-number email ("save this — the next lockout you won't have to search"), the neighbor referral ask ("everybody knows somebody locked out once a year — the referral card with your name on it"), and the realtor introduction kit for agents (the leave-behind that gets the agent's client your number at closing). Trust is the product; the email keeps the name on the fridge.

The honest catches

  • The 3-minute answer wins the lockout, not the drip. Email keeps the customer for life, but the fastest phone answer wins the job — the locksmith who answers on the first ring beats the locksmith with the prettier follow-up. Automate the after-care, not the availability.
  • Your list import is the real setup cost. Every tool here imports CSV; the work is exporting the customer list and tagging it properly — job type (residential/commercial/landlord), service (lockout/rekey/master-key/access-control), source (realtor/property-manager/HOA/direct) (estimate — most dispatch exports won't have all of these out of the box). Budget an afternoon, not ten minutes. A list without service tags can't run the rekey engine or the commercial drip — the tags are the product. Our deliverability audit checklist covers the list-hygiene half.
  • Email can't fix a 2-star reputation. The gratitude drip multiplies what the vans deliver — a company with 2.3 stars will automate its way to more detailed complaints, and the locksmith who scratched the door frame gets photographed. Fix the service first, then automate the ask.
  • The commercial list is earned, not bought. Never buy a property-manager list and blast it — that's the spam trap, and the complaint ceiling ends your sending career. Every property manager, realtor, HOA board, and business owner on your list should have met you, referred to you, or opted in.

How often should a locksmith company email past customers?

Twice a year on the lock calendar — the October freeze-prevention campaign and the May travel-season smart-lock pitch — plus the lockout gratitude drip after every emergency call and the quarterly commercial touch. Past customers don't need a newsletter — they need the number on the fridge and the email that arrives before the freeze. The commercial segment is different: monthly, short, useful.

Can I email property managers and realtors about rekey contracts?

Yes — the ones who know you, on a real relationship, as B2B outreach with a clear opt-out. The commercial master-key drip above is a capacity email to people who've worked with you, referred to you, or asked to hear from you. Never buy a property-manager or realtor list and blast it — that's the spam trap, and the complaint ceiling ends your sending career.

Should I email residential lockout customers and commercial accounts from the same tool?

Same tool, different segments — not different accounts. The lockout gratitude drip branches by service (the rekey prompt for homeowners vs the master-key pitch for property managers), and the campaigns run on separate calendars. Tagging handles it; one account keeps the list, the automations, and the referral engine in one place.

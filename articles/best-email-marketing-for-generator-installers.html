The honest catches

  • The sizing lie: never sell 26kW when 14kW covers the essentials. A 22kW whole-home unit typically runs $11,000-$15,000 installed (2026 guide, labeled) — oversizing by one frame adds thousands for capacity the house never draws. The email names the load calculation: critical circuits (fridge, furnace, lights, Wi-Fi, medical) vs whole home (A/C, everything at once). The installer who sizes honestly gets the review that names the storm.
  • The permit reality: never sell the install date until the permit lands. Permits, gas-line tie-ins, trenching, and inspections stall installs — the quote names the realistic window, and the permit-wait nurture carries the buyer through it. The email that promises "next week" before the permit is filed is a cancellation with a marketing budget.
  • The dead-battery truth: the maintenance visit is the product. Per an owner-operated electrical contractor's October 2026 trade-press commentary (labeled): most generator failures found during outages were visible months earlier — dead battery, rodent nest, stale fuel. Never email a "maintenance-free peace of mind" promise; the email sells the annual visit, and the visit is what keeps the unit alive.
  • Keep storm buyers, service-plan members, and commercial accounts on separate lanes. The builder doesn't want the homeowner's storm-season blast; the service-plan member doesn't want the sales pitch; the surveyed-not-quoted buyer doesn't want the install-day texts. Three buyers, three cadences, zero cross-sends. The operator pattern (estimate, labeled): the installer who cross-sends teaches all three audiences to ignore the list.
  • The fuel-source honesty: name the trade-offs, never hide the tank. Natural gas tie-ins, propane tanks, and diesel each change the install, the runtime cost (~$25-$150/day in fuel while running, 2026 guide, labeled), and the permitting. The email names the fuel-source decision in the survey funnel — the buyer who learns about the propane tank at install time is a one-star review in progress.

FAQ

How much does a whole-home generator cost installed?

Generac's own typical project range is $8,000-$16,000 (generator + equipment + installation, company figure, labeled). 2026 pricing guides put typical projects at $9,000-$20,000, with complex installs reaching $25,000+ (industry guides, labeled). Equipment-only, 2026 guide figures (labeled): a 22kW Generac runs roughly $5,500-$6,500, Cummins $6,500-$8,000, Briggs & Stratton $5,000-$6,000, and Champion $4,000-$5,500; labor and permitting add another $3,500-$8,000. Ownership adds roughly $300-$600 a year in maintenance and $25-$150 a day in fuel while running.

How many homes actually have a standby generator?

About one in 20 U.S. homes has permanent backup power (market-research estimate, labeled), with Texas, Florida, and California as the growth epicenters — and NOAA recorded 27 U.S. billion-dollar disasters in 2024 (federal figure, labeled). Generac's own earnings statement says only 6.5% of the U.S. addressable market is penetrated (company statement, labeled), with each point of incremental penetration worth roughly $4 billion in retail value — the buyer pool is almost entirely unpenetrated.

How long do power outages actually last?

U.S. customers averaged 11 hours of power interruptions in 2024 — nearly double the previous decade's annual average (EIA Electric Power Annual 2024, federal figures, labeled) — with major events accounting for about 80% of those hours. Routine outages run only about two hours a year; major-event interruptions averaged nearly nine hours per customer. The map is local: South Carolina averaged 52.3 hours in the Helene year; Louisiana averaged 33.5 hours a year from 2020-2024 (federal reliability data, labeled).

Who dominates the standby generator market?

Generac holds above 70% of the U.S. home-standby market with roughly 9,200 dealers — the largest dealer network in the category (company's 4Q2024 earnings statement, labeled). Kohler, Cummins, Briggs & Stratton, and Champion round out the field; Generac's 2025 launch of the first 28kW air-cooled model (industry coverage, labeled) pushed the air-cooled segment further into whole-home territory.

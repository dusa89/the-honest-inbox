Why people actually leave MailerLite

Four real reasons, ranked by how often they come up — not the invented grievances that pad out other "alternatives" listicles.

1. The free plan got gutted (June 2026). 1,000 subscribers and 12,000 emails a month became 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails. If you were happily living on the old free plan, you now either pay or leave — and this is the single biggest driver of the "MailerLite alternatives" search. The honest comparison: AWeber's free tier now covers 500 subscribers / 3,000 emails per month, GetResponse's covers 500 contacts / 2,500 sends, and Kit's free plan covers 10,000 subscribers. MailerLite — the cheapest paid tool in the category — now has the weakest free plan in it.

2. Comfort's 10× send cap. Comfort is cheap because the sends are capped: 1,000 subscribers means 10,000 emails a month, 5,000 subscribers means 50,000. Illustrative math: at 5,000 subscribers ($49/mo Comfort), a weekly broadcast to the full list runs ~20,000 emails a month; a 5-email welcome sequence adds roughly 5,000–10,000 depending on signup volume; one promo blast with a reminder adds ~10,000. That's 35,000–40,000 — comfortable. Add a second weekly send or a full-list re-engagement blast and you're at the ceiling, and everything past it is a reason to upgrade. Frequent senders hit this wall; infrequent senders never notice it.

3. Account approval friction. MailerLite manually reviews new accounts before your first send — a genuine anti-spam measure. Most accounts clear it, but legitimate businesses do report sitting in review for a day or more (a recurring pattern in user reviews; we haven't hit it first-hand). If you need to send today, tools with instant activation exist.

4. Outgrowing the simplicity. MailerLite does email well and deliberately little else. The day you need built-in webinars, real sales funnels, advanced automation branching, or a CRM attached to your list, you're shopping — not because MailerLite failed, but because you graduated.

The 6 alternatives, matched to your reason for leaving

1. Kit — if you're a creator

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) is the answer when your list is the business — newsletters, paid subscriptions, digital products. The free plan covers 10,000 subscribers, which swallows the free-tier complaint whole: you can run on $0 for years. Paid Creator starts at $39/mo (1k) — $20 more than MailerLite Comfort at every tier — so you're paying for the creator tooling (commerce, paid newsletters, creator network), not for savings. Don't switch to Kit to save money; switch because you sell to an audience. Full price curves in MailerLite vs Kit.

2. GetResponse — if you outgrew the simplicity

This is the graduation move. GetResponse Starter matches Comfort's price at 1,000 subscribers ($19) and stays within a few dollars at every size ($54 vs $49 at 5k; $174 vs $179 at 25k) — while bundling native webinars, conversion funnels, and deeper automation that MailerLite simply doesn't have. If reason #4 is yours, this is the one switch where you gain features without paying more. The step up to Marketer ($59/mo) is where the real automation depth lives. See GetResponse vs MailerLite for the full head-to-head.

3. AWeber — if you want a better free tier or human support

The sleeper pick. AWeber's free plan — 500 subscribers, 3,000 emails a month — is now objectively more generous than MailerLite's gutted 250/2,500, which makes it the honest answer for reason #1. Paid Lite starts at $25/mo (1k), a few dollars above Comfort, and every paid plan includes phone support — a real differentiator if you'd rather call a human than search a help center. AWeber won't win on automation depth, but for newsletters-and-promos businesses it does the job with the best support in the category. Compare the two old guards in AWeber vs GetResponse.

4. Brevo — if your list is big and your sends are rare

Brevo charges per email sent, not per subscriber — a monthly newsletter to 25,000 people runs roughly $139/mo where per-subscriber tools charge $174–$199. But read the fine print against your reason for leaving: if you're fleeing MailerLite's send cap because you email often, Brevo's volume tiers punish exactly that behavior and the savings evaporate. Brevo is the answer for big-list-infrequent-send businesses only — and note its entry tier has no automation, so welcome sequences need the higher Business tier.

5. ActiveCampaign — if you need real automation and CRM

When "email tool" undersells what you need: ActiveCampaign is a marketing-automation platform with a CRM attached. Starter runs $19/mo at 1,000 subscribers — the same as Comfort — but $99/mo at 5,000, nearly double. You pay for branching automation, lead scoring, and sales pipelines that MailerLite will never have. This is a business-systems upgrade, not a cost play; small businesses that just send newsletters are buying complexity they won't use. See ActiveCampaign vs Kit for the automation-vs-simplicity trade-off.

6. Flodesk — if design is the whole brand

Flodesk's templates are genuinely the prettiest in email marketing, and for wedding planners, photographers, boutiques, and coaches, how the email looks is the marketing. Pro runs $25–$155 (annual, shown monthly) — competitive with Comfort on price. The trade: weaker automation, weaker segmentation, weaker analytics than everything else on this page. Fair trade for a design-first brand; a bad trade for anyone who segments and tests.

The honest case for staying: the Power tier

Here's the part most "alternatives" articles won't tell you, because they'd rather earn the switch commission: if your only complaint is the send cap, the fix costs $13–$20 a month, not a migration. MailerLite Power starts at $25/mo at 500 subscribers ($13 more than Comfort) and runs $39/mo at 1,000 and $69/mo at 5,000 ($20 more than Comfort at both) — with unlimited sends. At every size, Power undercuts Kit Creator ($39/$89) and GetResponse Marketer ($59 at 1k), so the upgrade keeps you on the cheapest serious tool in the category while deleting the cap entirely. The switching math nobody does: migrating costs an afternoon (export, import, rebuild every automation by hand — no tool imports another's automation logic), a week of parallel sending to catch breakage, and a deliverability warm-up on the new IP. That's worth it for webinars, funnels, or a real CRM. It is not worth it to dodge a $20/mo upgrade.

AWeber and GetResponse have sold email marketing since the dial-up era. But they aged into two completely different animals. AWeber stayed an email tool — and became the cheapest honest one for small lists. GetResponse turned itself into an all-in-one: email, webinars, sales funnels, courses, and a website builder under one bill. At 1,000 contacts the capability-matched plans (AWeber Plus vs GetResponse Marketer) sit $14 apart — and that $14 buys you a fundamentally different product.

TL;DR
Pick GetResponse if webinars, funnels, or courses are in your future — Marketer at $59/mo is the only plan in email marketing that ships native webinars, and one bill replaces two or three tools. Pick AWeber if you want email and nothing else: Plus at $45/mo undercuts Marketer, phone support comes on every paid plan, and the free tier is genuinely usable. Neither is the cheapest starter — that's GetResponse Starter at $19, but its single-automation limit makes it a demo, not a home.

What the money actually buys (it's not just price)

These are no longer the same category of product, and the comparison only makes sense if you know which job you're hiring for.

GetResponse became an all-in-one platform. Native webinars (100 attendees on Marketer, 300 on Creator at $69/mo) — the only email platform in our 2026 audit that ships webinars as a product feature rather than an integration. Sales funnels with promo codes and revenue reports, abandoned-cart recovery, a website builder, an AI course creator (up to 500 students, 0% transaction fees on Creator), and unlimited monthly sends on every paid plan. If your business runs webinars, launches, or a store, Marketer consolidates two or three vendor bills into one.

AWeber stayed an email tool — and sharpened that edge. Core automation, split testing, landing pages, signup forms, web push notifications, and ecommerce integrations. The moat is human: phone support on every paid plan (rare in this price band), plus a Done For You service — $79 one-time setup for a complete email system, then Plus-tier monthly. For a solo operator who wants a person on the phone and no feature bloat, that matters more than webinars ever will.

Three gotchas before you decide

  • AWeber bills your unsubscribers. Unsubscribed contacts still count toward your subscriber total — the plan you pay for prices the list you have, including the dead weight. GetResponse has its own version: the same email address on multiple lists counts as multiple contacts, which can trigger a recurring list-extension fee. On both platforms, clean and consolidate your lists before you migrate.
  • GetResponse Starter is a demo, not a home. $19/mo looks like the cheapest serious plan here, but it allows exactly one automation workflow and no webinars. One real welcome sequence plus one abandoned-cart flow already exceeds it. Budget Marketer ($59) or don't budget GetResponse at all — the $40/mo gap between Starter and Marketer is the real price of admission.
  • GetResponse prices are net — taxes land on top. The displayed prices are net amounts with local taxes applied at checkout, and the euro and dollar price lists are separate schedules, not currency conversions. And the 93.3% vs 89.4% deliverability gap comes from two different testers' audits — directionally useful, not a lab-grade head-to-head. Don't treat it as gospel at your list size.

Who should pick which

Pick GetResponse if: you run or plan to run webinars; you sell courses or digital products and want funnels, checkout, and email on one bill; or your automations are ecommerce-triggered (carts, purchases, behavior). Start on Marketer — Starter's single-workflow cap means you'll outgrow it in weeks.

Pick AWeber if: your job is email — newsletters, broadcasts, welcome sequences — and nothing else; you want a human on the phone when something breaks; your list is under 10,000 and Plus's pricing curve stays kind; or the Done For You $79 setup buys you a week of your life back. At $0 the free plan is the cheapest way to start either platform.

Switching between them

Migrating to GetResponse from AWeber is where the work lives: rebuild automations (GetResponse's webinar and funnel objects have no AWeber equivalent, so the port is really a redesign), consolidate duplicate lists first (multi-list duplicates will inflate your contact count and your bill), and budget Marketer — Starter's one-workflow cap won't hold a migrated automation library. Going GetResponse → AWeber, expect to lose webinars, funnels, and course content entirely — you'll be re-buying those from other vendors — so the move only makes sense if you were paying for the all-in-one and using only the email.

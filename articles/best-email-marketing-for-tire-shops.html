A tire shop does not sell tires the way a store sells shoes — it sells a calendar. Twice a year the entire customer base needs the same service in the same few weeks: the fall swap (late October to early November, the 7-degree-Celsius rule) and the spring swap-back. The US winter tire market alone is worth about $3.5 billion in 2025 and growing toward $5.6 billion by 2033 (6.2% CAGR) — and North America is 32% of the global winter tire market. Winter tires are the fastest-growing segment in the industry (4.12% CAGR 2026-2031) while all-season still holds 61.78% of the market — which means the buyer is confused and the shop that educates owns the sale. The honest catches

Your POS already does transactional — let it. The shop's point-of-sale and tire platform already handle invoices, appointment confirmations, and pickup notices. The email tool on this page is the marketing layer: the swap calendar, the hotel reminders, the upsell ladder, the education drip. Duplicating transactional mail in your marketing tool is how customers start ignoring both — and how you pay for two tools doing one job.

Don't blast the whole list on first snow. The first-snow rush blast feels like revenue, but sending it to everyone — including customers in Florida and the guy who swapped last week — trains the whole list to ignore you. Segment hard: the unconverted list gets the rush; the swapped get a "see you in spring"; the hotel clients get their own lane. The season is the business; the segments are the business's business.

The stored-tire program IS email. Shops that run tire hotels and don't remind anyone in spring leave the entire second rush on the table — the customer simply forgets, or goes somewhere closer. The hotel list is your owned audience; the reminder is the revenue. Automate it or lose it.

Climate-segment honestly. The swap calendar is a northern business. If your shop is in Arizona, the seasonal-swap engine doesn't exist — the rotation ladder and the inspection sequence are your engines instead. The tool rankings don't change; the campaigns do.

Capture at the work order, not after. The counter is the capture moment: "we'll email you when it's swap time" at checkout. The appointment book is the list — if you don't capture the email with the phone number, the October calendar has nobody to invite.

FAQ

When is the swap season? Late October to early November going into winter (the 7°C rule — summer compounds harden below it, even on dry roads), and late March to mid-April coming back out. The 2026 seasonal-tire guides agree: watch the temperature, not the calendar.

All-weather vs winter tires — what should I tell customers? The honest version: dedicated winter tires are still the best cold-weather choice; 3PMSF all-weather tires (41% of US winter-capable sales in 2025) are the convenience compromise. The education drip's job is to match the tire to the driver's winter — not to sell the most expensive set to everyone.

Should I email during the slow summer? Yes — but not the swap calendar. Summer is the rotation-reminder season, the tire-hotel renewal season, and the slow build of the September education drip list. The list goes quiet in summer; the machinery doesn't.

How do I collect emails at the shop? At the work order: "we'll email you when it's swap time" — the appointment book is the list. One good fall rush adds hundreds of names. The counter is the highest-traffic capture point in the business.

Email or text for the first-snow rush? Both, split by stakes: the rush blast and the appointment reminders belong in the pocket (the text), while the swap explainer, the education drip, and the tier ladder belong in the inbox (the email). Brevo's SMS lane exists for exactly this. Match the channel to the stakes.

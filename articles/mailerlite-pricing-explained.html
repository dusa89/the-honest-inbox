MailerLite quietly rebuilt its pricing in June 2026: the plans got new names (Comfort and Power), prices rose, and the free plan was cut to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month — the second cut to the free tier. Half the guides ranking on Google still quote the old names and the old free plan. Here's the current math, the send cap nobody charts, and the exact moment the free plan stops being free.

TL;DR
The free plan is a trial in everything but name: 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails a month, MailerLite branding on your emails, no direct support, and a hard wall at subscriber 251. Comfort starts at $12/mo (500 subs) and is the cheapest paid plan in the category — but it caps your monthly sends at 10× your subscriber count, which catches frequent senders by surprise. Power starts at $25/mo and removes the send cap entirely. Pick MailerLite if you send once or twice a week to a growing list; if you blast daily, run the send-cap math before you commit.

The three plans, decoded

First, the rename, because stale guides will confuse you. In June 2026 MailerLite renamed Growing Business → Comfort and Advanced → Power, and raised prices across both. Any guide quoting "Growing Business $10" or "Advanced $21" is pricing the old plans — those numbers no longer exist. The free plan stays "Free," but it shrank: 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails per month, down from the old 1,000 subscribers and 12,000 emails.

Two things stand out. Second, Comfort's price is cheap because the sends are capped. That cap is the whole story of this plan.

The send cap: the gotcha every "cheapest tool" ranking skips

Comfort caps your monthly sends at roughly 10× your subscriber count. At 1,000 subscribers you get 10,000 emails a month. The math most reviewers never do:

  • One newsletter a week: 1,000 subs × ~4.3 sends ≈ 4,300 emails. Comfort handles it easily.
  • Twice a week plus one promo: ~12–13 sends a month ≈ 13,000 emails. You're over the 10,000 cap.
  • Daily sender: ~30,000 emails. You need Power at $39 — which costs more than Kit Creator at $39? No: Kit Creator is $39/mo at 1,000 subs too, but Kit gives unlimited sends on paid plans. The price advantage evaporates the moment you're a heavy sender.

Automated emails count against the cap too — welcome sequences, abandoned-cart flows, every automated send. If your funnel is email-heavy, add your automation volume to your campaign volume before you pick Comfort. This is the single most expensive misunderstanding on MailerLite's pricing page: "cheapest" is only true for light-to-moderate senders.

The free plan: genuinely free, with four real limits

The free plan still exists, and at $0 it's real. But call it what it is — a 250-subscriber trial with MailerLite's logo on it:

  • 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails a month. Subscriber 251 moves you to $12/mo Comfort. That's the steepest subscriber-to-bill ratio change in the category — Kit lets you carry 10,000 subscribers free. If your list is anywhere near 250, plan for the jump before you cross it: export nothing, just know the $12 is coming.
  • MailerLite branding on your emails. Every free-plan newsletter carries their badge. Paid plans remove it.
  • No direct support. Free users get the help center and community only — no email or chat with the team. New accounts do get a 14-day premium trial with direct support access, so ask your hard questions in the first two weeks.
  • Three automations, limited extras. The free plan allows up to three automations — enough to learn the automation builder, not enough to run a real funnel. Pre-designed premium templates are also restricted on free.

One bright spot: the AI writing assistant (subject lines, newsletter content help) is included on the free plan, which is generous for a $0 tier.

Comfort vs Power: what the extra money buys

Comfort is the right plan for most small businesses: advanced automations, up to three landing pages, 24/7 email support, the Stripe integration for selling digital products, and Shopify/WooCommerce connections for stores.

Power roughly doubles the price at every band ($25 vs $12 at 500 subs; $39 vs $19 at 1,000) and buys: unlimited email sending (the real reason to upgrade), unlimited users (Comfort has 3), the AI writing assistant's full version, a custom HTML editor, Facebook integration, enhanced automations with multiple triggers, promotion pop-ups, a preference center, and live-chat support priority. The honest rule: upgrade to Power when your send volume outgrows Comfort's 10× cap, or when you have a team bigger than three — not before.

Three gotchas before you decide

  • The approval review. MailerLite is stricter than most tools about new accounts — you go through an approval review before you can send your first campaign. Plan for up to a day of waiting; don't schedule your launch email for the hour after signup.
  • SMS is a separate plan. Comfort and Power are email-only. If you want SMS on the same bill, MailerLite's transactional plan is separate — Brevo and Klaviyo bundle SMS into their marketing plans instead.
  • Old guides are everywhere. The June 2026 overhaul renamed the plans, raised prices, and cut the free plan. Any guide quoting "Growing Business," "Advanced," a $10 entry price, or 1,000 free subscribers is stale. Which plan should you actually pick?

Pick Free if: your list is under 250 and you're learning — sending a weekly newsletter, testing forms and landing pages. Treat it as a trial and know the $12 cliff is coming at subscriber 251.

Pick Comfort if: you're past 250 subscribers and send once or twice a week (campaigns + light automation under 10× your list size per month). This is the best-value paid plan in email marketing for moderate senders.

Pick Power if: you send daily, run heavy automation, have more than three team members, or need the custom HTML editor and Facebook integration. Don't buy it for prestige — buy it when Comfort's send cap or user limit is the thing blocking you.

ActiveCampaign is the one tool in this category nobody accuses of being thin — it has the deepest automation branching, the best lead scoring, and a real CRM for under $200 a month. The problem is the price of admission: you buy it for the automation, and the automation is rationed on the entry tier. Below: the six exits matched to your actual reason for leaving — and the honest case that if automations are what drive your revenue, the Plus tier you keep eyeing is cheaper than pretending you're a Starter user.

Leaving because you're paying for automation you're not using? Leaving because you need the automation but not the price? Staying on ActiveCampaign is about how much of the automation depth you actually use — not what you paid to get in.

Why users actually leave ActiveCampaign

Six real reasons, ranked by how often they come up — not the invented grievances that pad out other "alternatives" listicles.

1. The automation-cap wall on Starter. The number-one driver, and it's structural. ActiveCampaign Starter caps you at 5 automation actions — automations, not emails — and you bought the tool for automations. The moment your second welcome sequence, your cart recovery, and your lead scoring start feeling real, the entry tier is done. The actual automation product is Plus: $59/mo at 1,000 contacts, $179 at 5,000, $239 at 10,000. The honest price of "ActiveCampaign for automations" is 3× the headline $19/mo Starter — and that gap is the reason most people leave.

2. The bill climbs faster than anywhere else. Even setting the Plus question aside, the escalation is steep: Starter runs $19/mo at 1,000 contacts and $99 at 5,000 — a 5× multiple for a 5× list, with nothing added. MailerLite Comfort is $19 at 1,000 and $49 at 5,000; GetResponse Starter is $19 and $54. ActiveCampaign's per-contact curve is the priciest in its class, so every list-growth milestone re-opens the "is this still worth it" question.

3. The 10,000-contact ceiling. Starter has a hard cap: 10,000 contacts, and then you're moved to Plus whether your automations need it or not. It's a forcing function disguised as a tier limit — businesses that outgrow the contact ceiling but not the feature ceiling pay the Plus price for the privilege of growing.

4. The complexity tax. The steepest learning curve in the category. A CRM, sales pipelines, deal stages, lead scoring, predictive content — the depth is real, and so is the weekly cost of a dashboard built for a marketing-ops team when you're a two-person business. Users consistently report that the newsletters they send in 10 minutes in MailerLite take an hour to set up here. Complexity isn't a flaw if you use the depth; it's a tax if you don't.

5. No free plan to land on. ActiveCampaign has a 14-day trial and nothing behind it — no free tier for list-building from zero. If you're leaving and want to validate a new tool slowly, every exit on this page except ActiveCampaign itself gives you a free runway (Kit's covers 10,000 subscribers; AWeber's covers 500). The exit is softer when the landing pad costs nothing.

6. Support and setup friction. The recurring reviewer pattern: onboarding is heavy, deliverability setup (SPF, DKIM, domain verification) assumes a technical comfort level, and the support queue reflects a tool built for scale users. Solo senders describe the first month as configuration, not marketing. If reason #4 is why you're reading, this is its shadow.

The 6 alternatives, matched to your reason for leaving

1. MailerLite — if you're paying for automation you're not using

The direct swap for anyone leaving over reason #1 or #4. Comfort runs $19/mo at 1,000 contacts and $49 at 5,000 — the same entry price as Starter, half the bill at 5,000 — with real automation from the $12 entry and an interface that builds a welcome sequence in an afternoon instead of a weekend. What you gain: the simplest builder in the category and honest, shallow bills as your list grows. What you give up: lead scoring, CRM depth, and the 5-action ceiling is replaced by a shallower ceiling of your own. If your automations are a welcome sequence and a newsletter, you were never an ActiveCampaign user — you were a MailerLite user paying double.

2. GetResponse — if you need the automation but not the price

For the Plus-or-bust user who actually uses the depth but resents the curve. Marketer is the only exit that replaces real automation: webinar modules, funnels, a course builder, and unlimited emails — $59/mo at 1,000 contacts (dollar-for-dollar with Plus) and $114 at 5,000 ($65/mo cheaper than Plus's $179). The trade: lead scoring and the CRM pipeline aren't GetResponse's game, and it's the second-busiest dashboard on this page. Only take this exit if the automation modules are what you're buying. 3. Kit — if you're a creator who bought audience tooling by accident

For the ActiveCampaign user who is actually a creator — courses, paid newsletters, launches. Kit's free tier covers 10,000 subscribers (ActiveCampaign charges you from day one), Creator at $39/mo adds commerce, the Creator Network, and automations built around selling rather than pipeline stages. The trade: Kit's automation is built for selling products, not scoring leads — if your funnel needs conditional branching at ActiveCampaign depth, you'll feel the loss. 4. AWeber — if you want the anti-dashboard

The least-fashionable exit, and the right one for a specific reader: the small business done with marketing-ops software. Lite runs $25/mo at 1,000 contacts, the free tier covers 500 subscribers, and there is no CRM, no pipeline, no scoring to get lost in — the anti-ActiveCampaign. The trade: the automation is a generation behind even ActiveCampaign Starter's, and AWeber bills unsubscribed contacts (flagged in our AWeber vs GetResponse head-to-head). For a newsletter and a welcome sequence, it's honest money.

5. Brevo — if your list is big, quiet, and automated

The send-based exit. Brevo bills emails instead of contacts, so a large list that sends occasionally can run Standard at bands contact-priced tools can't touch — $97/mo for 50,000 emails a month against a big stored list, with real automation included from Standard up. The trade: frequent senders get punished by the meter (see our Brevo alternatives guide for the send-profile math), and the interface is the category's busiest. Only take this exit if your sends-per-subscriber-per-month is under 1 — otherwise MailerLite is the cheaper door.

6. Flodesk — if your send volume fits the flat rate

The design-first exit: a flat rate (from $54/mo) with the best-in-class email templates in the category. Our Flodesk vs MailerLite guide covers the template trap — gorgeous emails, thin automation. This exit fits the ActiveCampaign user whose "automation" was really one drip campaign with beautiful design: boutiques, studios, photographers. If your workflows branch on lead scores, Flodesk will feel like emailing from a gallery — pretty, and empty.

The honest case for staying: the ceiling is real

Here's what the exit guides won't say: below $200/mo, nothing on this page touches Plus's automation depth. Multi-branch conditional workflows, contact and lead scoring, CRM deal pipelines, sales automation that triggers on pipeline movement — GetResponse Marketer is the closest and it's still a module bundle, not a scoring engine. If those features are the reason your revenue comes in, the $59/mo at 1,000 contacts (or $179 at 5,000) is not overpriced — it's the cheapest way to buy that capability in the category.

The stay math, by profile. Starter users with 3+ automations planned: you're already a Plus user on a Starter bill — upgrade honestly or accept the downgrade; MailerLite is the cheapest honest door out. Scoring and pipeline users: stop shopping — no exit replaces the depth, and the 6-12 month cost of rebuilding equivalent workflows elsewhere exceeds the price gap. Newsletter-only users on Starter: you were overpaying from month one — MailerLite or AWeber is your exit, and the sooner the better. Approaching the 10,000-contact ceiling: decide before the forced upgrade does it for you. And one warning before you commit long-term: ActiveCampaign's annual discount varies by tier, so price the annual commitment against the Plus tier you might land on mid-year — the Starter discount means little if the ceiling moves you.

Migration notes

Contacts export clean from ActiveCampaign, including tags — but nothing else does. Automations are the trap: every workflow, conditional branch, and scoring rule has to be rebuilt by hand in the new tool's builder, and Plus users with 10+ automations should budget a full weekend, not an afternoon. Suppression lists and engagement history don't transfer. If deliverability is part of why you're leaving, don't skip the re-warm: a domain moved off ActiveCampaign's infrastructure to a new platform's shared IPs can land worse before it lands better. And cut over with a gap, not a cliff: run both tools for one send cycle so a failed import doesn't cost you a campaign.

GetResponse sells two different products under one brand: a $19/month email sender (Starter) and a $59–$69/month marketing platform (Marketer and Creator). The $40 jump between them is the steepest single-tier jump in email marketing, and it's where most buyers get it wrong — they either overpay for automation they'll never build, or they buy Starter and hit its one-workflow wall in month two. Here's the full price ladder, what each tier is actually for, and the honest rule for picking one.

TL;DR
Starter ($19/mo at 1,000 contacts) is a genuinely good email sender — unlimited emails, landing pages, one automation workflow. It's all you need if you send newsletters and simple welcome emails. Marketer ($59/mo) is where the real product lives: unlimited automation workflows, webinars, sales funnels, contact scoring. Buy it when automation is your engine, not your experiment. Creator ($69/mo) is only $10 above Marketer and exists for one audience — people who sell courses or paid newsletters. If that isn't you, Creator is $10 a month of nothing.

GetResponse gets expensive at scale in a way its $19 entry price hides completely. Compare that with MailerLite's Power tier ($99/mo at 8,000 contacts) before you grow into a bill you didn't plan for.

The $19-to-$59 jump: the real decision

Going from Starter to Marketer triples your bill. What the $40 buys:

  • Unlimited automation workflows (Starter gets exactly one). This is the actual reason to upgrade — one welcome series is all Starter can build; Marketer can run your welcome series, abandoned-cart flow, win-back campaign, and post-purchase sequence at once.
  • Webinars built in. Marketer includes webinars with around 100 attendee slots; Creator raises the cap (guides report anywhere from 100 to 300 depending on tier and date — check the current pricing page for the exact number). Running webinars on Zoom costs $15–$80/mo separately, so this is the fastest way to justify the jump if you teach live.
  • Sales funnels and abandoned-cart recovery. Full funnel pages with promo codes and revenue reporting — the e-commerce tier, essentially.
  • Contact scoring and advanced segmentation. Score leads on engagement, segment on behavior — genuinely useful past a few thousand contacts.
  • 5 users (Starter has 3) and 24/7 support across all paid tiers.

The honest rule: if you'd use webinars or funnels anyway, Marketer is priced fairly — you're replacing a separate webinar tool. If you just send newsletters, Marketer is $40/month of features collecting dust, and Starter is the whole product for you.

Creator: the $10 tier that only matters if you sell courses

Creator costs $10 more than Marketer at the entry band and adds exactly one thing that matters: the AI course creator (up to 500 students, 0% transaction fees per a September 2026 pricing guide), plus premium/paid newsletter subscriptions and a bigger website builder allowance (5 active sites). There is no other reason to pick it.

This makes Creator the easiest tier to evaluate in email marketing: do you sell courses or paid newsletters through GetResponse? Then it's worth the $10. Everyone else should stop at Marketer. Marketer is, in a sense, the awkward tier for creators — close enough to Creator in price that picking Marketer for a course business is a false economy.

The free plan: a trial with a ceiling you'll hit fast

The free plan carries 500 contacts and 2,500 emails a month, plus landing pages and signup forms — enough to learn the editor and capture your first subscribers. But 500 contacts fills up quickly, the email cap means roughly 5 sends a month to a full list, and the automation on free is basic. It's genuinely useful for validating the tool; it is not a free-tier you can run a business on. (MailerLite's free plan, for comparison, is smaller at 250 contacts — we priced both.)

One better deal: GetResponse offers a 30-day free trial of the paid features with no credit card required. Take the trial instead of the free plan if you're evaluating — you get the actual product, and 30 days is enough to know.

Three gotchas before you decide

  • The scale penalty. GetResponse's ladder climbs hard past 10,000 contacts — $179/mo Marketer at 10k, $349/mo at 25k. If your list will grow fast, price your future list size, not your current one. Some competitors stay flatter at scale.
  • Annual billing saves ~18%, with a catch reported by one guide: annual plans may be non-refundable if cancelled mid-term. Take the 30-day trial first, commit monthly for a quarter, and only then switch to annual if you're staying.
  • Webinar attendee caps move. Recent guides disagree on Creator's webinar cap (100 vs 300 attendees), which suggests GetResponse adjusts this feature without fanfare. If webinars are your reason for buying Marketer or Creator, confirm the current cap on the pricing page before you pay.

Which tier should you actually pick?

Pick Free if: you're learning or validating — under 500 contacts, figuring out forms and the editor. Take the 30-day paid trial instead if you're actually evaluating.

Pick Starter if: you send newsletters and one welcome sequence, period. Blogs, creators, and small lists that don't need e-commerce or webinars. This is the best value of the four paid tiers for pure email.

Pick Marketer if: automation is your engine — e-commerce (abandoned carts, funnels), lead scoring, or regular webinars that would otherwise need a separate Zoom subscription. The $59 is fair only if you use the bundle.

Pick Creator if: you sell courses or paid newsletters and want them on the same bill as your email — up to 500 students, no transaction fees. Anyone else is donating $10 a month.

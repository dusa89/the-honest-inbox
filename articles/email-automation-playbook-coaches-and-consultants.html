The 4 baseline sequences (build these first)

These are the sequences that do the actual work — the practice itself. Build them before anything clever:

1. Lead-magnet welcome → the discovery-call sequence (5 emails, 7 days)

Trigger: new subscriber from a lead magnet — checklist, mini-audit, free chapter. Sequence: five emails over 7 days: immediate delivery (one link, no pitch — pitching in email 1 trains people to ignore you), day 1 the diagnosis ("the mistake I see most [your niche] make" — plain-text, personal tone; the email that gets replies gets calls), day 3 the proof (one client story with a before and an after, not a testimonial collage), day 5 the objection handler (name the #1 reason people don't book before they think it), day 7 the direct invite ("I open 5 call slots a week" — scarcity only works if it's true; actually limit the slots). This sequence is the funnel; everything else in the playbook feeds it or monetizes it.

2. The no-book follow-up (3 emails, 7 days)

Trigger: clicked the booking link but didn't book, or visited the pricing page without booking. Sequence: three emails over 7 days: the clarification ("here's exactly what happens on the call — it's not a pitch"), the proof-with-specifics (one result, real numbers, from someone like them), and the soft close ("the slots are still open this week; the booking link is below"). This is where ActiveCampaign earns its name: "if they visited the pricing page but didn't book, send the objection-handler email" is exactly the behavioral trigger its Starter plan is built for. Coaches without behavioral automation run this as a manual timed sequence to the clicker segment — it converts fewer, but it converts.

3. Post-call follow-up (3 emails, 10 days)

Trigger: discovery call completed, no proposal accepted within 48 hours. Sequence: the same-day recap ("what we covered, the one thing to do this week"), day 3 the proposal nudge with a reply hook ("hit reply with your #1 question about the proposal"), day 10 the honest close ("should I close your file?" — the breakup email gets the most replies in any coach's funnel). This sequence dies silently if your email tool can't stop the pitch flow automatically when a prospect becomes a client — ActiveCampaign and Kit handle the prospect-to-client tag flip natively; on MailerLite's Comfort plan, check that your specific trigger is supported before you assume it.

4. Past-client and referral nurture (quarterly)

Trigger: past-client tag at program end, plus your referral-source list. Sequence: quarterly check-ins to past clients — not "I have a new offer" spam, an actual check-in: the result they got, what's changed since, the one new insight since their program ended. Every fourth email carries the referral ask ("who do you know who needs what you got?"). Past clients are the highest-converting audience for the next offer (our estimate — past-buyer conversion is consistently the highest segment in every funnel we've studied), and they're the only audience a coach can ever pitch without a sequence: the trust is already bought. (This is the operating logic of the sequence, not a study stat.)

The 3 advanced automations

5. The course cart sequence (6 emails, 5 days)

Trigger: cart open date, sent to prospects and past clients — never to current students. Sequence: day 0 the cart-open email (what's inside, who it's for, the price, the link — the long one; this email does the selling), day 1 the curriculum walkthrough (kill the "will this work for me" doubt, module by module), day 2 student stories (two or three specific results), day 3 the FAQ (time commitment, skill level, refunds, "what if I'm behind" — every question you preempt is a sale saved), day 4 morning the closing-tonight reminder, day 4 evening the last call (short; deadline, link, done — two emails on close day consistently outperform one, a standard launch observation, not a guarantee). The honest tool question: the launch week adds ~5 full-list sends to your month. On a send-capped plan, that's the whole month's volume in five days — see the send math below.

6. The webinar and replay machine

Trigger: registration, show-up, or no-show to a live masterclass. Sequence: registration confirmation, 24-hour and 1-hour reminders, the replay email to registrants who didn't attend (24 hours only — scarcity, again, only if true), and the replay-viewer sequence: "watched 50%+, didn't book" gets the objection-handler email; "watched under 20%" gets the highlight-reel email with the one key segment. This automation decides the tool question for webinar-led coaches: GetResponse Marketer ($59/mo at 1k) bundles webinars to 100 attendees; Creator ($69/mo) goes to 300 attendees plus course hosting. Running webinars on a separate subscription (~$15/mo) plus a cheap email tool is a three-bill stack — price the all-in-one against your current stack honestly.

7. Behavioral triggers (the sales-rep automation)

Trigger: page views, video watch percentage, email engagement scored as "hot." Action: pricing-page visit without booking fires the objection-handler email; 50%+ webinar watch without a booking fires the booking nudge; three unopened emails in a row fires the re-engagement check-in. This is the only automation in the playbook that requires behavior-based logic — and it's the one that prices the tier: on ActiveCampaign it means Plus ($59/mo at 1k) if you want lead scoring with it; Starter ($19) runs basic page-visit triggers within its 5-action ration. On Kit (Creator) and MailerLite (Comfort), the simpler versions of these triggers are included — verify your exact trigger exists on the plan before you build the funnel around it.

The send math: does a launch month fit the cap?

Worked example — a coach with 1,000 contacts on MailerLite Comfort ($19/mo, send cap roughly 10× the list = ~10,000 emails/mo): a weekly newsletter (4 broadcasts) = 4,000 emails. The 5-email call sequence to ~50 new leads a month = ~250 emails. Quarterly past-client nurture = ~1,000 per quarter. Post-call and no-book sequences = a few hundred. Quiet months: roughly 5,000–6,000 emails — comfortably under the cap. Launch month: the 6-email cart sequence to the full list = ~6,000 emails in five days, on top of the newsletter — now you're at 10,000–11,000, at or over the cap. The cap only bites during launches, which is exactly when you can't afford bounces. Your volume will differ; add your launch sends plus your drip sends before you pick the tier. Price the launch, not the average.

The calendar question: don't buy what you already pay for

Before you build a single automation in an email tool, name the three pieces of your funnel — and which tool owns each. The calendar (Calendly or similar) actually takes the booking; no email tool replaces it. The email tool drives traffic to the calendar — freebie opt-in, nurture sequence, "book your call" CTA, post-call follow-up. The payment or course platform takes the money and hosts the course — unless you pick a tool that bundles it (GetResponse Creator is the bundled pick; Kit Commerce is the per-sale pick at 3.5% + 30¢). The honest division of labor: the booking page fires the automation (new booking → tag in the email tool → stop the pitch sequence; no-show → the no-book follow-up), and the calendar's own reminder emails handle the show-up rate — don't rebuild reminders in your email tool and pay for sends twice. A $297 course selling 10× a month costs ~$107 in Kit Commerce fees (our calculation: 3.5% of $297 = $10.40 + $0.30 per sale) — at that volume, compare honestly against a flat-fee course platform plus the email tool you already like.

The 3 mistakes that kill coach funnels

  • Building on the wrong tier. Kit's free plan covers your whole list and none of your automations; GetResponse Starter gives you one workflow for a seven-automation playbook; Brevo's free and Starter tiers have no automation at all. Every sequence built on a tier that can't run it is a rebuild. Pick the tier from the table above, then build.
  • Pitching the whole list. Cart emails to current students, launch sequences to clients mid-program, discount offers to people who paid full price last month. The minimum viable segmentation is three tags — prospects, clients, past clients — and the pitch sequence must stop automatically when a prospect becomes a client. ActiveCampaign and Kit do the tag flip natively; on MailerLite, verify your trigger first.
  • Launching on a send cap without doing the math. MailerLite's 10× cap is invisible during newsletter months and brutal during a 5-day cart launch — the cap bites exactly when the revenue is on the table. Work the launch-month math from the send section above before the cart opens, not after the bounce.

Compliance summary, not legal advice: consent to add, unsubscribe on every send, and honest scarcity (the 5-slot limit, the replay deadline) must be true or they're deceptive claims. When in doubt, ask counsel.

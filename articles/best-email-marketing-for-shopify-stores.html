What a Shopify email integration has to do (the bar)

Not every "Shopify integration" is an integration. The ones that matter do all five:

  • Customer sync: buyers flow into the list automatically, with consent status attached (opted-in vs checkout-only — CAN-SPAM and GDPR both care).
  • Order + product sync: products, prices, images, and purchase history available inside the email builder — product blocks, not screenshots.
  • Abandoned-cart triggers: cart-abandoned events fire automations with the actual cart contents, on a timer (1 hour / 24 hours / 48 hours is the standard playbook).
  • Behavioral segmentation: first-time vs repeat buyer, category interests, order value — without exporting CSVs.
  • Revenue attribution: which email caused which order. Without this you're flying blind on the only metric that matters.

Anything short of this is a Zapier-class bridge: new-customer → add-to-list. Fine for a newsletter signup, useless for cart recovery. The honest question for any tool: does it see my carts?

The four integrations, graded

MailerLite — the value pick (sees your carts, $19)

MailerLite's Shopify connection is a first-party app in the Shopify App Store ("MailerLite Email Automation," developed by MailerLite). Install, authorize the MailerLite account, choose the subscriber group for the sync, and decide whether to sync all customers or only opted-in ones. Products, customers, and purchase data sync from the store, and the app surfaces pop-ups on the storefront with no coding. What the integration gives you:

  • Customer + product + purchase-data sync, with sync settings per group
  • Abandoned-checkout automation template, plus post-purchase follow-ups, purchase-milestone automations (e.g. reward every 3rd order), win-back flows, and welcome series
  • E-commerce blocks in the drag-and-drop editor: product galleries, individual product features, customer reviews, countdown timers
  • Auto-created smart segments from store data: first-time vs repeat customers, high-value shoppers, product-category interests

The honest catch: the automation engine behind this runs on a paid plan — free caps automations at 3, so Comfort ($19/mo at 1,000 contacts) is the real starting point, not $0. Behavioral multi-trigger automations (the "viewed 3 products, no reply" kind) need Power. MailerLite's e-commerce depth is wide, not predictive — no churn-risk scores, no AI send-time optimization at this tier. For stores that send weekly promos plus the standard flows, it's the cheapest way to get all five integration bars cleared.

GetResponse — the depth pick (Marketer tier, revenue math)

GetResponse's native Shopify integration syncs products, customers, orders, and abandoned carts, and the automation builder ships e-commerce-specific triggers: cart-abandoned, product recommendations (AI-generated from purchase/browse history), unique promo-code generation, and revenue tracking that attributes orders to campaigns. Also in the bundle: web push notifications and landing pages — the only tool here where the cart-recovery email and the on-site push are the same account.

  • Native sync of products, customers, orders, abandoned carts (plus WooCommerce, Magento, BigCommerce, PrestaShop connectors)
  • Prebuilt automation templates: cart-abandoned sequences (1h/24h/48h playbook), post-purchase flows, win-backs
  • AI product recommendations and promo-code generation inside emails
  • Revenue tracking per campaign — the attribution bar that free tools skip

The honest catch: the e-commerce automation builder lives on Marketer ($59/mo), not Starter ($19 — exactly one workflow). That's 3x MailerLite Comfort at 1,000 contacts. The math only clears if the store actually uses the extra depth: AI recommendations, web push, and full funnel analysis. A store that needs welcome + abandoned cart + weekly promos is paying $40/mo extra for features it won't open — that's the stay-or-go question, and most small stores should stay on MailerLite.

Brevo — the messaging pick (email + SMS + WhatsApp, send-based pricing)

Brevo's official Shopify route is the Brevo (PushOwl) app in the Shopify App Store: install, log in, and it syncs customer and order history on first connection, then keeps syncing. Brevo is the only tool on this bench with native SMS and WhatsApp on the same account — for stores where the abandoned cart goes to text, that's a real differentiator, and its pricing bills emails sent rather than contacts stored. A store with 10,000 contacts that emails weekly can sit far below a contact-priced plan's bill — Brevo's pricing model rewards big quiet lists and punishes frequent heavy senders.

The honest catch: free = 300 emails/day and no automation — a Shopify store sending cart-recovery emails off the free tier is a non-starter; budget a paid tier from day one. The app ecosystem around Brevo's Shopify connector is thinner than MailerLite's: third-party bridge apps (SyncBre and similar) carry poor App Store reviews (2.6–2.9 stars on the listings we checked October 2, 2026), so stick to the official Brevo (PushOwl) app and Brevo's own automation builder. The integration is narrower than GetResponse's — no AI product recommendations, simpler promo mechanics — but the SMS/WhatsApp native channel and the send-based bill are why some stores pick it.

Kit — the creator's storefront (thin commerce, wins elsewhere)

Kit's App Store includes a native Shopify app: install from the Shopify App Store (or Kit's own App Store), authenticate with your API key, and customers and orders flow in with tags. Forms and landing pages embed on the storefront. The commerce wiring is real — but Kit is a creator platform that happens to connect to Shopify, not a commerce platform. Abandoned-cart automations exist but the cart-depth (product blocks, recommendations, revenue attribution) is thinner than the three above.

The honest catch: Kit's price math only wins for a specific store: a creator selling digital products through Kit Commerce where the Shopify store is secondary, or a newsletter-first brand with a merch arm. Kit's free plan covers 10,000 subscribers but excludes the automation builder entirely — cart recovery needs Creator ($39/mo). A pure physical-goods store on Kit pays creator prices for thinner commerce wiring. If the store is the business, MailerLite beats it on every commerce bar at half the price.

The 3 setup mistakes that kill Shopify integrations

  • Syncing all customers without consent separation. Checkout emails are not marketing opt-ins. Every integration above lets you sync only opted-in customers to marketing groups — use it. Checkout-only customers belong in a separate transactional segment, and transactional email has its own rules (our deliverability guide covers why).
  • Running cart recovery before authenticating the sending domain. SPF/DKIM set up for your website do nothing for the email tool — authenticate the domain inside the tool and test-send first. Cart emails that land in spam are worse than no cart emails; the customer never saw the offer and you trained them to ignore you. Setup walkthrough per tool: SPF/DKIM/DMARC guide.
  • Importing a dead list on day one. Syncing 8,000 stale customers from two years of orders will tank your deliverability in a week. Warm the domain, send to engaged buyers first, and prune — the warm-up guide is the order of operations.

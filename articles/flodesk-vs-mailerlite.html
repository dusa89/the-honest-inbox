The template trap: five catches behind the beautiful design

This is the part Flodesk's affiliate army won't tell you, because most of them are creators who genuinely love it and never stress-tested the limits. Five things to know before your free trial ends:

1. Lite gives you ONE automation workflow

Flodesk Lite — the $25/mo plan — includes a single workflow automation (currently positioned as a "limited-time bonus," which is an honest flag that it could change). One welcome sequence. That's it. Your abandoned-cart flow, your post-purchase sequence, your lead-magnet delivery series — those all need Pro. Anyone running two automations simultaneously is on Pro, which puts Flodesk's real automation price at $28/mo at 1k — not $25.

2. Automation depth is shallow by design

Flodesk's workflow builder is built for "send this series of emails with time delays," which covers welcome sequences and simple nurture tracks beautifully. What it doesn't do well: conditional branching, behavioral triggers ("if they clicked, do X"), lead scoring, or purchase-event logic. MailerLite's automation builder has conditions, splits, and triggers Flodesk simply doesn't offer. If your marketing is one newsletter plus one welcome drip, Flodesk is plenty. If it's behavioral, Flodesk will frustrate you by month three.

3. The free plan doesn't send email

Flodesk's free plan gives you forms, landing pages, templates, and segmentation — everything to build your list. It sends exactly zero emails. The first time you want to email anyone, you're on a paid plan. MailerLite's free plan (250 subs, 2,500 sends) actually does the core job. Every comparison that puts both free plans side-by-side without saying this is misleading you.

4. The checkout features live on the expensive plans

Selling digital products from Flodesk needs Pro ($28/mo) for a single checkout or Everything ($54/mo) for unlimited checkouts, subscriptions, and abandoned-cart automations. Payments process through Stripe at Stripe's standard rates with no extra Flodesk fee — which is fair — but the plan gate means "Flodesk is cheap" stops being true the moment you sell things.

5. Lite caps out at 25,000 subscribers

Flodesk Lite has a hard ceiling of 25,000 total subscribers. Growing past it forces you onto Pro regardless of which features you use. MailerLite has no such feature-plan cliff — you just move up its contact bands. (Lite's cap also quietly makes it a starter plan, not a long-term home.)

MailerLite's honest weaknesses (it isn't flawless either)

  • The design gap is real. MailerLite's templates are fine; Flodesk's are genuinely in another league. If your brand is the aesthetic — photography, fashion, lifestyle, food blogging — this is not a trivial difference. Your audience judges the sender by the pixels.
  • The 10× send cap. Comfort lets you send 10× your subscriber count per month (1,000 subs = 10,000 emails). A weekly newsletter to 1k contacts uses ~4,300 — fine. Add a daily RSS feed, welcome sequences, and a second weekly send and you'll feel it.
  • Support is chat/email only. Flodesk also lacks phone support, so this is a tie — but neither tool is the "call someone" category.
  • The 2026 pricing overhaul. MailerLite renamed all its plans and raised prices in 2026, and cut free to 250 subscribers. It's still the value pick, but it's less of a steal than its reputation suggests — price your 12-month list before committing.

Who each is really for

Pick Flodesk if…

  • You are a creator, blogger, or visual brand whose email is primarily a beautiful broadcast — newsletters, announcements, launches.
  • One welcome sequence covers your automation needs and you're on Pro for unlimited workflows anyway.
  • Design quality directly affects your conversion (aesthetic audiences notice, and Flodesk templates convert on looks).
  • You keep your list clean — Flodesk only bills active subscribers, so hygiene-conscious senders blunt the tier curve.

Pick MailerLite if…

  • You need multiple automations with real logic — behavioral triggers, conditional paths, segmentation-driven sequences.
  • You want the cheapest credible option at every list size: $19/mo at 1k beats Flodesk's $25, and the gap includes better automation.
  • You want a free plan that actually sends email (250 subs / 2,500 sends covers early-stage lists).
  • You're allergic to feature-gated plans — MailerLite's Comfort includes automations, landing pages, and support without the Lite/Pro/Everything ladder.

The bottom line

The trap works like this: you see the templates, you sign up, you build one gorgeous welcome sequence — and then you need a second automation, or you want to sell a digital product, or your list hits 5,000 and you're on $59/mo Pro with shallower automation than MailerLite's $49 Comfort. Flodesk is a design-first tool priced like an automation tool.

The honest rule: if beautiful broadcasts are your marketing — creators, visual brands, aesthetic-first businesses — Flodesk Pro is worth it and the templates earn their keep. For everyone else — businesses running behavioral automations, segmentation, and sequences — MailerLite is cheaper, deeper, and honest about what the cheap plan includes. Don't pay a design premium for a marketing system you don't run.

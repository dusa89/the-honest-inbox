Free email plans are the best deal in software and the most misleading. The best of them — Sender, Brevo, Kit — genuinely run a small business at $0. But free tiers are shrinking every year: Mailchimp cut its free plan twice in 18 months, MailerLite halved its subscriber cap in June 2026, and Moosend killed its free plan outright. The honest move isn't finding the biggest free number. It's reading the wall — the exact moment free stops and the bill starts — before you build your list on top of it. Here are the seven real free tiers, what each actually gives you, and where each one ends.

TL;DR
Sender has the most complete free tier in email marketing — 2,500 subscribers, 15,000 emails a month, and full automation with behavioral triggers at $0. Kit's free Newsletter plan holds the most subscribers (10,000) with unlimited sends, but you get one automation and a paid Creator jump when you outgrow it. Brevo stores up to 100,000 contacts and sends 300 emails a day — the best free tier if your list is big but your send schedule is flexible. MailerLite's free plan (250 subscribers after the June 2026 cut) and Mailchimp's (250 contacts, 500 emails a month after the February 2026 cut) are trials wearing a free plan's clothes. Pick by the wall you'll hit, not the headline number.

When free stops: the wall behind each plan

This is the section that matters. Every free tier below is fine on day one. The question is what happens on the day you outgrow it — because that's the day your list, your automations, and your momentum are all hostages to the upgrade price.

Sender — the wall is 2,500 subscribers

Sender's free tier is the outlier: full marketing automation (welcome series, abandoned cart, post-purchase flows), custom event triggers that fire from actions on your own website, segmentation, landing pages, popups, and even transactional email — plus 24/7 support — at $0. The wall is clean and honest: at 2,501 subscribers you move to Standard, which starts around $10/month. That's the cheapest first paid tier in this whole comparison. The honest warning: Sender's template library is thinner than the big brands' and its integration catalog is smaller, so check that your stack connects (Zapier covers most gaps) before you build 2,500 subscribers deep.

Kit — the wall is automation, then 10,001 subscribers

Kit's free Newsletter plan is the biggest subscriber allowance in email marketing: 10,000 subscribers, unlimited broadcasts, unlimited landing pages and forms, tagging, segmentation, and even digital product sales. The catch is what you can't build: one basic automation and one sequence. If your business needs a welcome series, a nurture sequence, and an abandoned-cart flow running at once, free Kit gives you one of the three. Then the second wall: at 10,001 subscribers you land on Creator at roughly $29–$39/month at the 1,000-subscriber billing band (guides disagree on the exact entry figure — run the pricing slider). Creators who monetize through Kit's commerce features may never care; everyone else should read Kit's full pricing curve before deciding.

Brevo — the wall is your send schedule

Brevo's free tier is built differently: it barely limits contacts (up to 100,000 stored) and limits sends instead — 300 emails a day, roughly 9,000 a month. A list of 8,000 subscribers getting one monthly newsletter fits. A list of 2,000 getting weekly campaigns doesn't (2,000 × 4 sends = 8,000, and one extra campaign breaks the cap). Brevo also gives you SMS and MMS channels on free, which nobody else here does. The first paid tier, Starter, starts around $17/month for 10,000 monthly sends — and Brevo's paid pricing scales on sends, not contacts, so a huge dormant list costs you nothing extra. One honesty flag: comparison guides disagree on how much automation is genuinely included on the free tier (one September 2026 roundup says basic automation is included, another says workflows need the ~$18 Standard tier). Confirm on the pricing page before you rely on it.

Beehiiv — the wall is automation at $49/month

Beehiiv's free tier is purpose-built for newsletters: 2,500 subscribers, unlimited sends, a clean writing experience. It is not built for marketing automation — free gets you a basic welcome email, and real automations live on the Scale plan at $49/month. If you're a writer building an audience, that's a fair deal (unlimited sends is genuinely generous). If you're a business that needs abandoned carts and win-back flows, Beehiiv's free tier is the wrong on-ramp and its paid jump is the steepest here.

MailerLite — the wall shrank in 2026

MailerLite used to have one of the great free tiers: 1,000 subscribers, 12,000 emails a month. Then September 2025 halved it to 500, and June 2026 halved it again to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with 3 automations, 3 forms, 1 landing page, and MailerLite branding. What remains is still full-featured — real automation, A/B testing, all three editors — but 250 subscribers is a few months of growth for anyone trying. The paid jump is gentle: Comfort starts at $12/month. The full story, including the send cap on Comfort that "cheapest tool" rankings skip, is in MailerLite pricing explained.

Mailchimp — the wall is 500 emails a month

Mailchimp's free tier has been cut three times: from 2,000 contacts and 10,000 emails in 2023, to 500 contacts, to — on February 17, 2026 — 250 contacts, 500 emails a month, and a 250-per-day sending cap, with automation removed from the free tier entirely in mid-2025. Do the math on that: 500 emails a month to a full 250-contact list is two sends. Two. If your list grows past 250, the first paid tier is the steepest jump in this comparison relative to what you were getting free. Mailchimp's free tier today is a way to test the editor, not a way to run email. (The five cheaper alternatives, priced out.)

GetResponse — the wall is 500 contacts

GetResponse's free plan (500 contacts, 2,500 emails a month) is honest about being a trial: enough to learn the editor and capture your first subscribers, not enough to run on. The better deal is the 30-day free trial of the paid features with no credit card required — you get the actual product for a month, which is enough to evaluate it properly. The full tier breakdown, including the $19-to-$59 jump that decides everything, is in GetResponse pricing explained.

The 2026 pattern: free tiers are shrinking

This isn't a coincidence or a conspiracy — it's the business model. Free tiers are customer acquisition, and vendors tune them as acquisition costs change. In the last 18 months: Mailchimp cut free twice (automation removed mid-2025, then the February 2026 contact cut). MailerLite cut free twice (September 2025, June 2026). Moosend removed its permanent free plan entirely in 2026 — it's a 30-day trial now, so any "best free tools" list that still ranks Moosend is working from old data. The tools holding their free tiers steady — Sender, Brevo, Kit, Beehiiv — are the ones competing on free as a strategy, not as an accident. That's a signal worth more than any feature table: a vendor competing on free is less likely to cut it next quarter.

The honest rules for picking a free tier

  • Price your 12-month list, not your current one. If you'll have 3,000 subscribers in a year, the only number that matters is each tool's price at 3,000 — not the free headline. The wall is the plan.
  • Automation is the real free-tier currency. Subscriber caps are visible; automation gates are not. A free tier with 10,000 subscribers and one automation (Kit) serves a different business than one with 2,500 subscribers and full automation (Sender). Match the gate to your engine.
  • Daily send caps shape your calendar. Brevo's 300-a-day limit is generous until you run a launch week. If you send in bursts — launches, events, holiday pushes — a daily cap is a harder wall than a monthly one.
  • Never build on a free tier you haven't read the paywall of. Your list, your automations, and your sender reputation are switching costs. The vendor knows that. Read the first paid tier's price before you import a single contact.
  • Branding on free emails is real. Every free tier here puts the vendor's badge in your emails. For a personal newsletter nobody cares; for client work or a premium brand, it's a reason to start paid on day one.
The picks
Best overall free tier: Sender — 2,500 subscribers, 15,000 emails, full automation, $10/month when you outgrow it. The cheapest exit in the comparison.

Best for audience builders: Kit's Newsletter plan — 10,000 subscribers and unlimited sends at $0; accept the one-automation limit or pay for Creator.

Best for big lists, light sending: Brevo — 100,000 contacts stored, 300 emails a day, SMS included; watch the daily cap on launch weeks.

Skip as a free plan: Mailchimp (500 emails a month is a demo, not a plan) and anything still listing Moosend's dead free tier.

The rule: free is an on-ramp. Drive it like one — know your exit, price your future list, and never let a free tier you didn't research become the foundation your business is built on.

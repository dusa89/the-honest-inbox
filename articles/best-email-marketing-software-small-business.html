Every "best email marketing" list quotes the cheapest headline price and calls it a day. That's useless, because nobody pays the headline price for long. This guide prices six serious tools at three real list sizes — 1,000, 5,000, and 25,000 subscribers — ranks the free plans by what they actually let you do, and flags the billing traps each vendor hopes you won't notice.

Quick picks (details below)
Best value newsletter: MailerLite — cheapest paid plans at every list size we priced.
Best for creators: Kit — free to 10,000 subscribers, built to sell digital products.
Best all-in-one: GetResponse — webinars, funnels, and courses under one bill.
Best for big lists that send rarely: Brevo — priced on sends, not subscribers.

The free plans, honestly ranked

Free tiers are where vendors compete hardest — and cut hardest. Ranked by what you can actually run a business on:

  1. Kit — free to 10,000 subscribers. Unlimited broadcasts, landing pages, and forms. The catch: automation is limited on the free plan, so welcome sequences and drip campaigns need the paid Creator plan ($39/mo). Still, nothing else lets you email 10,000 people for $0.
  2. GetResponse — free to 500 contacts / 2,500 emails a month. A genuinely usable starter: landing pages, forms, basic automation. Outgrow it and Starter is $19/mo.
  3. Brevo — free to 300 emails a day. Unlimited contacts, which is rare. But 300/day means a 5,000-person list takes over two weeks to reach once. It's a testing tier, not an operating tier.
  4. MailerLite — free to 250 subscribers / 2,500 emails a month. This used to be 1,000 subscribers; it was cut to 500 in September 2025 and to 250 in the 2026 overhaul. Fine for validating an idea, not for running on.
  5. Flodesk — free list-building only. Forms and landing pages to collect subscribers; regular sending needs a paid plan. Useful as a waiting room, not as an email tool.
  6. Mailchimp — free to 250 contacts / 500 emails a month. Once the most generous free plan in the industry (2,000 contacts in 2022). Now it's a trial with a 250-per-day send cap and no automation. You'll outgrow it in weeks.

MailerLite — the value pick

Cheapest paid plan at every list size we priced: $19/mo at 1k, $49/mo at 5k, $179/mo at 25k on the Comfort plan. In late 2025/2026 MailerLite renamed its plans — Growing Business became Comfort, Advanced became Power — and raised prices across the board, but it's still the price leader.

Watch the send cap: Comfort limits monthly sends to 10× your subscriber count (1,000 subs = 10,000 emails/mo). Send daily to a big list and you'll hit it; Power ($39/mo at 1k, $69/mo at 5k) removes the cap. Annual billing saves 10%.

Who it's for: small businesses sending newsletters, promos, and basic automations who want the lowest bill without giving up a real editor, templates, and 24/7 email support (included on Comfort).

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) — the creator pick

Kit rebranded from ConvertKit in 2024. It's built for creators who sell: courses, paid newsletters, digital products, with built-in commerce and a creator network for cross-promotion. The free plan (10,000 subscribers) is the most generous in the industry by an order of magnitude.

The price jump is the story: $0 to $39/mo the moment you need full automations, then $89/mo at 5k and $199/mo at 25k on Creator. Kit is the cheapest tool at $0 and one of the priciest once you're paying — at 5,000 subscribers it costs nearly double MailerLite. Annual billing gives roughly two months free. Kit migrates your list from another tool free on paid plans.

Who it's for: creators, coaches, and newsletter operators monetizing an audience. Not the pick for a local service business that just needs a monthly newsletter — you'd pay creator prices for features you'd never touch.

GetResponse — the all-in-one pick

GetResponse retired its old plan names in January 2025; the lineup is now Starter / Marketer / Creator (plus Free and enterprise MAX). Starter at $19/mo (1k), $54/mo (5k), $174/mo (25k) includes unlimited sends — rare at this price. The real value unlock is Marketer ($59/mo at 1k): unlimited automation workflows, webinars to 100 attendees, sales funnels. Creator ($69/mo at 1k) adds webinars to 300, a course builder, and an AI website builder.

Notes: annual billing saves 18%, one of the best discounts in the category. Prices are quoted net of tax. GetResponse counts contacts per list — the same address on multiple lists can inflate your bill, so keep lists consolidated. Nonprofits get 50% off, the deepest nonprofit discount we've seen.

Who it's for: businesses that want webinars, funnels, and courses without stitching four tools together. If you only need newsletters, MailerLite is cheaper.

Brevo — the big-list, low-volume pick

Brevo is the odd one out: it prices on emails sent, not subscribers, with unlimited contacts on paid plans. A 25,000-person list you email twice a month (50k sends) costs $97/mo on Standard. On Mailchimp that same list is ~$270/mo. The bigger your list relative to your send frequency, the more Brevo wins.

The trap: the $9/mo Starter headline buys 5,000 emails with no automation. Automation starts on Standard ($18/mo at 5k emails). Price your real send volume with automation included, not the headline.

Who it's for: businesses with large lists and modest send frequency — event venues, publishers, seasonal businesses. Also strong for transactional email (order confirmations, password resets) on the same account.

Flodesk — the design pick

Flodesk's famous flat-rate pricing — one price no matter your list size — was retired for new users on December 2, 2025. (Existing accounts were grandfathered.) New users are on tiered plans: Lite (from $19/mo annual at 1k, capped at 25,000 subscribers and one workflow) and Pro (from $25/mo annual at 1k, unlimited workflows). At 10k subscribers Pro is $72/mo annual — still competitive, but the "never worry about list growth" era is over.

Fair credit: Flodesk counts only active subscribers — unsubscribed, bounced, and cleaned contacts don't push you into higher tiers. And the templates genuinely are the best-looking in the category for non-designers. No native A/B testing, though — a real gap at these prices.

Who it's for: design-led brands, bloggers, and boutiques where beautiful emails matter more than automation depth.

Mailchimp — only if you're already embedded

Mailchimp is fine software with a punishing price curve: $26.50/mo at 1k contacts becomes $75/mo at 5k and ~$270/mo at 25k on Essentials, for a fairly basic feature set. Standard (multi-step automation) is $40/mo at 1k, $100/mo at 5k.

The trap that actually costs people money: since 2023, Mailchimp bills for all contacts — including unsubscribed ones — unless you manually archive them. A 10,000-contact list with 2,000 unsubscribes bills as 10,000 until you clean it. Audit quarterly.

Who it's for: businesses already deep in Mailchimp's ecosystem (its 300+ integrations are the widest in the category) who'd pay more in migration pain than they'd save switching. New users in 2026 have better options above.

Billing traps, all in one place

  • Unsubscribed contacts that still bill (Mailchimp) — archive or delete them quarterly.
  • Send caps on "unlimited contacts" plans (MailerLite Comfort: 10× subscribers; Brevo: volume tiers) — price your send frequency, not just your list.
  • Automation paywalled above the headline price (Brevo Starter has none; Kit free is limited) — the cheapest tier often can't run a welcome sequence.
  • Free plans that quietly shrank — MailerLite 1,000→250, Mailchimp 2,000→250. Re-check before you build on one.
  • Prices quoted net of tax (GetResponse) and annual prices shown as monthly equivalents (most vendors) — read the checkout total.

The bottom line

At 1,000 subscribers, the spread between the cheapest serious option (MailerLite or GetResponse Starter at $19/mo) and the priciest (Kit Creator at $39/mo) is $20 a month — $240 a year. At 25,000 subscribers, the spread between Brevo Standard ($139/mo at 100k sends) and Mailchimp Essentials (~$270/mo) is $130+ a month — over $1,500 a year. The bigger your list, the more the choice matters. Pick by profile, price at your actual list size, and re-check annually — this market reprices constantly.

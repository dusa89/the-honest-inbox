This used to be the easiest comparison in email marketing: MailerLite was cheap and simple, ActiveCampaign was expensive and powerful, and the $20-a-month gap made the choice obvious. Then 2026 scrambled both sides. MailerLite renamed its plans to Comfort and Power, raised prices, and cut the free plan to 250 subscribers. ActiveCampaign consolidated into Starter/Plus/Pro/Enterprise and quietly started billing all contacts — including unsubscribed ones — in November 2025. The rematch is closer than anyone remembers: both platforms now open at exactly $19/mo for 1,000 contacts, and the winner depends entirely on what you send, not what you pay.

TL;DR
Pick MailerLite if your email is newsletters and promotions — Comfort at $19/$49/$179 (1k/5k/25k) beats Starter on every curve point until 5k, and the 10× send cap (10 emails per subscriber per month) rarely binds a normal newsletter cadence. Pick ActiveCampaign if automations drive your revenue — Starter caps you at 5 automation actions and 10,000 contacts, so the real automation home is Plus at $59/mo (1k), and it's genuinely the best automation engine under $100/mo. The trap on each side: ActiveCampaign bills your unsubscribed contacts since November 2025, and MailerLite's Power tier is what the old Advanced features became — check before you assume.

What the money actually buys (it's not just price)

These two products charge $19 at 1k for fundamentally different jobs.

MailerLite is a newsletter machine. Drag-and-drop builder that non-designers actually like, landing pages, websites, popups, surveys, and paid newsletters built in. The automation builder exists and is genuinely good for its class — visual, multi-trigger, easy to maintain. What it isn't: a behavior engine. If your marketing is "send the weekly email and a few promo blasts," Comfort gives you a tool that stays out of your way at half to a third of Starter's cost at every list size above 1k.

ActiveCampaign is a behavior engine. Site tracking, event-based triggers, split-path automations, lead scoring, CRM pipeline, and SMS under the same roof. The Starter tier teases this — 5 automation actions and a 10,000-contact ceiling make it a gateway tier, not a destination. Plus is the real product: the automation builder most reviewers still call the best under $100/mo, and the reason businesses graduate to ActiveCampaign instead of away from it.

Three gotchas before you decide

1. ActiveCampaign bills your unsubscribed contacts. Since November 2025, every contact in the system counts toward your tier — unsubscribed, bounced, and duplicates included. If you import a dirty 8,000-contact list to run a re-engagement, you could blow through Starter's 10,000 ceiling before you send a single email. Clean first, import second, and audit the contact count quarterly.

2. MailerLite's send cap is real but rarely the binding constraint. 10× your subscriber count in monthly sends means 1,000 subscribers get 10,000 emails/month — roughly 2–3 sends a week. Newsletter operators never notice. If you run daily deals or transactional-style volume on a marketing plan, you'll feel it before ActiveCampaign users feel anything.

3. The June 2026 MailerLite overhaul moved the goalposts. Comfort is the old Growing Business renamed, Power is the old Advanced — and prices went up across the curve while the free plan dropped from 1,000 to 250 subscribers. Any "MailerLite is $10" memory from 2024-era reviews is stale; the new floor is $19, exactly ActiveCampaign Starter's floor.

Which plan should you actually pick?

Take MailerLite Comfort if: your email program is newsletters, announcements, and promotions; your automations are welcome series + abandoned cart, not branching behavior trees; you want the lowest bill at every list size up to 25k. This is most small businesses.

Take ActiveCampaign Plus if: automations measurably drive revenue (courses, ecommerce, coaching, agencies); you need lead scoring, a pipeline, or SMS in the same system; your list is behaviorally segmented. Accept that you'll pay roughly 2× the MailerLite bill at every list size for the engine — and that Starter is a trial in disguise: budget for Plus from day one if automation is the reason you're here.

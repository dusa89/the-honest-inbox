The Kit-to-MailerLite move is the second most-searched migration in email marketing, and the money case is cleaner than Mailchimp's: Kit bills fairly — it counts unique, active subscribers only, no dead-contact padding — so there is no clawback trick here. The savings are pure plan arbitrage. The same 5,000-subscriber list costs $89 a month on Kit Creator and $49 on MailerLite Comfort. But three things can erase the win before you start: the free-plan downgrade (Kit's $0 tier carries 10,000 subscribers; MailerLite's carries 250), the send cap (Kit paid plans are unlimited; Comfort caps you at 10× your list), and Kit Commerce (leaving means leaving a 0% platform fee). This guide is the money math first, the seven traps second, and the weekend checklist last.

TL;DR
Migrating pays if you are on Kit Creator (or above) and send roughly weekly: you save $10–$40/mo at every verified band, and the Kit free-plan-to-paid cliff ($0 to $139 at subscriber 10,001) makes the escape urgent for fast-growing lists. Do NOT migrate if you are on Kit's free plan with no automation needs ($0 beats MailerLite's paid Comfort every time), if you sell through Kit Commerce (0% platform fee), or if you send daily (Comfort's 10× send cap pushes you to Power, where the price advantage vanishes). Budget a weekend: export the Confirmed-only CSV, start the MailerLite account early (approval review takes ~a day), verify domain auth from zero, rebuild the welcome sequence from scratch, and run both tools in parallel for one billing cycle.

The 7 traps

Trap 1: The free-plan downgrade — Kit's $0 tier is 40× MailerLite's

This is the trap that reverses the entire migration. Kit's free Newsletter plan carries 10,000 subscribers at $0, with unlimited broadcasts, forms, and landing pages. MailerLite's free plan carries 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month — MailerLite branding on your emails, no direct support. If you are on Kit's free plan and your list is under 10,000 with no automation needs, migrating to MailerLite can only cost you money: $0 becomes $12/mo at 500 subscribers, $19 at 1,000.

The one case where the free-plan move still wins: you need the visual automation builder. Kit's free plan excludes it — welcome sequences and drip campaigns are Creator features, so automations cost you $39/mo on Kit. MailerLite's free plan allows up to 3 automations, and Comfort at $19 (1k) includes full automations. A Kit free-plan creator who needs a welcome series faces $39 on Kit vs $0–$19 on MailerLite. That's the only free-plan scenario where the math flips. Decide this before you export a single CSV.

Trap 2: Exporting the wrong subscriber set

Kit's export is a straightforward CSV — but the default view includes unconfirmed, unsubscribed, and bounced contacts alongside actives, and importing that whole file is how you start MailerLite with a dirty list and a deliverability handicap. The correct path (verified against a current migration walkthrough of Kit's own export flow, October 2026): go to Subscribers, click the Status filter and select Confirmed, use the select-all checkbox plus the "Select all subscribers" option to grab everyone (not just the current page), then Bulk Actions > Export. The export processes in the background — you get an email link or grab it from the Notifications panel.

The good news: Kit's export is generous. By default it includes your tags, UTM parameters, and subscriber location data as extra columns, so your segmentation survives the trip. Tags map to MailerLite tags on import — that part is genuinely smooth. The bad news: what does not come along is covered in Trap 3.

Trap 3: Automations, sequences, forms, and pages don't transfer

Kit exports subscriber data — it does not export Visual Automations, Rules, Sequences, forms, or landing pages. None of it. Everything behavioral must be rebuilt in MailerLite's automation builder (Mailchimp migrators face the same wall; no email tool exports automation logic). Before you touch anything, inventory the full Kit setup: every automation, every sequence, every form, every landing page. Screenshot each flow with its triggers, delays, and emails.

Rebuild order matters, and so do the platform differences. Kit Sequences become MailerLite automation workflows — but note MailerLite's free plan caps you at 3 automations, and Comfort's landing-page allowance is up to 3 pages, while Kit's free plan gave you unlimited forms and landing pages. If you run eight Kit landing pages, consolidating to three is part of the migration work, not an afterthought. The silver lining, as always: most migrations rebuild leaner. That seven-email sequence from 2023 that half your list never finished? Replace it with four tight emails.

Trap 4: Kit Commerce sellers lose the 0% platform fee

This is the honest stay-on-Kit case that pricing tables miss. Selling digital products, paid newsletters, or subscriptions through Kit Commerce costs 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction on every plan, including free — and Kit's own cut is only 0.6%; the rest is card processing. MailerLite's selling story is a Stripe integration on a paid plan: Stripe's standard processing fees apply, and you're paying Comfort ($19+ at 1k) for the privilege.

Run your own product math. A creator selling a $20 product 50 times a month pays about $50 in Kit transaction costs ($1.00 per sale) plus $0 in plan fees on Kit free — vs the same Stripe costs on MailerLite plus the plan. The transaction fees are roughly equal; the difference is the plan bill on top. If commerce is a meaningful slice of your income, the migration's savings shrink fast. And the Creator Network — Kit's recommendations growth channel, including Paid Recommendations — vanishes entirely when you leave. For audience-growth-driven creators, that's worth more than $20/mo.

Trap 5: The send-cap math — daily senders gain nothing

Kit's paid plans include unlimited sends. MailerLite Comfort caps your monthly sends at roughly 10× your subscriber count — and this is the trap that quietly zeroes out the savings for the most active senders. The arithmetic:

  • Weekly sender, 1,000 subs: ~4.3 emails/month × 1,000 = ~4,300 emails. Comfort's 10,000 cap handles it. You save $20/mo. Migration wins.
  • Daily sender, 1,000 subs: ~30 emails/month × 1,000 = ~30,000 emails. Comfort's cap is 10,000. You need Power at $39/mo — exactly what Kit Creator costs at 1k. You save $0 and did a weekend of migration work for nothing.
  • Automated-heavy funnel: automated emails count against the cap too — welcome sequences, drip campaigns, every triggered send. Add automation volume to campaign volume before you decide.

The honest rule: Comfort beats Kit Creator only while your monthly sends stay under ~10× your list size. Run your last three months of Kit broadcast + automation volume against that ratio. If you're over it, either stay on Kit or price Power — Power at 1k is $39, the same number you're trying to escape.

Trap 6: The approval pause and domain auth from zero

Two startup mechanics, both from MailerLite's side. First, MailerLite reviews new accounts before your first sends — expect an account approval step and up to about a day of waiting. Start the MailerLite account before migration weekend, not during it; don't plan to end your weekend with a same-day launch to 20,000 people.

Second, SPF and DKIM authenticate your domain with the sending platform — Kit's records in your DNS do nothing for MailerLite. You must verify your sending domain in MailerLite and add its DNS records before your first real send, confirm the green checks in MailerLite's domain settings, and test-send to yourself. This is the most-skipped, most-punished step in any migration: mail from an unauthenticated domain lands in spam and torches the reputation you brought over.

And the consent question: MailerLite's import lets you add contacts as subscribed, and for a properly permissioned Kit list that's correct — these people opted in. But if your list's history is murky, import questionable segments as unconfirmed and run a re-permission campaign. That campaign will shrink your list, and that's the point.

Trap 7: The cutover gap and the day-one blast

Your website forms and landing pages keep collecting subscribers during the move. If you point the forms at MailerLite on Saturday but finish importing on Sunday, Saturday's signups exist in neither place. The fix: import first, keep Kit forms live while you rebuild, do a second "delta import" of anyone who subscribed during the move, and then update the website forms.

Then warm up properly. Your deliverability reputation partially follows the domain, not the platform — but a fresh sending setup plus a full-list blast to cold contacts is how new MailerLite accounts earn reviews and spam-folder placement. First campaign goes to your most engaged segment (opened in the last 60 days), then expand outward over 2–3 sends. Keep Kit live for one overlapping billing cycle as insurance; canceling early to save $39 while a Kit automation still fires welcome emails from the old platform is the definition of penny-wise. Export everything from Kit before you cancel — once the account closes, the archive goes with it.

The weekend checklist, in order

  1. Audit Kit. List every Visual Automation, Rule, Sequence, form, and landing page. Screenshot each flow (triggers, delays, emails). Note any Kit Commerce products and whether you use the Creator Network.
  2. Settle the free-plan question. On Kit free with no automation needs and a list under 10k? Stop — don't migrate. Need automations or crossing the 10k cliff? Proceed.
  3. Run the send math. Add your last three months of broadcasts + automated sends. If monthly sends exceed ~10× your list size, price Power, not Comfort — or stay.
  4. Open the MailerLite account early. Approval review takes up to ~a day. Don't start migration weekend on an unapproved account.
  5. Export Kit, Confirmed only. Subscribers > Status filter "Confirmed" > select all > Bulk Actions > Export. Grab the CSV from the email link or the Notifications panel. Tags, UTMs, and locations ride along.
  6. Verify your domain in MailerLite. Add the fresh SPF/DKIM records, confirm propagation and the green checks, test-send before importing anything.
  7. Import. Map Kit tags to MailerLite tags. Keep unconfirmed/questionable segments as unconfirmed.
  8. Rebuild the welcome sequence first. Then the rest of the automations (mind the 3-automation cap on free). Consolidate landing pages to MailerLite's allowance — three on Comfort.
  9. Warm up on the engaged segment. First send to openers from the last 60 days; expand over 2–3 campaigns. No day-one full-list blasts.
  10. Delta import, cut over the forms, run parallel. Catch the subscribers who signed up during the move, point forms at MailerLite, keep Kit live one billing cycle, then cancel — after a full export.

Who shouldn't migrate

Five honest exceptions. One: Kit Commerce sellers — the 0% platform fee plus Stripe-cost parity means the plan savings are the only prize, and it's small against real product revenue. Two: Creator Network users — the recommendations growth channel (and Paid Recommendations income) doesn't transfer. Three: daily senders — Comfort's 10× cap forces you to Power at $39, the same number you're leaving. Four: Kit free-plan users under 10k with no automation needs — $0 beats $12–$19 every time. Five: Kit Creator Pro users on subscriber scoring, advanced reporting, or Facebook Custom Audiences sync — MailerLite has no direct equivalent; price the feature loss, not just the bill.

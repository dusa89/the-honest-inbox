This is the comparison small businesses ask about most, and most answers get it wrong. They line up the paid tiers, declare MailerLite cheaper, and stop. The real story is stranger: Kit is dramatically cheaper at $0, and MailerLite is cheaper at every paid tier. The crossover point nobody charts isn't between two prices — it's between free and paid.

TL;DR
Under ~10,000 subscribers with basic needs, Kit's free plan wins outright — $0 vs $19+/mo. The moment you need paid features (full automations, no branding, advanced segmentation), MailerLite Comfort undercuts Kit Creator by $20–$60/mo at every list size we priced. Pick Kit if you're a creator monetizing an audience; pick MailerLite if you're a business sending newsletters and promos.

What the money actually buys (it's not just price)

Price curves don't capture the real difference: these tools are built for different people.

Kit is built for creators who sell. Tag-based subscriber management, visual automation sequences, landing pages, and — the differentiator — built-in commerce: sell digital products, paid newsletters, and memberships without a separate checkout tool. The Creator Network lets newsletters cross-promote each other. If your business model is "audience → products," Kit's $39/mo buys infrastructure MailerLite doesn't have.

MailerLite is built for businesses that send. A genuinely good drag-and-drop editor, template library, signup forms, pop-ups, and 24/7 email support on the Comfort plan. Less automation depth than Kit, more polish per dollar for straightforward newsletters and promotional campaigns. It counts only active unique subscribers — unsubscribed, inactive, and duplicate contacts don't inflate your bill.

Three gotchas before you decide

  • MailerLite Comfort has a send cap: 10× your subscriber count per month (1,000 subs = 10,000 sends). Daily senders hit this; the unlimited-sends tier is Power ($39/mo at 1k). Kit's paid plans include unlimited sends.
  • Kit's free plan limits automation. Welcome sequences and drip campaigns — the first thing most businesses automate — need the Creator plan. Price the plan you'd need in 6 months, not the one you need today.
  • Both renamed things recently. MailerLite's Comfort/Power were Growing Business/Advanced until the 2025–2026 overhaul (which also raised prices and shrank the free plan). Kit was ConvertKit until 2024. Old reviews use old names and old prices — check the date on anything you read.

Who should pick which

Pick Kit if: you're a creator, coach, or newsletter operator; your list is under 10,000 and your needs are basic (free plan = $0 for years); or you sell digital products and want commerce built in.

Pick MailerLite if: you're a small business sending newsletters and promos; you need full automations without paying $39+/mo; or your list is growing past 10,000 and the per-subscriber math starts to dominate.

Switching between them

Kit migrates your list from another email tool free on Creator and Pro plans. If you're on MailerLite's free plan approaching the 250-subscriber wall, export your list before you cross it — paid MailerLite starts at $12/mo and there's no reason to pay it if Kit's free plan covers your needs. Going the other way (Kit paid → MailerLite), the savings start immediately: $20/mo at 1k subscribers, $50/mo at 10k.

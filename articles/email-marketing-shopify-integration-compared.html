Every email tool on this site claims a "Shopify integration." Almost none of them mean the same thing by it. Some sync customers, orders, products, and cart events in real time with a free app and abandoned-cart emails on every plan. Some charge you $59 a month before the cart-recovery features unlock. And one popular budget pick — MailerLite — has a quiet gap its own help page admits: on Shopify it recovers abandoned checkouts, not abandoned carts. Those are different events, and nobody's pricing page says so. This guide is the honest map: what each tool's Shopify connection actually syncs, what plan the good parts need, and which tool to pick for your store — including the two tools we'd recommend for Shopify that we don't earn a cent from.

TL;DR
If your store lives on Shopify and email is a revenue channel, the tools built for commerce — Omnisend and Klaviyo — are the honest top picks (we don't carry them; no commission changes this). Inside our affiliate bench: Brevo is the value winner — the only tool here with abandoned-cart and purchase automations on every plan including free, and its Shopify app is rated 4.8/5. ActiveCampaign's Shopify Deep Data needs Marketing Plus or higher. AWeber connects through a third-party app, syncs roughly every 15 minutes.

Why "Shopify integration" is doing a lot of heavy lifting

Ask a store owner what the integration needs to do and it's a short list: sync the customers (respecting marketing consent), sync the orders and products, fire abandoned-cart emails automatically, show which emails drove revenue, and run win-back flows on purchase history. Here's the uncomfortable truth this guide is built on: of the eight tools below, only five do all of that natively, two hide the good parts behind higher tiers, and one has an abandoned-cart-vs-abandoned-checkout distinction buried in a help page. The rest of the article is receipts.

The eight, compared

Brevo — the free-tier outlier

The connection: official Brevo Shopify plugin, free to install. Syncs opt-in contacts and purchase data into Brevo; Brevo's own abandoned-cart guide confirms the plugin starts tracking site events and identifying abandoned carts automatically, and that it works with a free Brevo account.

The honest superpower: per a September 2026 comparison (omnisend.com, vendor-published but checkable): Brevo is the only tool in this class with purchase automation and abandoned-cart triggers on all plans, including free. Back-in-stock alerts start at Starter ($9/mo). Its Shopify app is rated 4.8/5. Revenue tracking works on all plans.

The catch: Brevo's most advanced ecommerce features (AI product recommendations, promo codes, gamified pop-ups) sit at Professional ($499/mo) — irrelevant for a small store, but know the ceiling. Multichannel flows (SMS, WhatsApp, web push) are the real differentiator at low tiers.

Pick it if: you want cart recovery without paying for cart recovery. For a small store, nothing else on this list matches free abandoned-cart automation.

GetResponse — the deepest bench, gated

The connection: native Shopify integration — syncs products, customers, orders, and abandoned carts; automated cart-recovery emails, AI product recommendations, promo-code generation, and revenue attribution to specific campaigns.

The honest superpower: the full ecommerce toolkit — AI-powered recommendations based on purchase/browse history, promo codes in emails, revenue tracking. It's genuinely the most complete cart-to-campaign loop here.

The catch: the good parts gate at the Marketer plan — $59/mo at 1,000 contacts (per the September 2026 comparison): cart abandonment, order-confirmation automation, product blocks in emails, promo codes, and revenue tracking are all Marketer-only. The integration setup itself starts by choosing an Ecommerce Marketing plan (per a 2026 setup guide). So the tool that looks competitive on price at the entry tier costs $59 before the ecommerce features unlock.

Pick it if: your store does enough volume that $59/mo is rounding error and you want the AI recommendations and promo-code machinery.

MailerLite — the budget pick with one real gap

The connection: official "MailerLite Email Marketing" app by MailerLite on the Shopify App Store. Real-time customer and order sync, product import into the drag-and-drop builder, revenue tracking, auto-generated segments (first-time customers, high-spending customers, repeat customers).

The honest catch — read this twice: MailerLite's own ecommerce-trigger help page (crawled early October 2026) states that of its 6 ecommerce triggers, Shopify and BigCommerce support all except "Abandoned cart." "Abandoned checkout" is supported. In practice: a shopper who adds items to the cart but never reaches checkout does not trigger a recovery email; a shopper who starts checkout and leaves does. MailerLite also requires customers to have subscribed and accepted marketing for the automations to fire.

Pick it if: you want the cheapest serious sender with real revenue tracking, and you're comfortable that "abandoned cart" here means "abandoned checkout." For many stores that's the bulk of recoverable revenue anyway — but now you know before you build.

Kit (ConvertKit) — creator-first, commerce-functional

The connection: official Kit Shopify app (per an October 2026 walkthrough) — API-key connect, customer sync, tags, and cart recovery built through Kit's automations rather than a purpose-built commerce flow. Kit's native abandoned-cart surface actually lives in its separate WooCommerce plugin ("send abandoned cart emails to recover lost sales," per the plugin listing).

The honest shape: Kit is a creator tool with a store attached, not a commerce tool. If your Shopify store is the merch arm of a newsletter/course/audience business — the audience comes first, products second — it's functional and the tagging model is genuinely good for segmenting buyers vs. subscribers. If the store is the business and email is the revenue channel, it's thin next to the commerce natives.

Pick it if: you're a creator who also sells things, not a store that also writes newsletters.

Constant Contact — native, quietly complete

The connection: native Shopify integration with abandoned-cart automation and sales tracking (Constant Contact knowledgebase, updated October 3, 2026) — part of the 300+ native integrations covered in this site's Zapier guide series.

The honest shape: no third-party app, no tier gate on the cart flow we could verify, and the same account that runs the events and CRM integrations runs the store. It's an honest mid-tier pick — not the cheapest, not the deepest, but the integration is real and native.

Pick it if: you're already on Constant Contact for the events/CRM side and want the store in the same account.

ActiveCampaign — deep, but gated by tier

The connection: Shopify Deep Data integration — orders, products, and cart events flowing into ActiveCampaign's automation engine, plus the deal-pipeline surface for sales follow-up (covered in this site's ActiveCampaign Zapier guide).

The catch: the Shopify Deep Data integration is available on Marketing Plus/Pro/Enterprise plans only (ActiveCampaign help center, May 2026). The entry tiers don't get the deep connection — you're paying mid-tier before the commerce features arrive, same class of gate as GetResponse, one rung higher.

Pick it if: you're already running ActiveCampaign for CRM + automations and the store feeds a real sales process.

AWeber — functional through a third party

The connection: via Combidesk's third-party "AWeber Email" app (AWeber's own docs) — customer sync into AWeber lists, abandoned-checkout routing to lists for recovery campaigns, pop-up forms on the store, roughly 15-minute sync (per a 2026 FAQ).

The honest shape: it works — AWeber's own blog describes "purchased product" and "abandoned checkout" triggers feeding its Workflow automation builder. But it's a third-party bridge, not a native app: another vendor in the chain, polling-speed sync instead of real time. For cart recovery, 15 minutes is fine. For real-time inventory or event flows, know the lag.

Pick it if: you're an AWeber loyalist and the 15-minute sync doesn't bother you. Don't switch to AWeber for its Shopify connection.

Omnisend and Klaviyo — the ones we'd actually pick for Shopify

This is where the affiliate disclosure matters: Omnisend and Klaviyo are not in our affiliate bench. We earn nothing if you choose them. And for a Shopify-first store, they're the honest answer — both are built commerce-first: deep product/order/cart sync, abandoned-cart and browse-abandonment flows as first-class citizens, SMS baked in.

The numbers (from Brevo's own Shopify-plugin comparison, 2026): Omnisend free covers 500 emails/month to 250 contacts, paid from $16/mo (6,000 emails/500 contacts). Klaviyo free covers up to 250 contacts, paid from $20/mo at 500 contacts. Both scale on contacts, not feature gates — the commerce features aren't held hostage behind a tier.

We include them because "the honest inbox" that hides the best answer for the category would be a lie wearing a disclosure. If your store is the business, start here. If you're on a budget or already in the bench's ecosystem, the rankings above are the real trade-offs.

FAQ

Does MailerLite really not do abandoned cart on Shopify?

It does abandoned checkout on Shopify — a shopper who starts checkout and leaves gets a recovery email. It does not fire its "Abandoned cart" trigger (cart-without-checkout) on Shopify or BigCommerce, per MailerLite's own ecommerce-trigger documentation. Check the help page yourself before building; vendors revise these tables.

What's the cheapest way to get abandoned-cart emails on Shopify?

Brevo, by a wide margin: abandoned-cart and purchase automations work on all plans including free, and the Shopify plugin itself is free to install. Every other tool here either charges for the feature (GetResponse's $59/mo gate) or doesn't fully offer it on Shopify.

Should I just use Shopify Email instead?

Honest answer: Shopify Email is fine for basic sends (10,000 free emails/month, then usage-based), but it's not an email marketing platform — the automation depth, segmentation, and cross-channel flows in this guide don't exist there. Stores that outgrow broadcasts land on one of the tools above. Price Shopify Email against Brevo free before assuming it's cheaper at scale.

Do I need Zapier on top of any of these?

For the core flows — customer sync, order sync, cart recovery — no: every tool above covers those natively (or via its own app). Zapier earns its place for the long tail: niche CRMs, ad-platform audiences, spreadsheets, forms tools outside the native bench.

Brevo is the one email tool that bills sends instead of contacts, and that single pricing decision splits the market in half. If you have a huge list and send rarely, nobody touches it — unlimited contacts free, pay only for what you send. But if you send weekly or daily, the meter runs against you: 5,000 subscribers at a weekly cadence is 20,000 emails a month, and suddenly the "cheap" tool costs more than every contact-priced competitor. Below: the six exits matched to your actual reason for leaving — and the honest case that the send-based model, for the right sender, is still the best deal in the category.

TL;DR
Leaving because weekly sending costs more than expected? MailerLite Comfort at $49/mo (5,000 contacts, 50k sends included) beats Brevo Standard's $69 for the same workload — the send-based model punishes frequent senders. Leaving because you need deeper automation or webinars? Brevo Standard is a platform of half-modules; ActiveCampaign or GetResponse are the real upgrades. But if you have 25,000+ contacts and send monthly, nothing on this page gets near Brevo's $139/mo Standard — MailerLite wants $179 at that size and only gets worse. Staying is about your send frequency, not your list size: compute sends-per-subscriber-per-month, and if it's under 1, stop reading and keep Brevo.

Why users actually leave Brevo

Five real reasons, ranked by how often they come up — not the invented grievances that pad out other "alternatives" listicles.

1. The meter punishes frequent senders. The number-one driver, and it's pure math. Brevo Starter runs $9 (5k emails), $17 (10k), $32 (20k), $56 (50k), $82 (100k) per month — but Starter has no automation, so the real entry price is Standard: $18 (5k), $35 (10k), $69 (20k), $97 (50k), $139 (100k). A 5,000-subscriber weekly newsletter is 20,000 sends a month — $69 on Brevo Standard, versus $49 on MailerLite Comfort (5,000 contacts, 50,000 sends included). Daily senders at 5,000 subscribers blow past 100,000 emails and hit $139 — where MailerLite's $49 Comfort still applies. Our Brevo vs MailerLite comparison charts the full crossover: once you send ~4+ emails per subscriber per month, Brevo stops being the cheap option.

2. Starter's no-automation trap. The $9 headline price is the category's best bait. Starter looks half the price of Standard at every band — but it buys volume without workflows, sequences, or triggers. If you need any automation at all, Standard is the real entry: roughly double the advertised "from $9" price. Budget for $18–$69/mo at small scale, not $9.

3. The shared-IP deliverability wall. On Brevo's lower tiers you share sending IPs with thousands of other senders — and past ~100,000 emails/month, independent reviewers consistently flag that a dedicated IP matters. Brevo's dedicated-IP world starts at the Professional tier ($499/mo). That's the real cliff in the ladder: there is no mid-range deliverability upgrade, just a $360 jump from Standard's top band.

4. The platform-of-half-modules problem. Brevo's dashboard carries a CRM, ads, landing pages, and multi-channel messaging alongside email — and several of them are a step behind purpose-built tools. Reviewers' recurring pattern: nobody's entire stack lives in Brevo; it's the cheap sender surrounded by compromises. If you actually use the platform layer, GetResponse's webinars-and-funnels bundle is the more serious version of the same idea.

5. The interface never got simple. The busiest dashboard in the category. Everything MailerLite does in an afternoon takes longer in Brevo, because the dashboard shares space with CRM and ad features you'll never open. For solo senders and volunteer-run lists, this is a genuine cost — time, not money.

The 6 alternatives, matched to your reason for leaving

1. MailerLite — if you're a frequent sender

The direct swap for anyone leaving over reason #1. Comfort runs $19/mo at 1,000 contacts, $49 at 5,000, $179 at 25,000 — contact-priced with 10× your subscriber count in sends included each month, so the meter runs with you instead of against you. At 5,000 subscribers sending weekly, you save $20/mo on day one; at daily send cadences the gap is $90+. What you gain: the simplest interface in the category and real automation from the $12/mo entry. What you give up: unlimited free contact storage (dedupe matters for your bill) and Brevo's transactional-API strength. Full send-profile math in Brevo vs MailerLite.

2. GetResponse — if you wanted an actual all-in-one

For the Standard user who likes Brevo's platform idea but has outgrown its execution. GetResponse is the serious version of the same pitch: native webinars, conversion funnels, a course builder, and unlimited emails on every tier. Starter runs $19/mo at 1,000 contacts — the cheapest major-tier entry in the category — but the real product is Marketer at $59, with the webinar and funnel modules Standard users were squinting at. The trade: it's contact-priced, so the big-quiet-list advantage vanishes — at 25,000 contacts Marketer is $349 vs Brevo's $139. Only switch if the modules are what you're buying. Our GetResponse alternatives guide runs the reverse math.

3. ActiveCampaign — if you hit Brevo's automation ceiling

The upgrade move for the Standard user whose automations have outgrown Brevo's builder. Starter matches GetResponse's own entry at $19/mo for 1,000 contacts, with the deepest automation branching, lead scoring, and a real CRM in the category; the trade is a bill that climbs to $99/mo at 5,000. Only take this exit if automations are the work: newsletter-only users are buying complexity they'll never use. See ActiveCampaign vs Kit for the automation-vs-simplicity trade-off.

4. Kit — if you're a creator who got here for the price

For the Brevo user who is actually a creator — courses, paid newsletters, launches. Kit's free tier covers up to 10,000 subscribers, which beats Brevo's $18–$69 Standard at every band for a list that size, and Creator ($39/mo at 1,000) adds commerce, the Creator Network, and automations built around selling. The trade: Kit is contact-priced and roughly 2× the value tools on paid tiers, so only switch if the creator tooling is what you actually need. Our Kit alternatives guide charts the full creator-side exits.

5. AWeber — if you want the anti-dashboard

The least-fashionable exit, and the right one for a specific reader: the small business that is done with multi-channel dashboards and wants email to just work. Lite runs $25/mo at 1,000 contacts, the free tier covers 500 subscribers, and there's no CRM or ads module to get lost in — it's the anti-Brevo. The trade: it bills unsubscribed contacts (flagged in our AWeber vs GetResponse head-to-head), and the automation is a generation behind Brevo Standard's. For a newsletter and a welcome sequence, it's honest money.

6. Flodesk — if your send volume fits the flat rate

The design-first exit: Flodesk charges a flat rate (from $54/mo at the sizes that matter here) with the genuinely best-in-class email templates in the category. Our Flodesk vs MailerLite guide covers the template trap — gorgeous emails, thin automation. This exit makes sense only for the Brevo user whose volume fits comfortably under the flat rate and whose brand lives in the inbox design: boutiques, studios, photographers. Run the send math first — high-frequency Flodesk senders save nothing over Brevo.

The honest case for staying: the meter is your friend

Here's what the exit guides won't say: for rare senders with big lists, Brevo is unassailable. 25,000 contacts with a monthly newsletter is 25,000 sends — $97/mo on Brevo Standard, versus $179 on MailerLite, $174 on GetResponse Starter, $199 on Kit. Nobody on this page comes within $75/mo of it. The same logic holds at any scale where contacts outnumber sends: Brevo's model is list growth without bill growth, and the longer your list grows the wider the gap gets.

The stay math, by profile. Rare senders (1 email/subscriber/month or less): keep Brevo and stop shopping — the competition prices your contact count, and Brevo doesn't care about it. Moderate senders (2–3 emails/subscriber/month): do the send-profile math from our comparison before deciding — this is where the crossover lives. Frequent senders (4+/month): take the MailerLite exit; you're the one profile Brevo punishes. And one warning before you commit long-term: Brevo's free tier is 300 emails/day with no automation and Brevo branding — a genuine trial, not a home. Commit to a paid band only after measuring a full month of real sends (confirmations, resends, and welcome sequences all burn quota), because unused emails don't roll over.

Migration notes

Brevo's unlimited contact storage makes imports painless — no dedupe anxiety on the way in or out. But automations don't migrate: budget an afternoon to rebuild every workflow, welcome sequence, and trigger in the new tool's builder, whatever it is. Contacts export clean from Brevo; suppression lists and engagement history don't. If deliverability is part of why you're leaving, don't skip the re-warm: a cold sender domain moved to a new platform with shared IPs can land worse before it lands better.

The 7 alternatives, matched to your store

1. Omnisend — the ecommerce-native rival

Omnisend was built to do exactly what Klaviyo does — Shopify-grade flows, abandoned carts, post-purchase sequences — for less money at every tier: $20/$65/$245 Standard vs Klaviyo's $30/$100/$360–$400. The free plan is the anti-Klaviyo free plan: same 250-contact/500-email shape, but with 24/7 support, A/B testing, and web push included — everything Klaviyo gates. Pro ($59/$90/$370) adds unlimited emails and the advanced reporting. The one structural difference: SMS is a Pro-plan add-on, so SMS-heavy stores should price Pro against Klaviyo's Email+SMS bundle, not Standard against Email. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

2. MailerLite — the straight switch

If your Klaviyo setup is campaigns, promos, and flows you actually built, MailerLite does all of it for roughly a third of the bill: $19 vs $30 at 1,000 profiles, ~$49 vs $100 at 5,000. The builder is simpler, the paid tier includes the automation, and there's no platform branding. The honest gap: Klaviyo's predictive analytics and Shopify attribution depth are in another league — if your team lives in those dashboards, that's the cost of the switch. Our full math is in MailerLite pricing explained.

3. GetResponse — feature parity for less

If you use Klaviyo's deeper features (segmentation, multivariate testing, webinars), GetResponse Starter is the closest match — $19/$54/$174, undercutting Klaviyo by $11–$226/mo at every tier. The warning from our GetResponse pricing explainer: the $19-to-$59 Starter-to-Marketer jump is the steepest single-tier jump in email — Marketer is where the real automation lives, so budget $59 if your flows are complex. Either way it beats Klaviyo's bill.

4. Brevo — the volume play

Brevo doesn't charge per profile at all — it charges per email sent. A 25,000-profile list with a weekly campaign costs roughly $139/mo where Klaviyo charges $360–$400. The tradeoffs are real: the entry Starter tier has no automation, and the 2,000-contact cumulative automation ceiling is the quietest trap in the category — read our Brevo-to-MailerLite migration guide before committing. Brevo is the answer when your list is big and your automation needs are modest. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

5. Kit — the free-to-10k play

Kit's free plan covers 10,000 subscribers — it swallows Klaviyo's entire entry tier whole. If your needs are basic (broadcasts, simple sequences, landing pages), you pay $0 for years while Klaviyo charges you $20–$100 for the same list. Paid starts at Creator ($39/$89/$199) — more than MailerLite at every paid tier — so Kit is the answer only while you fit in the free plan. Simple stores fit; automation-heavy stores don't. Full math in Kit pricing explained.

6. ActiveCampaign — the automation power pick

If the flow logic is your business — behavior-triggered sequences, lead scoring, CRM-grade automation — ActiveCampaign's Starter ($19/mo at 1,000 contacts) is the cheapest entry into serious automation, and the $59/mo tier is the real product. The catch at Starter: exactly one automation, which makes it a demo rather than a tool — budget the jump. The stores that should pick it over Klaviyo are the ones whose flows outgrew "campaigns plus flows" and became business logic. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

7. Flodesk — the design-first pick

Flodesk looks better than everything else here and knows it: $38/month flat, unlimited profiles and sends — no profile cliffs, no growth penalty. The templates are genuinely beautiful and the builder is the most pleasant in the category. It loses on power: weaker automation, weaker segmentation, weaker analytics than Klaviyo or any tool above. If your brand lives or dies on how the email looks (fashion, beauty, boutiques, creators selling merch), that's a fair trade. If your flows are the revenue engine, pick anything else. (No verified affiliate program — uncompensated.)

Who should stay on Klaviyo

Three honest cases. One: data-heavy teams — if your store genuinely uses Klaviyo's predictive analytics, cohort reports, and purchase-pattern insights to make money decisions, the alternatives don't match that depth; the bill is buying the analytics, not the emails. Two: SMS-heavy users on Email+SMS — SMS inside the same flows with one bill is genuinely convenient, and Klaviyo's SMS credit math is competitive at scale; switching means re-wiring SMS separately. Three: stores where migration cost exceeds the savings — a 100,000-profile store with 40 flows saves maybe $1,000/mo switching but pays weeks of rebuild labor. Everyone else: clean the list first (Klaviyo bills for checkout-captured non-buyers — archiving dead profiles can drop you a full tier before you spend a dollar).

How to actually switch

One afternoon, three steps. 1. Clean first — Klaviyo's active-profile billing means every profile costs you, including non-mailable checkout captures; archive and delete before you export, and the plan you need shrinks with the list. 2. Export profiles (unsubscribes and bounces included — your new tool needs the suppression list), lists, and templates. 3. Rebuild flows by hand — this is the real cost of switching; no tool imports Klaviyo's flow logic. Run the new tool in parallel for one send cycle before canceling, and don't cancel Klaviyo before your final send.

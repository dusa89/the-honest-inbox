Fitness email is not "newsletter" email. You send more of it (weekly class schedules, challenge daily prompts), to smaller lists, with a seasonal January surge — and your booking software already handles the reminders. Most "best email tool" lists rank the same generic winners and never price the reality that matters: a gym's list grows in January and dies in March, and you pay for both directions.

The thing most fitness email advice ignores: your booking software already does half the job

If you run Mindbody, ZenPlanner, Glofox, Pike13, or any class-booking platform, it already sends class reminders, waitlist alerts, receipts, and failed-payment nudges. Those are transactional — and they're the bulk of a fitness business's email volume.

So a marketing email tool in fitness has exactly four jobs, and no more:

  1. Schedule broadcasts. Weekly class timetables, holiday hours, substitute-coach announcements. High volume, low value per send — this is why the price table below matters.
  2. Challenge funnels. The 21-day, 30-day, or 6-week challenge sequence: signup → daily prompts → mid-challenge slump recovery → finish-line upsell. This is the highest-ROI email in fitness, and it needs real automation (multi-email sequences with delays and conditions), not just broadcasts.
  3. Reactivation. "We haven't seen you in 3 weeks" sequences, win-back offers, at-risk member drips. For gyms, retaining a member beats acquiring one by a wide margin — this sequence exists to stop silent churn.
  4. Upsell and cross-sell. Personal training to class members, nutrition coaching to PT clients, merchandise to everyone. Segmented offers, not blasts.

The honest consequence: any email tool that duplicates your booking software's reminders is a trap — you pay for the same message twice and train members to ignore you. When you evaluate the tools below, you're evaluating whether they run the four marketing jobs well. The reminders are already handled.

A note on SMS before the tools

Class reminders and last-minute schedule changes belong in SMS, not email — open rates on booking reminders are an SMS game. Several of the tools below (GetResponse, Brevo) bundle SMS; MailerLite does not. If your members expect text reminders and your booking platform doesn't send them, price the SMS add-on separately — don't pretend email covers it.

Who each tool is really for in fitness

MailerLite — the studio/budget pick

Unlimited automations on the cheap plan means your welcome series, 30-day challenge sequence, and reactivation drip all run simultaneously without a plan upgrade — this is exactly where Flodesk Lite's one-workflow limit disqualifies it for fitness. Segmentation by class type ("barre attendees," "6am crew") lets schedule broadcasts target the right people. The 10× monthly send cap is the number to watch: 1,000 contacts means 10,000 emails/month — a weekly schedule broadcast (4,300) plus one challenge sequence can get close. Price your send volume, not just your list.

Kit — the online-program coach pick

If you sell programs, courses, or challenge upsells online, Kit's built-in commerce removes the "email tool + separate checkout" tangle. The free-to-10k plan is genuinely the most generous in the category — a coach building an audience pays $0 until the audience is real. The trade-off: Kit is creator software, so gym-facing features (class schedules, event marketing) feel bolted on.

GetResponse — the live-class pick

Native webinars on the Marketer plan mean a coach running live Zoom-style workouts or group coaching calls doesn't pay for a separate webinar tool — that alone can justify the $59 entry point if webinars are your funnel. Marketer also bundles courses, which covers challenge upsells. Don't buy it for the webinar feature and then not run webinars; that's the entire value proposition.

ActiveCampaign — the high-ticket pick

For personal trainers selling $2,000–$5,000 packages, ActiveCampaign's lead scoring and CRM pipeline justify the bill — one converted consultation pays for a year. For everyone else in fitness, it's overpriced complexity. And know the billing model: since late 2025, ActiveCampaign bills all contacts, including unsubscribed ones — which collides directly with the January problem below.

AWeber — the simple pick

Straightforward, rarely the cheapest, rarely the worst. The Plus plan at $45/mo for 1k is honest mid-range; the free plan covers tiny lists. AWeber also bills unsubscribed contacts — same January problem as ActiveCampaign, at a lower stakes price.

Flodesk — the aesthetic pick (with a caveat)

Boutique studios with strong brands genuinely benefit from Flodesk's design quality — a barre or Pilates studio's audience judges the pixels. But the one-automation-workflow limit on Lite is a fitness dealbreaker for most: you need at least a welcome sequence, a challenge sequence, and a reactivation sequence running at once. Pro ($28/mo at 1k) removes the limit — so price Pro, not Lite.

The January trap: fitness lists swell and die, and you pay both ways

Fitness lists have the most seasonal churn of any vertical on this site. January challenge signups can double your list; by March, half those contacts are dormant — still billed as contacts on every contact-priced tool. Three defenses:

  • Clean quarterly. Remove or sunset inactive contacts before the list growth reprices you. This is a calendar task, not a software feature.
  • Mind the billing model. Tools that bill unsubscribed contacts (ActiveCampaign, AWeber) punish the natural attrition fitness lists have. Tools that bill active contacts only (Flodesk) or generous free tiers (Kit) soften it.
  • Run reactivation before deletion. The "we miss you" 3-email sequence to 60-day-dormant contacts is both the honest retention play and the cheapest way to earn the delete decision. Most tools' automation builders handle this natively.

The bottom line

The honest rule: a studio or coach running real automations picks MailerLite — $19/mo at 1k with unlimited workflows is the fitness sweet spot.

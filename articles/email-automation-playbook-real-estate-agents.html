The 4 baseline drips (build these first)

These are the four sequences from the vertical guide — the pipeline itself. Build them before anything clever:

1. New-buyer drip (6 emails, 14 days)

Trigger: buyer tagged at first contact — website search, open house, or referral. Sequence: six emails over 14 days: market update for their target area, 2–3 listing matches, a financing pre-approval nudge (the step buyers stall on), a "what the next 30 days look like" timeline, and the soft showing request. The job is turning a cold searcher into a scheduled showing before they bond with a listing portal.

2. Seller nurture drip (5 emails, ongoing)

Trigger: homeowner tagged as seller-curious — CMA request, valuation-tool signup, or "thinking about selling" conversation. Sequence: five emails on a slow cadence: local price-trend data, a before/after case study of a recent listing, the "what your home could sell for" hook, objection handling (timing, commission, market softness), and the listing-presentation offer. Sellers decide over months; this drip stays in the room the whole time.

3. Open-house follow-up (2 emails, 48 hours)

Trigger: attendee tag from the sign-in sheet — import only with consent (see the compliance section). Sequence: email one within 24 hours ("the home you toured, plus 2 similar listings"), email two at 48 hours (the showing request or the "didn't fit? tell me your criteria" reply hook). This is the highest-leverage 2-email sequence in the business — it converts warm foot traffic into appointments.

4. Past-client and SOI nurture (the listing pipeline)

Trigger: past-buyer tag at closing, plus your sphere-of-influence list. Sequence: quarterly market updates to the full SOI and past-buyer segments — not "just listed" spam, actual local data. The average past buyer refers or returns in 5–7 years; this drip is what makes sure they call you and not the agent whose postcard they got yesterday. (This is not a stat from a study — it's the operating logic of the drip.)

The 3 advanced automations

5. New-listing alerts for saved-search buyers

Trigger: buyer clicks 2+ listings in one zip code or tags themselves "looking in 89123" — the email tool's behavior tracking, or a saved-search feed from your CRM. Action: when a matching listing hits, the buyer gets it the same day with your commentary, not the portal's generic feed. The honest mechanics: most email tools can't watch your MLS. Agents wire this with a saved-search RSS feed or a Zapier bridge from the CRM into a campaign trigger — the automation fires from the email tool, the inventory comes from the CRM/IDX feed. If you sell in a market where listings move in days, this automation is the difference between your buyer seeing the home and someone else's buyer seeing it.

6. Price-drop and behavioral re-engagement triggers

Trigger: buyer views 3+ listings but no reply in 14 days, or a saved listing drops in price. Action: the price-drop alert ("the home you saved is down $15k") or the re-engagement check-in ("still looking in Summerlin?"). This is the only automation in the playbook that requires behavior-based multi-trigger logic — and that logic is a paid-tier feature: on MailerLite it means Power ($39/mo at 1k), not Comfort ($19). Everywhere else it means the automation-grade tier too (Kit Creator, GetResponse Marketer, ActiveCampaign Plus). Price this jump before you promise yourself behavioral triggers.

7. The referral and home-anniversary engine

Trigger: date-based — 60 days post-closing, 12-month home anniversary, then annually. Action: 60 days after close: the review request (Google review while the memory is warm). 12-month anniversary: the "happy home-iversary" email with their home's current estimated value. Annually after: the referral ask — "who do you know thinking about buying or selling this year?" Referral business costs you nothing but the automation; this engine is the cheapest revenue in the playbook because the emails are date-triggered and run forever once built.

The send math: do the drips fit the cap?

Worked example — an agent with 1,000 contacts on MailerLite Comfort ($19/mo, send cap roughly 10× the list = ~10,000 emails/mo): four broadcast campaigns a month = 4,000 emails. The new-buyer drip to ~50 new buyers a month = ~300 emails. Quarterly SOI nurture = ~1,000 per quarter. Open-house follow-ups and anniversary triggers = a few hundred. Total: roughly 6,000–7,000 emails a month — comfortably under the cap. The cap only bites agents who send weekly listing alerts to large lists: a 5,000-contact list with weekly campaigns burns ~21,500 emails a month, still under Comfort's ~50,000 cap at 5k — but daily alerts plus heavy drip volume is where Power ($39) or a send-based tool earns its keep. Your volume will differ; add your campaign sends plus your drip sends before you pick the tier.

The CRM question: don't buy what your brokerage already has

Before you build a single drip in an email tool, check your brokerage CRM — LionDesk, kvCORE, and BoomTown all ship drip campaigns, and many agents are already paying for automation they never turned on. The honest division of labor: the CRM owns transaction triggers (listing alerts, price changes, saved-search feeds) because it holds the MLS data; the email tool owns the narrative drips (buyer education, seller nurture, SOI updates) because its builder, templates, and deliverability are built for it. Agents who duplicate the whole system in both tools pay twice and maintain neither. Zapier or native integrations bridge the two — a new saved search in the CRM can tag the contact in the email tool and fire the matching drip.

The 3 mistakes that kill agent pipelines

  • Building on the wrong tier. Kit's free plan covers your whole list and none of your automations; GetResponse Starter gives you one workflow for a seven-automation playbook; MailerLite's free plan caps you at 3 automations. Every automation built on a tier that can't run it is a rebuild. Pick the tier from the table above, then build.
  • Automating from an unauthenticated domain. SPF and DKIM authenticate your domain with the sending platform — records set up for your CRM do nothing for your email tool. Verify the sending domain in the email tool, confirm the green checks, and test-send before the first drip fires. A welcome sequence that lands in spam is worse than no sequence.
  • Importing without consent, alerting stale listings. You cannot add open-house sign-in sheets to your list without a clear opt-in — CAN-SPAM penalties run up to ~$53,000 per email, and your unsubscribe handling must work (every email tool appends it automatically; let it). And listing alerts must stop when homes go pending — an alert feed that sends sold homes trains buyers to delete your emails unread.

Compliance summary, not legal advice: consent to add, unsubscribe on every send, and fair-housing care with filtered sends (the full two traps are in the vertical guide). When in doubt, ask your broker.

Most "email marketing for restaurants" lists rank tools like you're a blogger. You're not. A restaurant's email list exists to do three things: fill seats on slow nights, sell event tickets, and pull people back in for a second visit. The weekly special email is the whole channel — and picking a tool built for 500-contact newsletters means your pricing and send allowances are designed for someone else's business.

The thing nobody tells restaurants: capture is the tool decision

Your email tool doesn't fill seats. Three other things do, and they're all upstream of it:

  • Capture — Wi-Fi splash pages, QR codes on check presenters, your POS (Toast, Square), reservation platforms. A 10,000-email-a-month list never exists if you're only asking for emails at the register.
  • Your reservation system (OpenTable, Resy) actually takes the booking — the same way a coach's calendar takes the call. Email drives traffic to the reservation page.
  • Your send rhythm — weekly specials, monthly events, holiday menus. Restaurants send 4–8 campaigns a month to thousands of contacts. That's a high-send, large-list profile most tools price punitively.

The honest setup: build capture first (Wi-Fi splash + POS sync), then pick the tool priced for weekly sends to 5,000 contacts — not the tool cheapest at 500.

What restaurants actually need (and don't)

Restaurant lists run big. A working restaurant's list typically runs 2,500–10,000 contacts (our estimate — one downtown spot we studied added ~80–120 emails a week from Wi-Fi capture alone). Entry-level bands don't tell the story; price at 2,500 and 5,000.

What matters: send allowances that survive weekly campaigns (4 sends × 5,000 contacts = 20,000 emails a month — do the multiplication before you pick), event registration forms, birthday/anniversary automations, segmentation (regulars vs. first-timers vs. event attendees), POS or reservation-platform sync, and SMS overlap for day-of reminders.

What doesn't matter much: e-commerce features (abandoned cart is for stores), landing pages (your menu is your landing page), and creator-specific tools (Kit's commerce fees, webinar suites — unless you're teaching cooking classes).

The send-volume trap every restaurant hits

Do the math before anything else. A restaurant with 5,000 contacts sending a weekly special emails 20,000 times a month:

  • MailerLite Comfort: 10× contacts = 50,000 emails at 5k. Fine — with room for a mid-month event blast.
  • Constant Contact Lite: 10× contacts = 50,000. Fine on paper — but Lite's automation won't run your loyalty sequences, so restaurants land on Standard ($110/mo at 5k) anyway.
  • Brevo free: 300 emails/day ≈ 9,000/month. Dead on arrival for a 5k weekly sender — it's a $35/mo paid plan, not a free plan, for you.
  • Flodesk Everything: unlimited everything at $54/mo. This is the crossover the whole table hinges on — above ~5,000 contacts it's the cheapest automated option on the board.

The pattern: flat-rate pricing wins restaurant economics. Every per-contact tool gets punishing above 5,000; Flodesk's flat $54 stays $54 at 10,000 contacts (our estimate — flat-rate per the December 2025 overhaul). If your list is under 2,500, the per-contact tools are cheaper; if it's over 5,000, the flat rate starts pulling away fast.

The SMS overlap, honestly addressed

Restaurants need SMS more than any vertical here: day-of reservation reminders, waitlist pings, flash specials. But email tools do SMS as a side quest:

  • Constant Contact SMS add-on: $10/mo for 0–500 texts (US only, from 2026 discount roundups). Real but shallow — 500 texts covers reminder traffic for one location, not marketing blasts.
  • Brevo SMS: bundled and send-based; the most natural SMS+email combo on this list.
  • Everyone else: no native SMS worth naming — you'll integrate a dedicated SMS tool (Attentive, SimpleTexting) separately.

Our honest recommendation: don't buy your email tool for its SMS. Pick the email tool on email economics, and add SMS only when reminders are measurably saving no-shows. The one exception is Brevo, where SMS arrives as a genuine bonus on plans you'd buy anyway.

The three automations that pay for the subscription

If you run exactly three automations and nothing else, you've bought the tool's value:

  1. Birthday/anniversary offer (set-and-forget). Capture the date at signup; auto-send a free-dessert or discount code 7 days before. This single flow is why restaurants buy automation at all.
  2. First-visit win-back (60 days). Tag first-time visitors (POS sync or reservation data); if no second visit in 60 days, send "we miss you" with an incentive. Regulars are made here, not acquired.
  3. Event pre-fill (per event). Wine dinner, chef's table, holiday menu — segment past event attendees first, send them early access, then broadcast the remainder. Event attendees convert at multiples of the cold list (our estimate — event segments consistently outperform broad broadcasts).

Every tool on this list except Brevo's Starter tier runs all three natively. The differentiator is not capability — it's whether the price at your list size lets you afford the automation tier.

Billing traps, restaurant edition

  • Weekly-send math — 4 sends × 5,000 contacts = 20,000 emails/month. Price that, not the monthly newsletter you imagined.
  • Constant Contact's Lite trap — one basic automation template is a welcome email, not a loyalty system. Restaurants land on Standard.
  • Per-contact pricing above 5,000 — every contact-priced tool climbs; Flodesk's flat $54 is the crossover to beat.
  • SMS sold as email — no email tool's SMS add-on replaces a dedicated SMS platform. Constant Contact's 500-text add-on is reminders, not marketing.
  • Event fees elsewhere — Eventbrite's fees dwarf any email tool's price. Constant Contact's native event forms are the only option here that keeps the whole event inside one subscription.
  • Approval reviews — MailerLite's strictest-in-category approval applies to restaurants too; apply with real business details, especially alcohol-promoting venues.

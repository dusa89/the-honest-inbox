The thing nobody tells agents: you need two email systems

Agents email for two completely different jobs, and one tool can't do both well:

  • Listing alerts and behavioral follow-up. New listing matches for a buyer, price drops, saved-search emails, "we noticed you viewed 123 Oak St." These are triggered by MLS data, which lives in your CRM (Follow Up Boss, LionDesk, kvCORE, BoomTown), not in Mailchimp. Your CRM runs these drips natively.
  • Nurture and broadcast. Monthly market updates, "just listed / just sold" announcements to your sphere of influence, buyer/seller education drips, holiday and home-iversary touches. This is what an email marketing tool is for — better templates, better deliverability analytics, and list management your CRM can't match.

The honest setup: keep listing-triggered emails in your CRM, and run nurture emails in an email tool. Buying a premium email platform hoping it will fire MLS-triggered alerts is the most common waste of money in agent marketing. No standalone email tool plugs directly into your MLS feed.

What agents actually need (and don't)

Agent list sizes are small. Most solo agents' sphere of influence runs 200–2,000 contacts (our estimate — agent databases commonly sit in this range). That means the entry-level plan of almost every tool covers you, and "best for 50k subscribers" advice is irrelevant. Price at 500–1,000 contacts.

What matters: drip/sequence automation (buyers, sellers, past clients), segmentation by tag (buyer vs seller vs SOI vs renter), decent templates for property photos, and integration with your CRM (Follow Up Boss syncs natively with Mailchimp and ActiveCampaign and reaches the rest through Zapier).

What doesn't matter much: e-commerce features (abandoned cart, product recommendations), landing-page depth (your brokerage site handles that), and massive contact tiers you'll never fill.

The four drip sequences that win listings

Tools are the easy part. The agents who win with email run systems, not sends. Keller Williams' famous 33-touch annual cadence is the industry classic — here's the email skeleton for it:

1. New-buyer drip (6 emails, 14 days)

  1. Day 0 — Welcome + search setup. Thank them, link their saved MLS search, ask for must-haves vs nice-to-haves (reply bait that starts a conversation).
  2. Day 2 — Financing reality check. Pre-approval explained plainly, with your preferred lender's intro. Buyers who stall here stall forever.
  3. Day 5 — What the process costs. Earnest money, inspections, closing costs — kill the fear of unknown fees with real numbers.
  4. Day 8 — Neighborhood intel. Your actual local knowledge (commutes, schools, the block-by-block stuff Zillow doesn't have). This is why they email you instead of reading a portal.
  5. Day 11 — Social proof. One recent buyer story, plain and specific.
  6. Day 14 — Tour invite. Direct CTA: "Here are 3 that fit your criteria — which day works?"

2. Seller nurture drip (5 emails, ongoing)

  1. Email 1 — Market snapshot. Real numbers for their neighborhood: median days on market, sale-to-list ratio. Specific beats glossy.
  2. Email 2 — Staging that actually sells. The 3 changes with the highest return in your market, not a 40-point checklist.
  3. Email 3 — Pricing honestly. How overpricing costs sellers (days on market + first-offer anchoring), with one local example.
  4. Email 4 — The process, demystified. Timeline from listing to close, what each week looks like.
  5. Email 5 — CTA. Free home valuation offer + your calendar link.

3. Open-house follow-up (2 emails, 48 hours)

  1. Same evening — Thank you + one photo. "Thanks for stopping by 123 Oak St — here's the kitchen shot everyone asked about." Ends with: "Want a private walkthrough?"
  2. Day 2 — The nudge. Comparable recent sales nearby + "I can set up a showing this week." Two emails is enough; a third feels like pressure.

4. Past-client and SOI nurture (the listing pipeline)

NAR's annual buyer/seller profile consistently finds most sellers hire an agent they've used before or been referred to — your past clients are your future listings. Minimum viable system:

  • Monthly market update to your full list (this is where Kit free or MailerLite shines).
  • Quarterly check-in email to past clients — plain text, personal tone, no template. "How's the house treating you?" wins more referrals than any newsletter.
  • Home-iversary email on the purchase anniversary — automated, one email a year per client, feels personal every time.

Compliance: two traps agents fall into

  • You cannot add people to your list without consent. Scraping names from the MLS, importing a purchased list, or adding every open-house sign-in sheet to your newsletter without a clear opt-in violates CAN-SPAM (FTC penalties run up to ~$53,000 per email). Every list-building form needs an unchecked-by-default consent box, and every email needs a working unsubscribe. Your CRM and your email tool handle this — let them.
  • Fair housing applies to marketing. Excluding people from housing-related advertising based on protected classes is a federal issue — HUD's case against Facebook's ad targeting is the famous example. Email sent to your full opted-in list is not the problem; filtered sends that look like steering are. When in doubt, ask your broker. (This is not legal advice.)

Billing traps, agent edition

  • CRM + email tool double billing. Your brokerage CRM already has email automation — check what's included (LionDesk, kvCORE, and BoomTown all ship drip campaigns) before paying for an email tool that duplicates it. Many agents need the email tool only for broadcast newsletters.
  • Unsubscribed contacts still bill (Mailchimp, ActiveCampaign) — agents accumulate dead contacts fast from open-house lists. Clean quarterly.
  • Kit's $0-to-$39 cliff — free covers everything until you need full automation, then it's $39/mo at 1k. Budget the jump before you build your whole drip system on free.
  • Constant Contact's contact bands — the price jumps in steps as your list grows ($55/mo at 1k → $75/mo at 2,500). Growing agents should watch the band they're in.
  • Send caps on "unlimited" plans — MailerLite Comfort's 10× cap and Constant Contact's 12× cap matter when you start sending weekly. Price your send frequency, not just your list.

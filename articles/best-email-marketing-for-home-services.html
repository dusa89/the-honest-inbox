What a home services business actually needs from email

Forget "engagement metrics." The four jobs that matter:

  1. Seasonal reminder campaigns. Furnace tune-ups before winter, AC servicing before summer, gutter cleaning before fall — the whole business rhythm of a trade company. One home-services software vendor cites an HVAC client that used seasonal reminder emails to lift repeat business 20% over a year (anthemsoftware.com — their case study, their numbers; treat it as a best-case, not a promise). The lesson holds regardless: the list of past customers is the cheapest lead source you own.
  2. Review-request sequences. A home-services SEO case study documented the pattern that works: an automated ask ~24 hours after the job (usually by text), a monthly email reminder to past customers, and techs who ask in person before leaving — growing one shop from 3 reviews to 108 in six months (designloud.com, published 2026 — their client, their timeline). Your email tool needs a simple automation: job done → wait a day → ask for the review. That's it.
  3. Maintenance-plan renewal drips. The annuity every trade company wants. A three-email sequence — 60 days out, 30 days out, expiring — is the entire playbook, and it runs itself once built.
  4. Weather and event blasts. Heat wave coming → AC check emails. A batch of 5-star reviews → brag blast. New service launched → announcement. This is bulk-sending with segmentation, nothing fancier.

Notice what's missing: no lead scoring, no landing-page builder Olympics, no AI subject-line generators. Your field-service software (Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro) already runs dispatch; the email tool only has to hold the customer list, send the campaigns, and run a handful of simple automations. Paying for a marketing department's toolkit is the trap.

The 4 campaigns that pay (steal this sequence)

Whichever tool you pick, build these four first. They cover the revenue a trade company leaves on the table:

  1. The seasonal tune-up reminder. One email, two sends a year: "spring AC check" in March/April, "fall furnace check" in September/October (flip the months for Southern Hemisphere — the principle is shoulder-season). Offer: the tune-up price, the booking link, done. Home-services marketing guides consistently list this as the highest-ROI campaign type for the trade (masterful-marketing.com, 2026).
  2. The review-request sequence. Trigger: job marked complete in your field software (or a weekly CSV import if you don't integrate). Email 1: next day, thank-you + review link. If no review after 14 days, email 2: the gentle nudge with the direct link. Keep it to two emails — more reads as begging. Pair with the tech asking in person; the email is the follow-through.
  3. The maintenance-plan renewal drip. 60 days out: "your plan renews soon, here's what you used this year." 30 days out: the renewal offer with the loyalty rate. Expired: the win-back with a first-year discount. This is the annuity that smooths the seasonal revenue dips every trade company lives with (youngupstarts.com, Sept 2026).
  4. The weather blast. Heat wave forecast → "AC not keeping up? Here's the tune-up special." Freeze warning → "pipe-insulation checklist + our emergency line." Timely beats clever every time in this niche.

The honest catches

Your list import is the real setup cost. Every tool here imports CSV; the work is cleaning the customer file (dedupes, dead emails, people who never consented). Budget an afternoon, not ten minutes. The deliverability payoff is real — a dirty import is how small-business lists end up in spam. Our deliverability audit checklist covers the fix.

Jobber/ServiceTitan/etc. already send some of this. Most field-service software sends appointment confirmations and post-job surveys. Don't double-send: let the field software handle transactional job emails and let the email tool handle marketing campaigns and review asks. If your field software's marketing module is "good enough," run it for a quarter and measure — the dedicated email tool wins on automation depth and cost, but free-and-present beats paid-and-unused.

The $42-to-$1 ROI figure is old. The widely cited "email returns $42 for every $1 spent" comes from a Litmus study that trade marketing sites still quote (estrat360.com, 2024). It's directionally true for owned-list email and it's still the reason this channel beats buying leads — but it's a vendor study, not a measurement of your shop. Your real number is repeat bookings per campaign; track that, not the industry stat.

Text beats email for urgent asks — use both. The review request works best as text-then-email (the case-study pattern above), because a tech's text lands while the good experience is fresh. Email wins for the seasonal campaigns and renewals that nobody texts about. If you only do one, do the email — it costs almost nothing and the list compounds.

Is Kit any good for contractors?

Technically usable, practically wrong. Kit is priced and built for creators selling digital products — steep subscriber-based price jumps and no trade-oriented anything. MailerLite or Brevo do the same job cheaper.

Do I even need email marketing if I already text customers?

Yes — for different jobs. Text wins the urgent, time-sensitive asks (review requests right after the job). Email wins the campaigns nobody texts about: seasonal reminders, maintenance-plan renewals, weather blasts. The two together are the pattern that moves both reviews and repeat bookings.

Brevo is the only tool in this family with a transactional double life. Every other tool here is a marketing platform that borrows Zapier for plumbing. Brevo is a marketing platform and a transactional-email/SMS engine (the Sendinblue heritage), with its own SMTP API, native SMS/WhatsApp, and ~140 native integrations (Omnisend's August 2026 marketplace count). That inverts the Zapier question entirely: the marketing side is already covered natively, so Zapier's job on Brevo is narrower — feeding contacts from tools Brevo doesn't know, and working the transactional lane that nobody else's Zapier app can touch. Twelve triggers, a Send-transactional-email action, and CRM pipeline searches (Find Deal, Find Company) that only one other family member has.

TL;DR
Brevo's Zapier app is midweight — 12 triggers, 3 searches, a small action list — but two of its tricks are family exclusives: transactional-email events as triggers (a receipt email's bounce or open can start a Zap), and a Send transactional email action that fires one-to-one mail through Brevo without touching your marketing-automation tiers. Use Zapier for contact intake from non-native tools and for transactional wiring; don't use it for anything Brevo does natively (Shopify orders, WooCommerce, Typeform) or inside its own automations. Watch the transactional triggers on volume — "Transactional Email Delivered" fires on every receipt, and that's the fastest meter in this guide. And one account trap worth knowing: Shopify's native app is for new Brevo accounts; older accounts are still on the legacy integration — check which one you're on before building anything.

What "native" covers (the transactional head start)

Brevo's native bench is broad (~140 marketplace integrations, Aug 2026 count) and skews differently from everyone else's because of the transactional heritage. The headliners, verified October 2026:

  • Shopify — with an account split. Brevo's native Shopify app covers new accounts; older accounts are on the legacy Shopify integration (Omnisend's Aug 2026 comparison table flags this explicitly). This is the one native integration worth checking your account age for — order and browse data flow natively either way, but if something looks off, the legacy/new-app split is the first suspect.
  • WooCommerce, WordPress, PrestaShop, BigCommerce, Wix — storefronts and sites without a middleman. The ecommerce plugins carry order/product data for purchase-behavior segmentation.
  • Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM — the CRMs most small businesses actually use, all native. With a native CRM bench this wide, Zapier's CRM job is mostly "the odd one out" (Kommo, systeme.io, a spreadsheet-as-CRM).
  • Google Analytics, Google Ads, Facebook Messenger, Typeform, OptinMonster — analytics, ads, chat, and forms. The Typeform native path matters: it means the form-to-contact Zap most people build first on other tools is a native checkbox here.
  • The transactional side needs no connector at all. Brevo's SMTP API, transactional email, and SMS/WhatsApp are product features, not integrations. Receipts, confirmations, and alerts send through Brevo directly — which is exactly why the Zapier app's transactional triggers and send-action are interesting: they're programmatic access to the engine, not the marketing product.

The honest shape of a Brevo stack: native handles the website, the store, the CRM, and the transactional engine. What's left for Zapier is the long tail of apps Brevo doesn't know — the intake layer — plus the transactional events no other integration surface exposes.

What the Zapier connection actually supports

Brevo's Zapier app surface, from Zapier's own help doc (updated 215 days ago, checked October 2026) plus the app directory:

Triggers — the contact events: Contact Added, Contact Updated, Contact Subscribed to Specific List(s), Contact Unsubscribed. Note the gap honestly: there is no single "new or updated contact" trigger here (unlike some family members) — intake Zaps either pick "added" or "updated," and migrations that re-sync contacts can double-fire if you use both. One trigger, one contract.

Triggers — the marketing-email events: Marketing Email Delivered, Marketing Email Opened, Link Clicked, Marketing Email Hard Bounced, New Campaign Status. Familiar family territory — the follow-up engine (click the pricing link → create a sales task) with the familiar volume warning below.

Triggers — the transactional events (the family exclusive): Transactional Email Delivered, Transactional Email Opened (labeled Instant in the help doc), Transactional Email Hard Bounced. No other tool in this family's Zapier app lets a receipt email start an automation. A failed delivery confirmation for a paid invoice is now a trigger event — which is either a support-ticket machine or a bill, depending on volume.

Searches — the CRM surface: Find Contact (by email or mobile), Find Deal, Find Company. This is the quiet surprise: the Brevo Sales Platform's pipeline objects are inside the Zapier app. The only other family member with pipeline objects here is ActiveCampaign — and Brevo's version is find-only, not write. Find-first-then-write flows are possible for contacts; for deals and companies, it's find-first-then-route.

Actions — the documented core: Add or Update Contact. That's the whole action list in Zapier's help doc — honest about how thin the write side is.

Actions — the directory extras: Send transactional email, Create note, a CRM-flavored Create person, and a beta API-request action. The one that matters is Send transactional email: a Zap can fire a one-to-one transactional email through Brevo's engine — confirmations, receipts, alerts — without consuming anything from the marketing-automation side (the 2,000-contact cumulative automation ceiling on Free/Starter from our migration guide doesn't touch this lane).

Three observations. First: this app is trigger-heavy and action-light — it's built to let Brevo announce things outward more than to let Zapier do things inside. Second: the transactional triggers are the genuinely new capability; everything else is the family's standard intake kit. Third: the beta API-request action is the escape hatch for the write side — raw API calls with Brevo's auth baked in, which means the action list is exactly as big as Brevo's public API when you need it to be.

The task math (and the transactional meter)

  1. The trigger is free. Every action step costs one task.
  2. Free plan: 100 tasks/month. Starter: $19.99/mo billed annually, 750 tasks. (seedprod.com's 2026 tested guide; verify on zapier.com before checkout — Zapier revises tiers.)
  3. Transactional triggers are the fastest meter in this guide series. "Transactional Email Delivered" fires on every receipt, confirmation, and password reset. A store sending 500 transactional emails a day is 15,000 trigger events a month — with one action step each, that's 15,000 tasks, twenty times the Starter plan. Transactional triggers get a filter (hard bounces and opens on invoice mail only, never raw "delivered") or they don't get built. This is the single most expensive mistake on this app.
  4. The contact-inbound math is gentle. A 2-step Zap (trigger + Add or Update Contact) firing 100 times a month = 100 tasks — exactly the free ceiling. Add a filter or formatter step and 50 fires burns the free plan. Polling delay is the hidden tax: Transactional Email Opened is labeled Instant, but the contact and campaign triggers are polling-cycle — design as if new contacts arrive up to ~15 minutes late on the free tier.

The worked example: a Typeform → Brevo intake Zap (new response → Add or Update Contact = 1 task per response). At 60 responses/month: 60 tasks — free plan survives. At 200: 200 tasks — $19.99/mo billed annually. And the honest check: Typeform is natively integrated, so this Zap only earns its keep for form tools with no native path (a niche booking widget, a custom HTML form). Count expected actions before building, not after — and check the native list first.

Five Zaps Brevo users actually run (with honest task math)

1. Niche form tool → Add or Update Contact (the intake Zap). Not Typeform — that's native. This is the booking widget, the quote calculator, the custom form Brevo doesn't know, feeding contacts in with source tags. Two steps, 1 task per submission. At 100 submissions/month: exactly the free ceiling; at 500: Starter. The rule: if the tool has a native integration, use it — this Zap is only for the ones that don't.

2. Google Sheets new row → Add or Update Contact. The spreadsheet-as-CRM intake: event sign-up sheets, imported prospect lists, partner referrals. Two steps, low volume, and the sheet doubles as an audit trail of what the Zap did. Same math as every intake Zap — 1 task per row.

3. New Brevo contact → Kommo / systeme.io sync (the outward lane). On Zapier's own Brevo directory: contacts added to lists flowing out to Kommo (update-or-create) and systeme.io (add contact with tags). This is the CRM-that-Brevo-doesn't-know pattern — the outward direction native can't push. Two steps per contact; price it against how many contacts actually need to live in both systems.

4. Transactional Email Hard Bounced → Slack alert (the invisible-failure Zap). Failed receipts are the email nobody watches: a bounced invoice-confirmation is a customer who thinks you took their money and vanished. This trigger (filtered to invoice/receipt mail only — never the raw firehose) posts to Slack or creates a task. Volume is tiny, value is enormous — this is the best Zap in the guide and it barely touches the meter.

5. Stripe payment → Send transactional email (the receipt Zap). A purchase fires a transactional email through Brevo's engine — order confirmation, download link, appointment details — without touching the marketing-automation tiers at all. This is the family's cleanest "do it in the transactional lane" pattern: no automation ceiling, no campaign-send accounting. Caveat: it bills against your Brevo transactional quota, so verify your in-account limits before wiring a high-volume store to it.

The decision rule: native engine, Zapier edges

  1. The transactional lane is Brevo's — respect the billing. Brevo is send-volume-priced (see our Brevo-to-MailerLite migration guide for the inversion math: the migration only pays when send frequency is high relative to list size). Zapier Zaps that trigger sends — especially the transactional send action — add sends to your Brevo bill. Every Zap on this app should pass the test: does it reduce sends, or manufacture them?
  2. Native first, always. Shopify, WooCommerce, Typeform, Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive — these exist, they're maintained, and they don't consume tasks. Zapier's job is the gaps: the form builders, booking tools, and CRMs with no native path, plus the outward lanes native can't push.
  3. Transactional triggers get a filter or get killed. The bounced-receipt alert is brilliant; the unfiltered "every delivered email" trigger is a five-figure task bill wearing a workflow costume. Filter to the mail that matters (invoices, receipts, confirmations) before you ever turn one on.
  4. Keep automation logic in Brevo. The in-product automation builder is where sequences live — not in Zaps that shadow it. Zapier feeds Brevo (contacts, tags, events) and receives Brevo's announcements (triggers, searches); the journey itself stays in one place, where you can see it. (And remember the ceiling: Free/Starter plans cap cumulative automated contacts at 2,000 — don't pay Zapier to feed automations your plan can't run.)

The honest catches

The transactional meter is the whole game. This guide's most important number isn't a price — it's a pattern: transactional triggers fire at email-volume scale, not contact scale. A "Marketing Email Opened" trigger on a 10,000-contact campaign is thousands of tasks; a "Transactional Email Delivered" trigger on a 500-receipts-a-day store is 15,000 a month. The family rule applies double here: filter before you scale, and price the filter as part of the build.

No single "new or updated contact" trigger. The app offers Contact Added and Contact Updated separately. Pick one per Zap and accept its contract — or use both and design for the double-fire on re-syncs. This is a small thing that produces real duplicate-tag messes.

The action list is thin by design — the help doc lists one. "Add or Update Contact" is the documented core; the directory extras (Send transactional email, Create note, Create person, beta API request) exist but the beta API-request action is where the write side gets serious. If a flow needs heavy writing into Brevo, that's a sign to check whether Brevo's own API (or Make/n8n, verified live before buying) is the honest tool — not to force it through one documented action.

Check your Shopify integration generation. New accounts get the native app; older accounts sit on the legacy integration. Same data, different plumbing — and if your order-triggered Zaps behave oddly, the generation split is the first thing to rule out before blaming Zapier.

Zapier costs stack on top of Brevo costs. A business on paid Brevo plus Zapier Starter pays two subscriptions — see our Brevo vs MailerLite breakdown for the tier discussion (Brevo pricing itself deliberately not quoted here; it moves). Make sure the Zaps can't be native checkboxes or in-product automations first — the cheapest integration on this app is the one you delete.

FAQ

Is Brevo's Zapier integration free?

The Brevo app inside Zapier is free to connect. What costs money is Zapier itself: 100 tasks/month on the free plan, then $19.99/mo billed annually for Starter with 750 tasks. Brevo charges nothing for the connection — but Brevo's own send-volume billing still applies to every email the Zaps cause to send, which is the cost most people forget to model.

Do I need Zapier if Brevo already sends transactional email natively?

For the sending itself: no — that's the point of Brevo's double life. You need Zapier for the wiring around the engine: getting data in from tools Brevo doesn't know (the intake layer), pushing contact events out to tools Brevo can't reach (the outward lane), and reacting to transactional events (the bounced-receipt alert) that have no native handler. If the whole flow lives inside Brevo's world — Shopify order in, receipt out — Zapier adds cost and latency for nothing.

Can Zapier send transactional emails through Brevo?

Yes — the Send transactional email action exists in the app directory, and it's the cleanest one-to-one sending pattern in the family: confirmations and receipts that bypass the marketing-automation tiers entirely. Two caveats: it bills against your Brevo transactional quota (verify in-account limits before wiring a high-volume store to it), and it needs a properly authenticated sender domain — no verified domain, no sends.

How many tasks does a typical small business use?

Honest ranges: a niche-form → Brevo intake Zap at 60 submissions/month is 60 tasks (safely free). A sheet-import Zap at 100 rows is 100 tasks (exactly the free ceiling). A filtered bounced-receipt alert is a handful of tasks a month. An unfiltered transactional-delivered trigger on a 500-receipts-a-day store is 15,000 tasks — the family outlier, and the one that ends with a billing conversation. Most Brevo users running 2–3 modest intake Zaps land in the 100–500 tasks/month range; the transactional lane is where the meter either stays silent or leaves the building.

What's better than Zapier for Brevo?

For intake from the big tools: Brevo's own native integrations (free, ownerless, no tasks) — Shopify, WooCommerce, Typeform, the CRMs. For marketing logic: Brevo's automation builder, not Zaps. For the Zapier-shaped gaps at volume: Make charges per operation and n8n is a fixed server cost (verify live — automation pricing moves). For heavy write-side flows: Brevo's own API through the beta API-request action or a direct integration. The "better than Zapier" conversation on this tool usually ends at "use the native engine and keep Zapier as the intake valve" — which is also the cheapest answer.

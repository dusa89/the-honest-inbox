The entry price is the trap: ActiveCampaign Starter and GetResponse Starter both cost $19/mo at 1,000 contacts, which makes this look like a tie. It isn't. These are two different categories of product that happen to send email. ActiveCampaign is an automation engine with a sales CRM — conditional content, split automations, attribution, deal pipelines, lead scoring that feeds a sales process. GetResponse is an all-in-one platform — email plus native webinars, sales funnels, a website builder, and an AI course creator, with unlimited sends on every paid plan. The right pick has almost nothing to do with price and everything to do with how you actually sell.

TL;DR
Pick ActiveCampaign if you sell through a sales process — follow-ups, deal stages, lead scoring, attribution, and automation depth decide whether the tool earns its keep. The realistic entry is Plus at $59/mo ($49 on annual billing), because Starter caps automations at 5 actions and ships no CRM pipelines. Pick GetResponse if you sell through webinars, courses, launches, or funnels — the realistic entry is Marketer at $59/mo ($48.38 on annual billing), because Starter allows exactly one automation workflow and no webinars. Yes: the tiers people actually buy are $59 vs $59 at 1,000 contacts — a parity almost nobody charts, and it means the decision is purely about the product, not the price. What the money actually buys (the category split)

ActiveCampaign buys automation depth. The moat is the workflow builder: unlimited-action automations on Plus and up, conditional content, split automations, predictive sending, and attribution/conversion tracking (Pro). On top sits a real sales CRM — deal pipelines, contact and deal scoring, task automation — which is why agencies and B2B sellers keep paying the contact-based premium. Send limits scale with your list (10× contacts on Starter/Plus, 12× on Pro, 15× on Enterprise), and a dedicated IP arrives only at Enterprise. The honest way to think about it: ActiveCampaign is priced like a sales tool that happens to send email, and its per-contact cost is the premium for that.

GetResponse buys consolidation. The moat is breadth: native webinars (100 attendees on Marketer, 300 on Creator at $69/mo — no other email platform in our 2026 audit ships webinars as a product feature), conversion funnels with promo codes and revenue reports, abandoned-cart recovery, a website builder, and an AI course creator with premium newsletters. Every paid plan includes unlimited monthly sends, and billing counts only active subscribers. The honest way to think about it: GetResponse is priced like a bundle that happens to start at $19, and its value depends entirely on which bundled tools you'd otherwise buy separately — run two webinars a month and the Marketer plan already paid for its Zoom-equivalent bill.

Five gotchas before you decide

  • ActiveCampaign bills your dead weight. Since November 2025, new accounts pay for all contacts — including unsubscribed, bounced, and unconfirmed ones. GetResponse counts only active subscribers. A 10,000-contact list with 15% dead weight costs ActiveCampaign ~18% more than the sticker table suggests. Prune aggressively before you import.
  • Both $19 plans are demos, not homes. ActiveCampaign Starter caps every automation at 5 actions with no branching, no landing pages, and no CRM pipelines. GetResponse Starter allows exactly one automation workflow and no webinars. The real price of admission is $59 on both sides — which is precisely why the comparison is fair at parity: you're choosing a product philosophy, not hunting a coupon.
  • Send caps cut one way only. ActiveCampaign limits sends to 10–15× your contact count (a 5,000-contact list on Starter gets 50,000 sends/mo — fine for weekly, a real ceiling for daily senders). GetResponse puts no send cap on any paid plan. If you email daily to a big list, the cap is the binding constraint on ActiveCampaign long before the price is.
  • Webinars are a second bill on ActiveCampaign. If your funnel runs on webinars, ActiveCampaign needs Zoom or GoToWebinar plugged in — separate vendor, separate bill — while GetResponse's webinars are native to the plan. Going the other way, ActiveCampaign's deal pipelines and lead scoring have no GetResponse equivalent at all; a webinar-led business on ActiveCampaign is paying twice, a sales-process business on GetResponse is missing its core tool.
  • Growth forces ActiveCampaign's hand sooner. Starter hard-caps at 10,000 contacts — cross that line and Plus is mandatory ($239/mo at 10k vs GetResponse Starter's $79). And both vendors move prices annually, so annual-billing savings (~20% AC / 18% GR) are real money at list sizes where the monthly bill is three figures.

Who should pick which

Pick ActiveCampaign if: you sell through a sales process — consultations, quotes, follow-up sequences, deal stages — and lead scoring, conditional content, and attribution are what make the list pay; you're an agency running client pipelines; or your automations are genuinely complex (branching, splits, revenue attribution) and shallow builders keep blocking you. Start at Plus; Starter's 5-action cap will annoy you within a month.

Pick GetResponse if: you sell through webinars, courses, launches, or funnels and want email + webinars + funnels + a website on one bill; you send daily to a large list and need unlimited sends; you're starting from zero and want the free tier before committing; or only active subscribers should be on the bill. Start at Marketer; Starter's single-workflow cap won't hold a real automation library.

Switching between them

Migrating to ActiveCampaign from GetResponse is the harder direction: GetResponse's webinar, funnel, and course objects have no ActiveCampaign equivalent, so those assets get rebuilt on other vendors (and re-billed separately), while automations get rebuilt deeper — conditional content and scoring that Starter-tier workflows couldn't express. Budget Plus from day one. Going GetResponse-ward from ActiveCampaign, expect to lose the CRM — deal pipelines, contact scoring, and attribution don't port, so map which pipeline stages actually drove revenue before you move, and consolidate your lists (GetResponse bills active subscribers only, so a messy list still costs you). Either direction, prune dead contacts first: ActiveCampaign charges for them, and importing 15% dead weight is the most expensive line item in most migrations.

FAQ

Is ActiveCampaign worth the premium over GetResponse for a small business?

Only if the sales process does the work. A consultant closing 2–3 extra calls a month from lead-scored follow-ups pays for the $59/mo difference many times over. If your job is newsletters and promos, the premium buys depth you'll never touch — GetResponse's Marketer at the same $59 entry wins on pure value.

Which is better for webinars?

GetResponse, decisively — native webinars at 100 (Marketer) or 300 (Creator) attendees are part of the plan. ActiveCampaign has no native webinar product; you'll integrate Zoom or GoToWebinar and pay that vendor separately.

Can I migrate my automations between them?

Not one-to-one. Automation logic ports (triggers, conditions, delays), but the objects don't: webinar and funnel steps have no ActiveCampaign equivalent, and CRM pipelines, scoring, and attribution have no GetResponse equivalent. Treat every migration as a redesign of the selling system, not a copy-paste.

Do both have free plans or trials?

GetResponse has both: a free tier (500 contacts, 2,500 sends/mo) and a 30-day trial with no card required (vendor claim — verify at signup). ActiveCampaign has no free plan — a 14-day free trial is the only way in.

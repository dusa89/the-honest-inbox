The honest catches

  • The 30C credit is gone — never quote it again. The federal Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit (30% up to $1,000 per home port) expired for property placed in service after June 30, 2026 (federal sunset, labeled). Installations completed and operational by that date can still claim it retroactively (IRS Form 8911) — everything after cannot. A September 2026 survey of ranking installer guides (trendytechtribe, labeled) found one still advertising the credit as available with no end date: that installer is selling on dead terms, and the buyer who discovers it at tax time is a one-star review with a refund demand. The email names the live lane instead: state and utility rebates, $250 to $1,000+ (Qmerit 2026, labeled).
  • The panel truth: never sell Level 2 before the panel says yes. Most homes built before 2000 with 100-amp service are in the panel-upgrade danger zone — and the upgrade ($1,500-$3,000 to $1,500-$5,000+; installer-network figures, labeled) is often bigger than the install itself. The quote names the panel verdict up front; the education sequence carries the upgrade math honestly. The email that promises "$1,700 installed" before seeing the panel is a change-order with a marketing budget.
  • The rebate-map discipline: zip-specific, never promised. Utility programs change by territory and by quarter — the email maps the rebate to the zip, labels every figure an estimate that changes, and never promises a dollar the utility can withdraw. The operator pattern (estimate, labeled): the installer who promises a rebate the utility canceled teaches the whole list to distrust the next email.
  • The permit reality: never sell the install date until the permit lands. Electrical permits (~$282 average, Qmerit 2026, labeled) and inspections stall installs — the quote names the realistic window, and the permit-wait nurture carries the buyer through it. The email that promises "this week" before the permit is filed is a cancellation with a marketing budget.
  • Keep new-buyer, fleet, utility-program, and second-EV households on separate lanes. The fleet manager doesn't want the homeowner's commute math; the second-EV household doesn't want the Level-1 education; the utility-program enrollee doesn't want the sales pitch. Four buyers, four cadences, zero cross-sends. The operator pattern (estimate, labeled): the installer who cross-sends teaches all four audiences to ignore the list.

FAQ

How much does a Level 2 home charger cost installed?

The typical Level 2 install runs about $1,700 (Qmerit 2026 national data, installer-network figure, labeled), with straightforward setups landing $1,700-$2,000 (Argonne National Laboratory 2026, labeled) and the national range running $800-$3,000. Equipment alone: a Tesla Wall Connector is $585, the Universal Wall Connector $660, and the Emporia Classic $449-$479 (installer-market pricing as of October 11, 2026, labeled). The panel upgrade is the wild card: $1,500-$3,000 (Qmerit) to $1,500-$5,000+ (Treehouse) — most homes built before 2000 with 100-amp service are in the danger zone. Permits average ~$282.

How fast is Level 2 charging vs Level 1?

Level 1 (a standard 120V outlet) adds roughly 3-5 miles of range per hour — about 30-40 miles overnight (industry figures, labeled). Level 2 (a 240V circuit, typically 40-48 amps) delivers on the order of 25-40 miles of range per hour — the overnight full refill (industry figures, labeled). A 40-mile-a-day commuter outgrows Level 1 in a week; that commute math is the installer's best sales email. Standard installs take 2-4 hours of electrician time; panel upgrades stretch to a full day or two visits.

Is the federal EV charger tax credit dead?

For new installs, yes. The 30C Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit (30% of equipment-plus-install cost, up to $1,000 per home port, census-tract-restricted; up to $100,000 per location for businesses) expired for property placed in service after June 30, 2026 (federal sunset set by the 2025 budget law, labeled). Installations completed and operational on or before that date can still claim it retroactively on the tax return for that year (IRS Form 8911). The live incentive lane is now state and utility programs ($250 to $1,000+ in many territories; 21.6% of EV drivers used a utility rebate, per Plug In America's 2026 survey, labeled) — the installer's rebate email carries the zip-specific map, never a federal promise.

Do EV chargers add home value?

A 2024 Zillow study found homes with EV chargers sold for roughly 3-5% more than comparable homes without one (trade figure, labeled). The installer uses it in the homeowner-education sequence and the "selling your home?" lane — the charger is an appliance the listing can name, and the review email can ask the buyer about the appraisal.

How is EV-charger-installer email different from electrician email?

The driveway is the funnel: a buyer-driven, rebate-timed, education-heavy install with a 14-day conversion window, a panel-verdict branch, and a fleet-depot B2B pipeline — where an electrician's funnel is the breakdown (repairs, panels, permits on demand). The EV-charger installer teaches first (only ~20% of buyers got dealer charging education; J.D. Power 2026, labeled) and quotes second.

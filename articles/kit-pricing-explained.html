Kit Pricing Explained: What You'll Actually Pay at Every List Size

Kit's pricing page shows you three tidy cards: $0, $33, $66. What it doesn't show you is that those prices are the annual rates, that the bill jumps in fixed subscriber bands behind a slider, that selling a $20 product costs you a transaction fee on top, and that one extra subscriber past 10,000 can take your bill from $0 to $139 a month. Here's the full math, band by band.

TL;DR
Kit's free plan is the most generous free tier in email marketing — 10,000 subscribers at $0 — but the visual automation builder (welcome sequences, drips) is excluded from it, so most growing businesses land on Creator at $39/mo (1,000 subs). Creator is the right plan for almost everyone; Pro's extras (subscriber scoring, advanced reporting, Facebook audiences) only earn their keep if you actively use them. Watch the 10,001-subscriber cliff and the 3.5% + 30¢ commerce fee.

The three plans, decoded

The first confusion is the headline pricing. The $33 and $66 figures on the pricing page are the annual rates. Monthly billing costs more:

Annual billing knocks roughly 16.7% off every band — effectively two free months. If you know you'll be on Kit for a year, it's a real discount, not a gimmick. But note the trap: Kit counts your subscriber band at signup, and list growth can bump your band mid-year.

Two things to notice. First, the curve steepens fast past 10,000: 10k to 25k costs you $60/mo more, and 50k to 100k costs $300/mo more. Second, there's no free tier past 10,000 — subscriber 10,001 moves you from $0 to $139/mo on Creator. That's the steepest $0-to-paid jump in the email category, and it's the number-one reason Kit users get bill shock.

The free plan: genuinely free, with one real catch

Kit's free plan is legitimately the most generous free tier we've seen: up to 10,000 subscribers, unlimited broadcasts, unlimited forms and landing pages, and you can sell digital products with 0% platform fee on the commerce side. For a newsletter that's just starting and growing, it's free for years.

The catch: the free plan excludes the visual automation builder — welcome sequences, drip campaigns, and behavior-triggered emails are Creator features. So the moment you want a new subscriber to automatically receive a 5-email welcome series, you're paying $39/mo. Price the plan you'll need in 6 months, not the one you need today.

What it costs to actually sell something

This is the line nobody reads. Selling through Kit Commerce — digital products, paid newsletters, subscriptions — costs 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction on every plan, including free. Kit's own cut is 0.6%; the rest is card processing. A $20 product sale costs you about $1.00.

And the Creator Network's Paid Recommendations (getting paid to promote other newsletters) carries a 23.5% fee to Kit on your earnings. It's a growth feature, not a revenue feature — run the math before you count that income.

Three gotchas before you decide

  • The annual discount is real, but the band can move. If your list grows mid-year, your band — and your bill — moves with it, annual billing or not.
  • Pro is only worth it if you use its three things. Subscriber scoring, advanced reporting, and Facebook Custom Audiences sync are the whole case for Pro. If you don't touch them, it's $40/mo extra for nothing. Most small businesses never need it.
  • Old guides quote old prices. Kit raised prices roughly 35% in late 2025 — any guide showing a $29 entry price or $25 Creator plan is stale. Check the date on anything you read, including this page (checked September 30, 2026).

Which plan should you actually pick?

Pick Free if: your list is under 10,000 and you're mainly sending newsletters or broadcasts — no automated sequences yet. This can carry you for years.

Pick Creator if: you need automations (welcome sequences, drips, behavior triggers), you want Kit branding removed, or you want free migration from your old tool. This is the plan most growing businesses end up on.

Pick Pro if: you're actively using subscriber scoring, advanced reporting, or Facebook audiences — or you need more than 2 users on the account. Otherwise, skip it.

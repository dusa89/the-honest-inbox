AWeber is the old guard that stayed an email tool — founded 1998, sharpened over decades into the cheapest honest small-list emailer with a human on the phone when things break. It is also the platform that charges you for your unsubscribers, with no webinars, no funnels, no course hosting, and no way to sell anything. "AWeber alternatives" gets searched by two kinds of people: those who outgrew email-only, and those who just noticed the dead weight on their bill. Below: the six exits that genuinely make sense, matched to your real reason — and the honest case that AWeber's 3-month free runway means most of you should stay longer than you planned to.

Why people actually leave AWeber

Five real reasons, in order of how often they come up — not the padded grievances from other listicles.

1. It's email-only in an all-in-one world. AWeber never grew beyond email: no webinars, no sales funnels, no course hosting, no website builder. The moment your business needs a webinar, you're buying a second tool — and at that point GetResponse Marketer ($59/mo at 1k) starts replacing two or three bills with one. If your job is email, email-only is a feature. The day it stops being your whole job, it's the reason you leave. See AWeber vs GetResponse for the full capability split.

2. The Lite-to-Plus gap. AWeber Lite is $25/mo at 1,000 subscribers — a clean budget tier, and $6 less than Kit Creator. But Plus, the tier most growing businesses actually need, is $45/mo at the same list size. That's a $20 jump between tiers, and it lands exactly when your list is big enough to matter but not big enough to shrug at. MailerLite Comfort undercuts both at $19/mo with automations included. The Lite tier is genuinely competitive; the Plus tier is where the comparison gets uncomfortable.

3. You pay for dead weight. AWeber bills by subscriber total — and unsubscribed contacts still count toward it. A list of 5,000 contacts with 800 unsubscribers bills you for 5,000. Every other tool counts differently: Flodesk bills only active subscribers, MailerLite bills unique active subscribers, Brevo bills sends rather than contacts. If you never clean your list, this is the most expensive habit on the platform — and it's the single easiest reason to leave, because a one-hour cleanup cuts the bill on every alternative too.

4. Creators can't sell anything. There's no commerce layer, no paid-newsletter engine, no storefront. A creator monetizing a newsletter on AWeber is duct-taping Stripe and Zapier onto a platform that was never designed for it — while Kit ships commerce, Beehiiv charges 0% platform fees on paid subscriptions, and Substack handles the whole payment stack for a 10% cut. If selling is the business, AWeber is the wrong chassis, not the wrong price.

5. The automation ceiling. AWeber's automation is solid for welcome sequences, broadcasts, and drip campaigns — and it stops there. No branching logic, no lead scoring, no CRM pipeline, no predictive anything. ActiveCampaign's Plus tier ($59/mo at 1k) is a fundamentally different machine: a marketing-automation platform with a CRM attached. Nobody outgrows AWeber's email; they outgrow its brain.

The 6 alternatives, matched to your reason for leaving

1. MailerLite — if it's the price

The direct undercut. Comfort runs $19/mo at 1,000 subscribers — $6 less than AWeber Lite and $26 less than Plus — with real automations, segmentation, and a landing-page builder included. You give up the phone support (MailerLite is ticket/chat-only) and the generous free tier (250 subs / 2,500 sends vs AWeber's 500 / 3,000). But on paid plans, it's the same honest-email job for less money at every verified band: $49 vs unlisted AWeber Plus at 5k, $179 vs ~$230 (est.) at 25k. If AWeber's Plus price stung and you don't need webinars or a CRM, this is the move.

2. GetResponse — if it's the all-in-one

The graduation move. Starter at $19/mo matches AWeber Lite's price at 1k while adding the webinar-and-funnel world AWeber will never have — with one serious caveat: Starter allows exactly one automation workflow, so budget Marketer ($59/mo) if you're migrating a real automation library. Native webinars, conversion funnels, course hosting, and a website builder under one bill means the second-tool subscription AWeber forced you to buy disappears. You're paying more than AWeber Plus ($59 vs $45) for capabilities AWeber can't sell at any price. See GetResponse vs MailerLite for the head-to-head.

3. Kit — if you're a creator who sells

The exit for creators whose business is the audience itself. Kit Creator ($39/$89/$199) ships commerce tooling, the Creator Network, and paid recommendations — the monetization layer AWeber doesn't have — plus the best free tier in the category (10,000 subscribers at $0) if you're pre-revenue. The trade: you pay the creator premium at every band ($39 vs Lite's $25 at 1k), and commerce carries per-transaction fees (3.5% + 30¢ per Kit's own docs, with 2026 sources disputing the figure — verify before pricing a product). Switch if selling is the business; stay if it isn't. The full case in MailerLite vs Kit.

4. ActiveCampaign — if it's real automation and CRM

When the job outgrew email. ActiveCampaign Starter starts at $19/mo at 1k — cheaper than AWeber Lite — and Plus ($59/mo at 1k, $179 at 5k) is the best automation engine under $100/mo: branching logic, lead scoring, sales pipelines, predictive sending. The Starter caps matter (5 automation actions, 10,000 contacts), so price Plus, not the headline. This is a business-systems upgrade, not a cost play: businesses that only send newsletters are buying complexity they won't use. See ActiveCampaign vs Kit for the automation-vs-simplicity trade-off.

5. Brevo — if you have a huge list and send infrequently

The billing-model inversion that punishes AWeber's dead-weight problem hardest. Brevo bills by emails sent and stores unlimited contacts for free — a 20,000-contact list that sends monthly costs the same as a 2,000-contact list with the same volume. Starter runs $9 (5k emails) to $32 (20k emails); Standard — the tier with real automation — runs $18 (5k) to $69 (20k). The trap we flag on our Brevo vs MailerLite page: Starter has no automation, so the "from $9" headline is volume without workflows. Right for large lists with light send volume; wrong for anyone who lives in automation.

6. Flodesk — if it's design-first and broadcasts

The aesthetic exit with a billing-model kicker: Flodesk counts only active subscribers — the exact opposite of AWeber's bill-your-unsubscribers policy. Lite is $25/mo at 1k (same as AWeber Lite), with templates that genuinely look like a designer's work. The honest limits, from our Flodesk vs MailerLite page: Lite includes a single workflow automation (positioned as a "limited-time bonus," which is an honest flag it could change); Pro ($28/mo) is the real automation price; and the free plan sends zero emails — it only builds your list. Pick it if your sends are beautiful broadcasts to an active list; skip it if automation does your selling.

The honest case for staying: the longest free runway in the category

Here's what most "alternatives" articles skip, because they'd rather earn the switch commission: AWeber gives new accounts 3 months completely free, then 25% off — the longest free runway in email marketing, full stop. Nonprofits get the same deal with 501(c)(3) paperwork. The free tier (500 subscribers / 3,000 emails) is now objectively more generous than MailerLite's (250 / 2,500). Phone support comes on every paid plan — a genuine rarity at this price — and the Done For You service is a $79 one-time complete setup for operators who'd rather buy a week of their life back.

The stay math: if your job is newsletters, broadcasts, and welcome sequences, the 25%-off Plus plan ($33.75/mo effective at 1k, roughly) undercuts MailerLite's reputation as the value pick — with a human on the phone. The dead-weight billing is real, but a one-hour cleanup before you judge fixes the number you're actually comparing. Switch when you need webinars, funnels, commerce, or a CRM — those are architectural, not priced. Until then, the company that's survived since 1998 is usually cheaper than the article telling you to leave it.

How to actually switch (if you're sure)

1. Clean first. This matters double on AWeber: purge unsubscribers and duplicates before export, because you're billed for them here and every alternative bills the cleaner list smaller. 2. Export everything — lists, tags, segments, landing pages. 3. Rebuild automations by hand — no tool imports another's automation logic; the welcome sequence is the first rebuild and the most important. 4. Warm up the new sender — run both platforms in parallel for one full send cycle and watch deliverability before canceling AWeber.
